This project is never "finished". There are always mistakes to fix, more topics to explore, more games to enjoy, and more research to be done on how children actually learn and develop.

This article, therefore, explains how you can help develop and improve the project, and the general planning for the future.

## Feedback

This website does not track or store any data, and it never will. Privacy concerns play a role here, especially because this website is mostly for children, but it's also simply about keeping the website fast, lightweight and easy to use.

There are no comments, you can't make an account, there is no large forum or shared chat, nothing of the sort.

Others are, of course, free to create this if they want. You are free to use these games, play them, talk about them, create another online home for them. But I will never do this ("officially"), because I never use such platforms myself and they add a maintenance cost for which I simply don't have the time or money.

If you want to give feedback or help out, simply **mail me** at `tiamopastoor@gmail.com`!

This includes informing me about issues or mistakes. But I'm also very much open to your thoughts after trying a game (negative or positive), or suggestions for topics or game ideas to create.

I have no ulterior motive. There is no money to be made here and I certainly do not want to indoctrinate kids. I am not under any contract, and if this project takes sponsors, they will not have any power over it. Absolutely everything is open for discussion, change, and improvement. 

We're trying to build a _better_ path of education and growth here. All voices are welcome.

## Roadmap

After 20+ years of making creative projects, I learned that it's a marathon, not a sprint. I learned a thing or two about staying productive and hitting deadlines, consistently, for years on end.

This means _Explorers of the Fun Frontier_ is not my "main project" or my "full-time job". I don't intend to work on it non-stop until it's completely done.

Instead, as with most of my projects, my approach is elegantly titled "one big update per year".

* Every year, I will add the **next level** to this project.
* This means the next one or two games in every topic. The next game that is slightly more advanced again, for slightly older or more skilled players.
* This will usually launch all at once, combined with general improvements to the website.
* And then I completely move on to other projects for some time.

By creating this "batch" of games at the same time, I can be far more efficient and make far fewer errors. For some time, my brain is fully in the mode of "make educational games". This means I have the right habits and schedule for it. This means my brain remembers all the ways I solved similar problems before. This means the software needed to make these games is already loaded and open to the right template.

At the same time, creativity is something that needs to be recharged. To learn and solve problems, humans need to _disengage_ half the time. I need to get away from the project for a sufficient period of time, otherwise I'll go insane. I'll start working more and more slowly, and make worse and worse games. Diminishing returns, you might call it.

In other articles, I talked about how most things in this world are "systems". Treating them as if they're standalone is usually factually wrong and leads to worse decisions.

That's why I create these games in batches and launch them that way too. (Though there are always exceptions, of course.) It allows me to see connections between games and skills. It's much easier to learn a skill if the game can connect it to _other_ skills you're learning at the same time. It allows me to develop them to be more cohesive, to catch any major flaws before anyone else sees the games. Even though I _know_ this, I am still regularly surprised how beneficial it is to work this way. How developing game 5 suddenly gives me an insight that massively improves games 1--4 that I made the past few weeks.

It also means that I won't _instantly_ fix issues or act on feedback. I'll add it to the list, I'll fix it, but the updates might take a few months to "go through".

As I said, this is a completely free project and not my main job. I need to be disciplined and smart about the development, lest I turn it into a sprint that I have to give up at some point.
