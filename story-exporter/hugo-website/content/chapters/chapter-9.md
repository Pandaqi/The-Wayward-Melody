# The Square Sloth

The Chaos Cat had come. The Sort Stork waited in the dark. 

As far as traps go, this one was pretty uninspired. He had found the biggest cardboard box they had. One so big that an entire cat fit inside. Even the Group Giraffe might fit inside. He had no idea why they made boxes this big. Perhaps this one was from last year, when they carried an entire group of musicians to Irina's birthday.

Anyway, he placed a twig below one side of the box. This propped up that side. The cat could easily walk underneath the box now, suspecting nothing. Then, the Sort Stork only needed to kick away the twig, and it would fall down on her head! Haha!

He waited in silence. The Chaos Cat looked around her. Wary. Checking over her shoulder.

Until the silence was broken by a machine. It beeped, and sputtered, and out came ...

What?

A package shaped like a pyramid. Triangles on four sides, ending in a pointy top. What was _that_? Did someone order a tiny pyramid? Boxes should be _square_. Or, well, a square in three dimensions, which was a cube. That's the right way of things. Simple. Predictable. Fits nicely inside any container. Baskets? Usually square. Back of a delivery truck? Square too. 

A package arrived shaped like a _ball_ now. Then another shaped like a _star_. It made him so angry that he couldn't help looking away for a while. 

When he looked back to his trap, the Chaos Cat was gone.

Her voice whispered in his ear, from the dark, but somewhere near.

"Hello storkie. Heard you were looking for me."

He yelped. His wings unfolded and flapped around him, hitting nothing except a few idle machines.

"You can't scare me!" he lied. "Where is the package? Speak up!"

"Look. Just---open your eyes, will you?"

He did. And there it was. The shiny package. She hadn't destroyed it yet! It glowed and shimmered, in the center of the room. There was a chance he could save everything. That everything would be all right.

The Sort Stork started forward. Soon it turned into something that was half running and half flying. He heard the footsteps of the Chaos Cat right behind him, keeping pace. Until the cat's footsteps stopped.

He kept going. "I'm faster than you! You've made a big mistake now! I _win_."

His feet kicked against something. As he rubbed his painful toe, he heard a CREAK and a WHOOMP. He reached for the Shiny Package, but it moved away from him.

As soon as he looked up, his entire world had gone dark. Completely dark. No machine lights, no white circles where a cat's eyes should be.

Ugh.

He ... had trapped ... himself.

Chaos Cat's voice sounded dull. "Huh. Didn't think you'd fall for that. They let _anyone_ run Cloudmail Castle these days ..."

The Sort Stork was about to scream _let me out!_ But no, he had to stay calm. Pretend this was all his plan.

"You can tell me now," he said. He subtly pressed against the cardboard wall. It was too big and too heavy. "What's inside the package? I can't escape anyway. You win, evil cat."

"You still haven't figured it out?" Her voice became even smaller, almost a whisper. "I can't say ... you'll think that I looked inside ... the biggest mistake of any mail deliverer ... me and my dad will be fired ... I can't tell ..."

Wait. She _didn't_ look inside the package? And still she knew what was inside?

_Now_ he figured it out.

Cloudmail Castle had a magical key. It could turn it on, and turn it off. And magical things were _shiny_; they glowed. Everyone had been complaining to the king about how bad their mail service was. About delayed packages, broken packages, bright packages waking them at night. The king had probably had enough. He had ordered the key weeks ago, planning to shut down Cloudmail Castle.

The only thing that saved them ... was how _bad_ they were at delivering that key.

"It's the key, isn't it?"

"Yep," said a small voice. She was right next to him; only a cardboard wall separated their cheeks. "I can't let the castle die. I _won't_ let them take Cloudmail Castle! We're the best place ever. We bring so much joy. And, sometimes, by a miracle, letters too."

They giggled. 

Then she became serious again.

"I love it here. I'm sorry. I couldn't let the key be delivered. But I also couldn't destroy it. One day soon, the king will realize and go up here to take it himself. We need to find a solution."

The Sort Stork fell silent. He needed some time to sort his own thoughts. Everything had made sense to him before, when he was sure the cat was evil and hated them. Simple. Easy. Took no effort. But now he didn't know what to say. She wasn't evil; but stealing and lying also wasn't good. Maybe she was just somewhere in between. 

Just like him.

"I'm sorry I chased you. And tried to trap you. And about my "ONE STORK, ONE LETTER" rule that I had for years. Can you ... can you please let me out of my own trap now?"

She giggled again. 

"Only if you let me deliver packages, like a stork."

"Look, cardboard wings won't let you fly---"

"Do you _want_ to stay in this box forever, sir?"

"Fine, fine, fine. We'll find a way."

She wasn't strong enough to lift the massive box, of course. But she received help. When the cardboard walls were lifted, he saw that morning had already come. Several storks were trying to create piles again. They had complained first, but now they saw how well it worked. They were measuring wingspans and deciding who should get a certain package. 

A sloth bumped into them. Slowly, of course, as sloths do. It was less of a bump and more of a pat. He was carrying a few packages---regular, simple, square packages---to put inside a bag. When he saw the pyramid-shaped package, and the star-shaped one, he dropped the packages and almost fainted.

The Group Giraffe yawned as she entered the room. Then she froze, utterly confused. "The ... the Chaos Cat ... she's right next to you? Hello? Why are you not---"

"Long story. The Shiny Package is back, and we must _not_ deliver it!"

"What?"

"Hide it somewhere. Distract the king if he comes. You know what, I'll do it myself---"

The sloth bumped into him. It was a proper bump, too. Good speed. Nice BOINK sound. That's how you knew the sloth was _really_ panicking.

"We need you here, boss. Have you seen these packages? They don't fit anywhere! How am I supposed to put a star-shaped box next to a heart-shaped box, on top of a ball-shaped box? They'd just slide off. And we're wasting space!"

They made an attempt. But everything about the Sorting Room was _square_. Because letters and packages had always been square. The baskets that storks carried were square. Normally, they could fit at least five packages. But with packages in such strange shapes, they could only fit one or two. Like most babies learn at some point, you simply can't push a triangle through a square hole.

"New group! New group!"

"Noooo," complained a certain Capybara. "We already have so many groups! So many rules!"

"Agreed!" yelled a group of storks. They frowned, and looked around, but nobody knew what packages to pick up and start to deliver. And so they delivered nothing. "Let's go back to ONE STORK, ONE LETTER. Yes. Let's do that."

This was enough to convince a few storks to pick up _one_ package and go deliver it. But it just wasn't enough. With that system, they would take a _week_ to deliver what arrived in a _day_.

"No! Stop!" said the Sort Stork. "I mean ... but ... I don't know ... just ... wait ..."

The Sort Stork didn't know what to do. He forgot how to speak. He couldn't make sense of it anymore. They were right: things were too complicated. They were also wrong: the king would shut them down if they went back to their old rules. If they went back to before they made groups and tried to be smart about it.

He felt light-headed. Sick. Everything around him became a blur, as if he wore broken glasses. The Group Giraffe kept asking questions, but he didn't respond anymore. The Chaos Cat asked questions, but he didn't hear anymore. They both left him alone to try and save the situation.





And, so, everyone started to panic. The packages piled up. They were already late, and now it was getting worse. They didn't have enough storks for this. A stork would normally carry an entire pile, but now they had to settle for only one package with a weird shape. Two pizzas were already cooled off. A puppy had broken loose and chased Chaos Cat. 

The only one who seemed calm, was the sloth. He was off in a corner collecting packages with the same shape. No, not only that. He collected packages with shapes that _fit together_. If one package had a bump, the one next to it had a hole. Together, they'd fit nicely, like puzzle pieces. Two triangles fit together too, if you flipped one of them around. And if one package was a donut, then a ball-shaped package could fit inside that hole.

He was making progress.

The Sort Stork focused on it. He tried to ignore the chaos, the panic, the envelopes flying around the room. It worked. He walked to the sloth, feeling calmer just by looking at someone so calm.

"How do you do it?" he whispered.

"Slow and steady, boss. Slow and steady. It starts with one group. Find one thing to figure out, and you have less chaos to worry about."

Less chaos? The groups _caused_ chaos! 

Or maybe they didn't.

Maybe ...

His beak fell open and he forgot to blink. For the first time, he saw the truth. 

His old rule---ONE STORK, ONE LETTER---used groups too. It just used them badly. It made every letter its _own group_. And, dear listener, you probably realize that groups of one thing are pretty useless as groups. Their power is in containing _multiple things_. 

Before him was only chaos. Hundreds of letters and packages. But with groups ... with just a few smart groups ... he could make it clear. Simple. Easy. Predictable.

Hundreds of letters turned into just five groups. Now _that's_ simple. Could count it on one hand, if he had hands. That's _structure_ and _order_, just how he likes it.

ONE STORK, ONE GROUP.

He made a decision then. He allowed the Shiny Package to be delivered to the king---to give him the key that could end Cloudmail Castle.

Because the best way to convince him not to do that ... was to show how _great_ they were. By delivering everything. On time. Unbroken. Uneaten.

He took a deep breath. And he helped the Square Sloth make sense of the chaos.
