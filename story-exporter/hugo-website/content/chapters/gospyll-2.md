# The Gospyll According To Judash

When Krystina arrived in Jerrysalum, she was surprised to find a crowd waiting for her. They waved at her---or possibly her donkey---with palm branches. They sang songs, asked her questions, and wanted to briefly touch her clothes.

I, Judash, had tried to prepare her for it. After spending years in the big city, spreading the message for her, I knew she was adored. I knew many were waiting for her. The sick hoping to be healed; the starving hoping to be fed. I, also, like to write about myself in third person, so that is what I will do. Perhaps it's a way to distance myself from the acts I would later commit.

It had taken several days to travel to Jerrysalum. Judash was a black sheep, and Krystina's most important disciple, her right-hand man all along. He had whispered in her ear all this time. Nevertheless, his preparations were lacking. Or, rather, they had the exact opposite effect. 

By the time they entered the city of beige buildings, littered with market stalls and Amor soldiers, Krystina had really started to believe she was special. She _was_ a demigod. She _could_ make a change. The platypus stood a little taller, held herself a little more queenly.

She had barely entered Jerrysalum before pausing to kneel before a bunny beggar. She gently placed money in his hand, one of only three golden coins she had, then continued.

To Krystina, it seemed as if every single building, every corner of the road, had another starving beggar. She stopped more than she walked, and soon she had given away the few things she had. Perhaps this was because of her upbringing, having lived in poverty herself, because nobody else seemed to noice.

All other citizens in Amor did not see it. Or they pretended not to. They were more likely to bump into beggars and say "sorry, did not see you there", over and over. All the Amor soldiers saw, with a very grumpy and suspicious expression, was Krystina making their lives difficult.

"We should've brought far more food and coins," mumbled Krystina.

"From where?" answered Judash. "We barely have anything ourselves. And we quit our jobs as fishermen to follow you."

"Remember my miracle where I broke a few loaves of bread into hundreds?"

"Yes," said Judash with a smile. "And I remember you never being able to repeat it."

"I'm feeling magical today," said Krystina with a sly smile.

By now the Amor soldiers were getting antsy. A massive crowd had formed around Krystina and her donkey, and the situation turned dangerous. Some climbed over each other to get closer to Krystina. The requests to "heal my grandfather" and to "feed our family" became one large noise in her ears. 

The soldiers intervened and broke up the crowd. Some stronger animals, such as bears and tigers, tried to fight back, but thought better of it when swords were drawn.

"This is dangerous," whispered Judash, waiting for soldiers to pass. "We shouldn't have attracted this much attention."

"How can I heal the sick if they don't know I am here?"

"There are ... subtler ways," said the black sheep, shepherding Krystina and the disciples away from the crowd. "You can multiply bread? Great! Do it! But there's no reason to do it in front of the Amor soldiers, on the forum, with grand gestures. That's offensive to our ... oppressors. That's like dangling a juicy slab of meat before a tiger and expecting them not to bite."

Wise words from a wise man, if I may say so myself.

"What do you suggest, Judash?" asked Krystina as she fled into narrow alleys.

"Create the food. Fill baskets of bread. But do it silently, hidden from view, and then ask your disciples to spread it around the city."

"But nobody would know that I did it."

"Does that matter?"

"Of course it does!" responded Krystina immediately. "I've given my _life_ to this mission. I was perfectly happy building wooden chairs in Nazreti, but I left my home and spread the message because everyone told me to. The least they can do is recognize me."

Judash froze, picking at his stubborn wool. "What matters is the things you say. Not who says it."

"I'm the magical one here," said Krystina. "Others are free to take my place once they can do what I can."

Krystina climbed on some wooden crates, then pointed at a tower in the distance. The temple in Jerrysalum had a beautiful large clock, constructed by the finest Bearchitects, which even fishes in the sea could use to tell the time. It was operated by some strong ox slaves attached to ropes and pulleys, walking circles on the ground.

"Tell everyone to gather in the temple at midnight," said Krystina. "And I will perform my miracle and provide food. They need to see _me_, Judash. Who will believe in an anonymous person in the shadows? Who will pray to the God of Never There or the Messias Without Name?"

With that, Krystina left Judash in the dust.

Long after the black sheep watched his savior stomp off, he was approached by a figure who remained in the dark. He couldn't figure out their animal species. In fact, he didn't know _any_ species with such a tall, pointy head. He figured this animal must be wearing some new invention called a _hat_.

"Do you happen to be a follower of Krystina?" asked the gravely voice.

"I am far more than just a follower," answered Judash with pride. Then he regretted giving away too much to a figure in the shadows.

"We are looking for information on her and her whereabouts," said the voice. 

"What is your name, good man?" asked Judash.

"Not important. We want to ... keep an eye on her."

Judash face turned even darker than his scratchy wool. "Then I don't know what you're talking about. Leave Krystina alone. Don't bother me again."

"As you wish," said the shadows, which soon became nothing more than an actual shadow.

Now we've come to a point where I must disagree with the Gospyll of Lent. Lent should not be fully trusted in any case, as he was not a disciple. He states that Krystina was able to survive the scorching desert for forty days without food or drink, because _magic_, and _divine_, and _iron will_, and all that nonsense.

She was just a platypus. Let me repeat: she was just a platypus who grew up in poverty and food scarcity. She was used to going without food, she was used to living in harsh conditions. At this point, I'd known Krystina all my life, and though she was kind and lovely and good, I did not believe that she was some demigod. For that I saw her do far too little magic.

Which is why I was anxious to see her "perform" that evening.

___

Thousands had gathered in the temple. Animals of every sort imaginable, some starving to the bone, some clearly not starving and just interested in the event. 

Krystina would have liked all her disciples there. For support, for friendship. But the most important one, Astra, was not even allowed in the temple. She was a tax collector for Amor, reviled and considered as unclean as a pig. Only Judash and a few other disciples could join her that night.

As the clock struck midnight, Krystina entered to cheers.

Judash shook his head, his fur, his entire body. They were treating her like some pop star. As if she were the lead singer of the Amor Orchestra. They wouldn't even cheer and bow this much if the emperor of Amor came to visit personally!

"Welcome, everyone, I am glad you are---"

Krystina's gaze paused as it noticed some shady figures leaning against the temple walls. They were selling their wares to the massive, desperate crowd. Some sold clothes, implying these were holy garments. Because Krystina had touched them once, accidentally, or something similar. Some merchants sold food, implying these were holy spices, because Krystina had magically made them from rock alone. And some sold nothing at all, but simply stole coins, traded with other merchants, or tempted people into buying "insurance" from the Jurad High Priests. In this case, "insurance" means that animals paid a lot of money to hear empty promises that the real God would save them if needed.

"Begone!" yelled Krystina. "This is a holy place! Keep your foul money and swindling out of this place!"

"We have every right to be here," said a large pig with a scar on his face.

"We have been doing business in this temple for decades," said a swine. "And there was never any problem---"

"Begone!" copied the crowd. Quickly, all of them had found a merchant to angrily stare at. 

Too quickly, Judash felt. So quickly that the crowd might almost do _anything_ that Krystina told them to do. If she were to scratch her ear, because she was itchy, Judash imagined they would all follow. If she were to stumble and fall flat on her beak, Judash imagined the crowd would immediately follow suit and turn it into a holy gesture.

"Now, now, be serious," said the pig.

The problem was, of course, that Krystina _was_ very serious. She had quickly turned the crowd against them, pushing them out of the temple, and in one case even destroying their stall and scattering their bag of coins all over the marble floor.

"This is madness!" yelled the swine. "You will be punished for this!"

"God decides punishment," said Krystina confidently, "not some greedy swine."

Soon, the entire crowd had chased away the merchants. Even the sleepy soldiers, who stood outside as an attempt at a guard, were gone. The final stall that remained was flipped over, its contents scattered, as the crowd cheered. All that was left behind were a few loafs of bread.

Judash trembled. He felt unsafe. He felt disappointed.

What had he done? Krystina behaved like the Queen of the Jurads. It wasn't long ago that she was a naïve little girl, uncertain if she even had magic. 

Now the bread was given to a confident Krystina, to perform her miracle.

She smiled at the crowd. With a loaf of bread in her beak, she closed her eyes and thought back to the first time she did this. She felt a buzz through her body, a tingling in her belly. She felt a tapping on her shoulder, similar to when that dove would visit her, and tried to send this energy into the bread. It was exhausting; she felt lost. Applying her magic was like navigating a ship through stormy seas, in darkness, with pirates lurking. She was just as likely to _not_ apply her magic, as she was to apply the _wrong_ magic, such as turning a loaf of bread back to life---and who knows what horrors would appear then.

After half an hour of silence, she had to admit defeat.

Trembling, the bread falling from her exhausted beak, she opened her eyes. Her body tensed up at the expectation of embarrassment and anger.

The crowd hadn't left, or asked questions, or moved from their spot. They still believed.

"And let this be a lesson to us all," said Krystina quickly, "in _patience_. Yes, it is very good to be patient."

"Indeed, indeed," murmered the crowd. Though those words were often drowned out by rumbling stomachs.

"And there was another lesson this night," added Krystina on the fly, "about handling _disappointment_, yes. Sometimes life doesn't give you lemons, and you still have to continue."

"Lemons? I thought you were making bread?" asked a chameleon in the crowd.

"It's a figure of speech from where we were born," explained Judash.

"Now the final lesson of this beautiful night," said Krystina, smiling as she came up with these. "It is that we should share all we have, even if we have little. Love thy neighbor, love thy fellow life."

The bread was broken into pieces, as planned, but these pieces were _really small_. Barely enough to serve the appetite of a baby ant. Still, nobody left the temple without at least tasting a crumb or two. And they were all satisfied. They spoke of Krystina and how she wasn't afraid to show failure. How she still made sure everyone had something to eat, despite having so little.

Truly, they said, she was the savior. And then they crashed some other Passover dinner to still their hunger.

As they left the temple, Judash felt a tug on his wool. He turned around and was confronted by the same shadowy figure, in a similar alley, as earlier that week.

"Blasphemy!" hissed the figure. "The Jurad codes clearly state you're not allowed to eat leavened bread during Passover. Krystina is a heretic, breaking down our good systems of faith."

"Perhaps ... the Jurad codes need to be updated," replied Judash. "God has changed his mind on a few things, and sent his daughter to give the message."

"His daughter! I can't believe my ears!" The creature almost revealed itself, as Amor soldiers with torches passed by and illuminated the alley. It stepped away just in time. "It's against our rules to declare yourself related to God."

"And that's why you probably missed the last ten saviors he sent. Leave me alone. I stand by Krystina, the best being I have ever known."

"Even after what she did tonight?"

"She told criminals to leave, spoke with those in need, and shared what little food we had. Does that sound like a crime to you? Or just to your religious beliefs?"

"But ... but ... this goes too far! She was seen healing during the Sabbath, our day of rest."

"Sickness doesn't rest. What good is it if you can follow all the rules and innocent people still die?" 

Judash turned and walked away. This being clearly wasn't going to change their mind, and he had no interest in being arrested by Amor soldiers.

Some of you might have figured that this shadowy figure is the same one as the snake that tempted Krystina in the desert. This is a point, however, where I have to contend with the Gospyll of Lent again. It is a well-known fact that _all_ snakes have a severe lisp, drawing out their _s_ and _z_ sounds. This makes it near impossible to understand a thing they're saying! I find it hard to believe Krystina actually talked to a snake and understood them. Moreso, the snake claims to be some all powerful god. But the reason we're in this mess, is because we fought the gods---and won. 

So who was he then? Just a regular snake of flesh and blood, is my theory.

It must also be stated that the Amori already _had_ their own gods. Jupiter, Cupid, etcetera. They were suspiciously similar to the godchildren siblings they just defeated, with Jupiter being a fierce tiger. With a single city already having many clashing faiths---Juradism, Amori Gods, The Defeated Godchildren---one might feel it was getting a bit full in the religion department. The pot was boiling over, so to speak, and some messiahs had to leak out.

Judash pretended to walk away. Then, suddenly, he spun around and took one quick glance backwards to confirm his suspicion.

The being was a Jurad High Priest. Wearing the hat that belonged to the job, and wearing the robe that made it hard to tell their animal species.

"Now that you've seen me," said the being, annoyed, "consider this a request. You shall deliver Krystina to us, before she does any more damage to our faith. We are beaten down enough by Amor as it is. If you let Krystina break our laws, soon there will be no proper Jurad left!"

"Why are you asking? I have no power; you have all."

"We can't be seen getting our paws dirty, of course," whispered the priest. "Besides, whenever we approach her, she magically vanishes from our grasp. Miraculous. So deliver her to us. Or ... get her to take back her words and apologize. Otherwise we have no choice but to follow Momo's Law and sentence her to death!"

"She _is_ Momo's Law," mumbled Judash.

Judash only felt a short moment of doubt. The events of that night scared him. It tilted his entire view of the savior and what their mission was. But he loved Krystina with all his heart, and was certain she would listen if he expressed his worries again.

"Then I deny your request."

___

Krystina gathered at the fountain. Being a platypus, she had to regularly swim. All this hot, dry sand was annoying her to no end. She had already planned to return to her natural habitat once Passover had passed over.

Anyone in need could easily find her. In need of healing, if they had a broken bone, or were dying from illness. In need of food or money, for obvious reasons. Or in need of spiritual guidance. An answer to life's great questions such as "what is the meaning of life?", "what is God's plan?" and "which sandals should I wear today?"

Krystina was well-versed in Jurad doctrine. She had studied it a great deal when she was young, because it gave her hope and strength during those hard times. This knowledge as a kid was what impressed the local priests, which eventually led her here.

And so she often gave the easy answer. The thing she had memorized from the books and the stories. The religious texts of Juradism are surprisingly strict about sandal etiquette.

But as the Gospyll of Lent stated, Krystina had a strange affliction. She couldn't lie. In fact, her entire family had a range of odd curses and boons. But those will reveal themselves in later Gospylls---if the writers of those are as good at recording history as I am.

More and more, questions arrived that led her down her own path. As if placing a stamp of approval on certain Jurad rules, then adding her own seal of disagreement to other rules.

"My neighbor has stolen money from me," said a wolf. "Am I just in seeking vengeance and killing him?"

The first time Krystina received such questions, she was taken aback. She would stutter, blush, and try not to think of everyone as a potential murderer. Now, after years of doing "the mission", she was used to it. But her answer had changed. "No. Violence is never the answer, not even against greater violence. One should trust in the law to settle disputes. And if it's not enough, work to change the law for the better."

"Trust the law? We are occupied!" yelled a goat. "The Amor Empire has conqured us and treated us like beggars and slaves! Why should we trust _their_ laws?"

Judash stepped forward. He was a sheep, yes, but he was a large one---and very strong, wise, intelligent, smart, and good with words. He was especially intimidating when his wool had fully grown out. And there could be quite some time between freak accidents with magical scissors shaved his wool again. More and more often, Judash felt the need to step between Krystina and angry crowds. To shield her, with his massive body, from potential danger.

"Krystina is wise," he said. "We should not draw too much attention. If we get too large, they will take notice. If we grow rebellious, the Amor soldiers will beat us down. And it is hard to speak the word of God when you have no tongue."

"That is not what I meant," said Krystina. 

Judash felt deflated. His wool almost seemed to shrink on demand, leaving him with empty spots on his skin. It was the first time she had directly gone against a disciple, and in such a public and embarrassing way too!

"Over and over, we ask ourselves the question: is God good? Should we trust in an almighty being, or decide good and evil for ourselves? Is freedom more important, or faith in something wiser than us?" 

Krystina jumped into the river again, and an awkward silence fell over the forum for a minute. 

When she returned, she explained herself in simpler terms: "God asks us to uphold his law, and to have full faith that his rules are the right ones. And so I ask you to do the same. To uphold the law. To pay your taxes to Amor. Happiness will be found in order and peace---and one day, we will all be free from oppression."

It is a hard sell, dear reader, to tell those who were invaded to be nice and friendly to their invaders. It was also, as expected, against Jurad codes.

But the beings of Jerrysalum seemed ready for a change. They had tried the cycle of vengeance, of answering murder with murder, of fighting for personal freedom and their place in heaven. The First Conflict had been years and years of fighting, death, and general miserable conditions. It had not worked out as they had hoped---or they would not be listening to Krystina now.

Time and time again, any living creature would return to a life of sin. They would fall and make mistakes, setting back the message of god and world peace.

Judash had been with Krystina almost all her life, and had _never_ seen her sin. The only being whom he had never caught in a sin. Mistakes, yes. Accidents, absolutely. But no sin. Even as a young girl, it was impossible to find her jealous. Or to see her secretly sneak her beak into the cookie jar, say a bad word about anyone, or be vain and take credit for her miracles.

He was determined to prevent her from falling now.

"Krystina," started Judash, "could I have a word with---"

Out of the corner of his eye, Judash saw the crowd thickening again. It seemed as if all of Jerrysalum had come out to ask her advice, or even just to see her and pay their respect. With immense effort, the twelve apostles had gathered some baskets of food, and repaired some old clothes to give out. That collection, however, was empty within seconds, and yet people kept asking for more. 

Judash saw at once, with great clarity, that their current trajectory would leave them beggars too, and sinful to boot.

"Queen of the Jurads!" yelled the crowd.

"Queen of the Jurads!"

"Queen of the Jurads!"

Krystina basked in the glory. She smiled, and waved, and touched, and healed---the only consistent bit of magic I will ascribe to her. She let people call her Queen of the Jurads. She even used the name herself too. It stopped being "_God_ will save us" or "_we_ will free ourselves from violence and poverty", it became,

"The Queen of the Jurads will lead us all to freedom and prosperity!" yelled Krystina.

Judash fell silent, unable to speak for the rest of the day.

That night, Judash met an unsurprising visitor. The High Priest had come with the same request as before. This time, he spoke with more urgency, and while jingling a bag of coins.

In those two things, Judash saw the solution to all problems at once.

A hundred pieces of gold. That would be enough to feed all the starving animals for years.

And Krystina arrested and scolded. Just to push her off her pedestal, right? Have them explain that she is just a platypus of flesh and blood, who is in way over her head. Just a stern lecture, a talking to, and she'd refrain from sinning. Right?

He took the bag of gold and promised to bring Krystina to the gardens, alone, unsuspecting, tomorrow night.
