# The Mind Camera

All over Shapesong, there were little villages that the Shape War hadn't reached yet. A beautiful tiny forest, a magical sea, a town where all the buildings were made of flowers. All these places were far away from the castle. They had barely noticed anything was going on. It is like this in every country.

The problem, of course, is that control is hungry. It is never satisfied. It will keep growing. Even a single village that was _not_ a Triangle was a bad thing in the eyes of King Triangle. Thousands of Shape Animals had changed their shape to be Triangle, just to make the King happy. Millions of Shape Animals had rebuilt their entire home to be a Triangle, just to make the King leave them alone.

But whoever seeks power and control is like a monster that is always hungry. Despite all this success, King Triangle still traveled further. He still looked all over Shapesong for a patch of "filthy flowers" that weren't Triangles. As if he _wanted_ to find animals who disobeyed him, so he could get angry again. And fill his dungeons, which were already overfull as it was.

The longer this went on, the more these beautiful places disappeared. A field of Sunflowers was forced to lose its Flower shape, and become a Triangle. A city built on water used Rectangles to keep the homes high and dry---well, needless to say, the city is now _underwater_. Some of these places disappeared before the King even arrived, because they were afraid and gave up already.

It's easy to do that. Understandable. When the enemy is so big, and evil, and everywhere. Why even begin to stand up? When surrounded by bullies, it feels wiser to not say anything at all.

But there's always that one place. That one place that is never defeated. The one place that is smart enough to see through the King's lies. The one place with friendships so tight that nothing can break it.

King Triangle had been king for a year now. _All_ of Shapesong had been forced to become a Triangle, or arrested and destroyed.

Only a small village of freedom remained: Flowerfar.

And Prince Circlesnake arrived today. He'd rolled to the edge of Shapesong to escape his father. By now, King Triangle probably realized his son had never actually been a spy.

He almost rolled over two Triangles sunbathing in a field of grain. They were young puppies.

"Hellokiedokie," said one.

"Haven't seen ya before," said the other. 

"Are ya from the Jigsaw Jungle?"

"What? No, I'm---" 

The Prince frowned. Had he finally found freedom? Was this the place? These animals didn't even know he was the Prince! They had no idea.

"Yes, I'm from Jigsaw Jungle. I was hoping to stay here for a while."

"If ya talk to my mother, she will build ya a nice home."

"How much would that cost?" asked the Prince.

The Triangle puppies stopped sunbathing. "I guess ya can always help her with the dishes once in a while?"

Surely he'd heard that wrong. They didn't even have _money_ here? They would build him a house, for free?

He rolled further into Flowerfar. Every home was different, but they were all made of wood and flowers---and they were all beautiful. The homes almost looked like they had _grown_ there, instead of having been built by hand. He couldn't even name the shapes. They were all wobbly, and curvy, and Circles but with holes, and Rectangles but not really. The only shape he could name, of course, was the Flower shape. What his father called the Cloud shape instead, because a Flower had done something to him when he was young or something.

He met the puppies' mother.

"I don't ask for much. Just a home for a Circlesnake," said the Prince. "A safe home. Away from the King. I understand I don't need to pay you?"

"Ah, the King. How is old King Circle these days?" said a large Hexagon dog.

"King ... he's been dead for a while."

"Oh. Shame. He visited us sometimes and complimented my homes."

"He knew this place existed?"

"Yup."

The Prince was baffled. Dumbfounded. Rooted to the spot in confusion, and that's saying something for a Circle. He was thankful his grandfather had let this place be whatever it wanted to be. And he was thankful he never told his son, King Triangle, about it. Probably to protect it.

"I don't need your fancy golden coins or something, no," said the dog. "What would I do with it? You can't eat coins. Can't make buildings with 'em. Useless, really."

"Well, I wouldn't call money useless ..." started the Prince, but he stopped himself. 

He'd always been a Prince. Always lived in a big castle. Never worried about food. Could buy whatever he wanted, whenever he wanted. _Of course_ he thought that money was super important. Of course he believed everyone would like to have more golden coins. It's probably the shape he saw the most when he was a boy. He had never even seen a world without money.

He looked around. The dog's daughters---the puppies who were sunbathing before---were running through the fields now and harvesting the grain. They moved it to another building, which made bread from it. They didn't need money; they simply made food themselves. They didn't need fancy clothes; they were happy running around in the sun, naked.

The dog waved her arms to get his attention again. 

"But, mister snake, I do want ... to ask for something else."

She pointed at a nearby tree. 

"Follow me. I want to see your thoughts."

___

Prince Circlesnake leaned against the tree. Anxious. Shivering. What would it show?

The dog had clearly done this many times. She had probably "read the thoughts" of everyone in Flowerfar. She placed the final shape into the Shaeprint, and its magic came alive.

Sparks flew from the tree. Insects left the leaves alone for a bit. After a few seconds, the sparks combined to create _images_. Images floating in the air. Images so real you thought you could touch them.

All the other villagers were gathered around. They gasped. They yelled. They cheered.

"I _told_ you!" said one puppy. "_That's_ what a Triangle looks like."

"To _you_. My Triangles look different. And better," responded the other.

The Prince blinked. He had to blink a hundred times more before he believed what he saw. And before he realized what it meant.

Everyone had eyes, of course. But were those eyes the same? Did they see the same thing? Maybe what you call _red_, dear reader, looks different than what someone else calls _red_.

In the same way, the Prince and the Dog could look at the same shape ... and see different shapes. The magical tree showed what their eyes _really_ saw. 

When the Prince looked at a Triangle, he saw something big and strong. When he saw a Triangle, it always had the pointy side up, as if ready to wear a crown.

When the Dog looked at the same Triangle, she saw something small and weak. Her Triangle lay on its side. As if it wasn't a Triangle at all, but just one half of a Rectangle, and the other half was missing.

"What you're saying ..." said the Prince. "We don't see the same things?"

"Not necessarily. Why would we? Our eyes are different. My mother always called our eyes the _Mind Camera_. Taking photographs that our minds try to figure out. Well, all our minds are different too!"

"How---" The Prince looked around. His Square was different from what others called a Square. His Hexagon looked different from what others called a Hexagon. Only _one_ shape looked exactly the same to all eyes: the Flower. "How does this even work? How can we ever do anything again!? If what we see isn't the same!?"

This made everyone frown.

"We've been doing just fine until now, haven't we?" said the dog. "We don't need to see the same thing. We just need to agree that we both call it the same. That if I see this, and you see that, we both call it a _Square_."

"I ... I guess we do," mumbled the Prince.

His final bit of faith in his father disappeared. He was trying to turn everything into a Triangle ... to _his eyes_. For all he knew, what he thought was a Triangle, looked completely different to everyone else. Maybe Shapesong was actually shaped like Squares right now. But the King's eyes saw Triangles, and it made him happy.

It made the Prince happy too.

This was a beautiful town. One that knew it didn't matter what you looked like---because what you looked like was _different_ to different eyes. 

He could call this his home.

The next day he woke up early and joined the puppies. He lay on his back in the grain fields. They watched clouds pass by, trying to recognize their shapes.

"That cloud! At the back! Looks like a tiger to me," said a puppy.

"No no," said the Prince. "It clearly looks like a house to me."

"Ya are blind," said another puppy. "Both of ya. That isn't a cloud at all, just a large white bird."

The Prince sat up, trying to look closer. "That other cloud looks more like a bird to me than that one."

They could do this for hours before they found a cloud that looked the same to all of them.

Or until someone blocked their view.

A large and intimidating fox hovered over him.

"_You?_" said Piqafox. As if the word was a mean curse word.

"Don't ya go interruptin' our cloud-watching!" said one puppy.

"It's okay," said the Prince. "I wanted to talk to the Piqarine Painter anyway."

"Who's the Piqarine Painter?" asked the other puppy. "And why do ya say it like they're spooky?"

"Just ... just go cloudwatch somewhere else, okay?" said the Prince. The puppies shrugged and skipped away.

The Prince looked deep in Piqafox' eyes. "I've asked Tessella. She told me to ask you. I want to join the rebels."

Piqafox frowned. "She didn't mention."

"I was never a spy, please, believe me. I just needed to make the King trust me. I never arrested any of you."

"That may be true," said the fox. "But you're still the Prince. You still made our lives _very_ difficult the past year."

"That was just an act!"

"An act of stupidity. All the rebels know that you're a liar."

The Prince sighed. He was afraid of this. He was never going to get a second chance, was he? None of the rebels could look past his Prince shape.

Piqafox surely looked past him as if he wasn't even there. Legends said she could see a color that nobody else could say. Maybe she was staring at something made of that color right now.

"Who's the mayor of this town? The leader?"

"They don't really have one."

"Then it's good the rebels are here. This is the last free town in Shapesong. It needs to defend itself."

"_No,_" said the Prince. "Go away."

"Excuse me? You just said you wanted to join us?"

The Prince rolled into her, bumping her out of the grain fields.

"This town is peaceful. Everyone here is happy. _Don't_ bring them violence and danger!"

"We are the _rebels_. We're the good side."

"Then show it," said the Prince.

They were far away from the magical tree with the Shaeprints. The one that could show what your eyes truly saw. But if they were close, the Prince was quite certain what Piqafox' eyes saw. A town to turn into rebels. A nice place for a fight with King Triangle. 

His eyes saw the most beautiful place on earth, and he would not let it be ruined.

"If you set one foot in this town," he said, "I'm going back to my father. Telling him all your secrets! Ha!"

The grain around Piqafox turned different colors. Red. Blue. Yellow. A color he'd never seen before. 

The Prince and the Fox stared into each other's eyes for what felt like days.

"You have changed, my Prince," she said. Then she turned around and left. 

I am happy to say, dear reader, that Flowerfar never saw anything of the Shape War. They barely had a clue what was going on. The word Triangle was just a friendly shape to them. _The Accident at Shape Castle_ was a fun story they told around campfires, getting all the details wrong. Because it would be _years_ before they learned what actually happened to King Triangle and the Rebelshapes that day.

They continued to live without money, without a leader, without a border or a weapon. They continued to build homes for anyone who wanted to live here. And the Prince continued to fight for it, even when he became King Circlesnake himself.

There's always that one place. The place that keeps dreaming. The place that keeps looking at what could be---what _should_ be---instead of what is. There's always that group that keeps standing up against bullies, and bad kings, and mean soldiers. Who looks for the truth, only believing their own eyes and not someone else's lies. Who says, _this is not the shape I want to see in the world_. 

Why couldn't that be you?
