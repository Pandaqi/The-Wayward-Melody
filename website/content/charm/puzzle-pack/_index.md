Puzzle Packs are **bundles of different puzzles around the same theme**. 

They are basically the opposite of buying one _specific_ puzzle. If you buy a particular Logic/Educational Puzzle, you get a PDF with only puzzles _of that type_.

In a puzzle pack, you only get puzzles **of different types**. Each page has a new one to interest you. It has a balance of language puzzles, math puzzles, visual puzzles, and more. The common thread, here, is their **theme** (and sometimes **difficulty**). Most puzzle packs will center around holidays or topics, modifying all puzzles to be related to it.

{{% double-view-item %}}

{{% section-foldable header="Why would I be interested?" %}}

Below are some extra advantages that puzzle packs have over other puzzles.

* It contains a **very wide variety of puzzles**. For those who are bored easily, or simply very curious about new challenges all the time.
* All puzzles share the same **theme** or **approach**. This helps focus on some relevant topic or maybe current interest of your child. This helps create some connections between the puzzles.
* This makes them ideal as a **gift** for a specific occasion, or something to **keep children engaged** through a holiday or study hour in school.

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="Why would I not be interested?" %}}

Below are some disadvantages that puzzle packs have against other puzzles.

* If you like a puzzle a lot ... then you might be disappointed that it **won't appear again**. Puzzle packs don't go "deep", they go shallow on a lot of different kinds of puzzles.
* They usually contain **fewer puzzles** than other puzzle products, at least in terms of raw numbers. Because every puzzle is unique and we need to create the explanation and design from scratch on each page, we simply can't afford to add hundreds to a pack.

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="How is the product delivered?" %}}

You always get a **PDF** file that contains the entire puzzle pack, usually sorted by difficulty.

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="How can I play?" %}}

You can solve the puzzles **digitally or physically**.

* You can print them, then play with pen and paper.
* You can also load the image into any note-taking app and simply draw over it on your screen!

Some puzzles ask you to cut out pieces or prepare things another way, but those are the exception and will clearly mention this.
