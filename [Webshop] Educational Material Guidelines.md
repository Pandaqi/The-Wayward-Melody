# Educational Material Guidelines

## Rule #1: As textless as possible

I don't understand how so much educational material has walls and walls of text on it. The kids don't understand. It's overwhelming.

As such, simply **show** what to do at the start. 

* Show a "good situation/solution" with a smiley face and green checkmarks
* Show a "wrong situation/solution" with a sad face and red crosses.

**Example:** To teach discrimination. Show a group of things that are the _same_ and happy faces, then show a group of things that are _different_ and unhappy faces. This clearly teaches that you should mark/cross/scribble the groups that are the same and not the others.

## Rule #2: It's obvious when you did it right

As much as possible, the answer reveals something or confirms something, knowing you did it right.

**Example:** To teach numbers. A bunch of random numbers are spread on the page, and it's your job to _connect_ them with lines. Only once you connected all the right ones (1,...,20 in order), do you see a final drawing that you recognize.

## Rule #3: Streamlined Packets

The entirety of a "bite of learning" (such as one step in a specific topic) is a single packet. Don't have _dozens_ of different products all vaguely doing the same thing.

**Example:** Take addition up to 10. There is a single PDF for it, which has _multiple_ pages approaching it in different ways and challenging in different ways.

## Rule #4: Very Light Branding

There's a branding on all materials: by doodles (music motif, dreamcatcher, stars, magic thingies), by fonts, by colors, by general terminology and vibe.

**But** this is very subtle and only reserved for the edges/extremes. The vast majority of all material is simply a white page with the content, no hint where it came from.

Why? Minimalist. Less work. Looks better. And teachers report hating seeing logos (such as Twinkl) on stuff, even cutting them all out.

## Rule #5: Cheap Printing

Mostly use outlined graphics. If filled with color, alternate and make it light colors. Be minimalist as always with number of pages and size of things.

## Rule #6: Easy Escalation

Design the exercises in such a way that they easily transfer/are reused in _further Factions_. Of course, extra changes and layers are always needed, but they should be minimal.

**Example:** 
* The addition to 10 exercise merely asks you to combine two groups of animals and pick the right option that has them combined. (4 tigers + 3 tigers = 7 tigers option.) 
* Then the addition _quiz_ shows you one of those final images and asks you to write down the _number_. (It shows 7 tigers and it's an open question; answer 7) 
* Then the addition _puzzles_ are just a simple rule like "every row/column states how many of a certain animal it should have" (requiring the puzzler to add/subtract). Enclosures are already given: only the _same_ type of animal inside an enclosure. => Or even simpler, always X of the same animal next to each other (usually 2 or 3, but X can go higher and higher as kids can add further, and this X is simply given.)
* Then the addition _escape rooms_ are about collecting/counting tigers all over the place and connecting into a more creative puzzle. Every solution is checked by/related to adding numbers.
* Then the addition _game_ is one of the many Fun Frontier things I envisioned.
