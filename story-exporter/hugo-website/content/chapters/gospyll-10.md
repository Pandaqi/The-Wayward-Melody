# The Gospyll According To A Disappointed Jurad

You know, when I grew up, I heard about our savior every single day. He or She---but most likely a He, somehow---would come one day. And he would be a mighty warrior, magical and powerful, and he'd drive away the oppressive forces and give back these lands to the Jurad people. Our Jurad faith started because we were cast out of our lands, enslaved and beaten, by empires such as the Amori. It seemed only fitting that it would end with a mighty war, in which we freed ourselves and we beat all ugly Amori soldiers into the _dirt_.

Well. The Amori Empire fell some thousand years ago. We have electricity now, and internet, and schools, and air-conditioning in the desert. Ghost-like creatures became Prima, became Apes, became Monkeys, became Humans. And our savior didn't come.

Krystina, you say? What about her?

She wasn't ... what I expected, to say the least. I expected a fierce soldier---and instead we got a weak platypus who spoke out against any violence against Amor. I expected someone who could move mountains and compel crowds to fight---and instead we got a small woman who told us to quit starting wars all the time.

Some Gospylls claim she could do magic---even though we have absolutely no evidence of magic existing anymore! Some claim she came back to life, and appeared to non-believers until they decided to become Krystian. Some say her feet were made of clay, or the cross was made of gold, or that angels sang when she died. Some say that the hours of darkness, after she died, was just a solar eclipse. Some call her a legendary being, taller than a tree, whose footsteps shook the earth. Some call her God himself, shapeshifted into an animal species we still think must be a joke. The later the Gospyll was written, it seems, the more magical and godly Krystina became.

I don't know if I believe the things they say of her. 

But I believe her _message_ now.

The Second Conflict did not end well, for anyone. They have weapons now, I tell you, that could wipe out all of humanity in one blow! Millions of Jurads were shipped off to camps, arrested, murdered. And for what? Another useless war. Another excuse about revenge and saving and the promised land. Among my brothers, who have lost many family members, there is much talk of taking revenge. There is much talk, again, of some savior that will push all non-Jurads from the world and give our lands back to us.

When is that savior coming? I don't know. Maybe they saw the state of Somnia, with humanity finally on top, and decided to back up the car and go rule the planet Mares instead.

Maybe I don't _want_ them to come anymore. We've had many messiahs before, and they freed us for a year or so, spilling blood in the process. And then they left, or sinned, and we were back to being enslaved again! And then I haven't even mentioned the other big religion that the next prophet, Muhammed, started! Which gave us the holy conquests, more wars, more killing, more taking of land.

The cycle has to end somewhere.

Krystina brought a new voice into the world. One that took the best parts of Jurad faith, but changed the message to one of non-violence, kindness, acceptance and forgiveness.

I probably won't convert to Krystianity, not like many of my family members have. But perhaps I should not have dismissed her message and called the whole story fake and useless all my life.

The cycle of sin, war and oppression has to end somewhere.

So, when my estranged aunt smiled and asked me what I was thinking about, as we were seated around an Easter dinner and enjoying some hot cross buns, I _obviously_ could not tell her my actual thoughts. Instead, I said: "Oh, you know, nothing."

"Don't look so glum, boy. It's a celebration! It's Easter! New life, new beginnings. Don't you want to search eggs with the other kids?"

"I am sixteen, aunt Yehma."

"So what?"

"I am tall enough to see all the hiding places at once."

"Being tall is no reason for being _boring_!"

I shook my head. I was getting too old for this. All the bright colors. And the egg painting---I've painted more eggs than I've painted anything else!

"You know your brothers and sisters want you there," said aunt Yehma. She pointed through the large glass kitchen windows. His younger brother and sister were running around the garden, screaming whenever they found another egg. Regularly, his parents would frown at each other, unsure if _they_ hid that egg. And whether they hid it _this year_ or _last year_.

"So?" I challenged Yehma, crossing my arms.

"Easter is about sacrifice for the greater good," said Yehma with a smile. "About standing strong together ... by allowing yourself to be vulnerable. Krystina was weak, small, never angry, never took revenge, never sinned. And look what came of it. One third of the world is her family now."

Silence fell. Most of my own family was running about the garden too. They had found ten of the eggs already.

"Noah! Noah!" called my sister, running inside and making a muddy mess of the carpet. "Come help us! There's an egg stuck in the tree and we can't reach it!"

Yehma winked. I groaned and launched himself from the chair with great effort.

"Fine, _fine_." I trudged into the garden. "But only this once!"

I tapped the tree, hoping the egg would just fall out. It didn't.

"Who even put it there?" I muttered, grumbled, and grumpied. "I'm taller than my dad."

I stood on my toes. My fingers grazed the egg, but I couldn't grab it.

"Ugh!" 

My siblings just smiled at me expectantly, as I started climbing the tree. How long had it been since I'd done that? I was supposed to be much stronger now, a sixteen year old man, but my arms trembled and I feared I would fall down any second now.

I _did_ fall, but someone saved me.

Once the egg was at my eye level, I saw something else behind it. Two bright, large eyes. They were attached to a bunny. She slithered around the tree as if she were a snake, trying to get her basket unstuck from behind a branch.

"Oh. Hello there. Didn't know humans could fly these days. Or, well, what you did was only half-flying---just the _falling_ part, I guess."

I can tell you, dear reader, that the previous Gospylls don't do it justice. Absolutely not! They don't use enough magical words to explain how it feels to be in the presence of someone so close to God.

"A ... talking ... bunny?" said I, my voice cracking.

My siblings laughed at me. I went red in the face and felt like crushing the egg. 

"Is this a joke?" I yelled. I reached for Astra Buni, as if trying to rip off a mask, or see who was behind the costume.

"I _was_ joking," said Astra, "I know humans can't fly. Not unless they go and sit in those metal containers. But _I_ am most assuredly not a joke!"

I looked to the bunny. And down at my siblings. And at the egg. And back at the bunny again.

I leaned forward.

"The Easter Bunny?" I whispered. "You are real?"

"When will humans learn that it's silly to ask a living being if they're real ..." muttered Astra to herself. "If they're not real, they can't answer!"

I was speechless, frozen halfway up a tree. My gaze turned to the egg. Was that a slight glow around it? Didn't it seem bigger than others? Was it even a chocolate egg? And that pattern---mesmerizing, beautiful, wonderful. Unlike anything I'd seen before. Unlike anything I'd ever painted myself, that's for sure.

Astra's soft paws fell over my hands. 

"Dear Noah," she said in a warm voice. "God has chosen _you_. If you accept this magical egg as a gift, you will accept their mission."

"God ... chosen ... me? Gift? Mission?" I invented some new words and grammar rules as I stuttered. I am creative like that.

"Please, erm, accept it quickly," said Astra, her voice raising rapidly in pitch. "And then use the egg to avert disaster."

I've read other Gospylls that claim I wasn't going to accept the egg. I want to clearly state that they are _wrong_. I'm not _stupid_. Magical Egg. Chosen by God. It easily removed my grumpy mood and my embarrassment in case it was a prank. 

I wanted to grab the egg, with both hands! But as I touched it, the earth shook and I saw the disaster Astra was talking about.

___

We all know that some Passion plays take it a bit too far. I need not mention the one by our local high school who thought it was a good idea to actually have their Krystina stay on a cross for three days. For "realism". Or the one were they arrested any audience member who said things that were against Momo's Law. Or the one in which I participated, years ago, where I had to run over the stage naked. Because that's what one of the apostles had supposedly done when they heard of Krystina's arrest in the middle of the night.

This year, our local elementary school had wanted to tell "the whole story". Not just the New Testament---Krystina and her life---but also the Old Testament. They had reached the part about Noah's ark and the Great Flood.

Yeah, so, it turns out you can't empty the local lake inside a small community theatre and expect it to stay there.

The streets turned to rivers. Chairs, props, wigs, a wooden cross, and more was dragged out of the theatre and onto the streets. The children playing the apostles followed soon. Some were good swimmers; others clearly not. Parents yelled for their children. A teacher managed to grab a lantern and stick to their spot. They became a safe haven for anyone dragged away by the strong currents. It didn't help that our street was on a hill and clearly sloped downwards.

Some hundred people, young and old, were pushed down the street at an alarming pace. 

The waves burst into homes who were unlucky enough to have an open door or window. My parents ran into the kitchen and already stood in a shallow layer of water.

I had a great view of the events from high in the tree. Astra Buni looked at me expectantly.

"What ... what can I ... what do you expect me to ..."

A baby was ripped from her mother's arms and now drifted aimlessly down the street. A high wave splashed over her---and the baby was gone from the surface. The baby's final cries put me into action.

I grabbed the egg and _jumped_. It just felt right. It felt like I was born to do it.

With Astra's magic, you need not say a spell, or think of the thing you want. In a way, the spell is already done _for you_, it's all predetermined and you have no choice. It's saved in the pattern she painted.

Life, I guess, is merely about unlocking and experiencing the magic.

I can confidently say I never before, in my life, jumped a distance equal to a soccer field. But that day I did.

And I landed among the waves, but they did not touch me. The egg exploded, a ring of light around me. And the river split in two.

I stretched out my arms, which seemed longer too, and held the drowning baby to my chest. A wall of water on my left, a wall of water on my right, and I helped everyone get up and walk onto dry land. Once all the people were safe, the egg had sucked in most of the water and disappeared.

One boy, whom I later learned had played the role of Momo, glared at me and said something about stealing his spotlight. Everyone else rejoiced and thanked me with a thousand kisses. I would have preferred a thousand chocolate eggs, but hey, you can't have it all.

News reporters arrived. A tall woman, with far too much lipstick on, pushed a microphone in my face.

"It seems a miracle! We could be talking about many deaths now, but thanks to you, everyone survived! They say you performed magic---what do you say to that?"

"Erm, well, first of all I'd recommend that the school doesn't do this again. Second of all, it wasn't me. It was the Easter Bunny!"

The woman lowered the microphone gave him a sympathetic look. "Awww. You still believe, do you?"

I blushed, then grinned. "Yes. Yes I do."

___

After talking with an animal, I must say my perspective shifted on a few things. For example, I noticed that Le Bjib---the holy text of Krystianity---rarely mentions that a character is human. For all we know ... the whole thing might be talking animals instead!

Then again, every major religion just copied what came before and added something on top. Juradism had the Old Testament. Krystianity simply copied that, then added the New Testament with Krystina's life. Then the Isla Mira came and copied _all that_---changing some small things, keeping only what they like---and added a new prophet and more story on top.

A bit like ... like what these Gospylls are doing too.

Astra Buni could not stay, as she still had to deliver millions of eggs this Easter. But she returned when the feast was over.

"So it is true?" I asked. "You are the final remaining apostle of Krystina? What was she like? Did she teach you magic?"

Astra shook her head. "I don't like to talk about it. But no, she barely understood her own magic, and did not teach me."

"Then how did you get it? Chosen by God?"

"There's an entire world and history outside of the scriptures, Noah. In fact, you'll find _most_ of the world there." Astra looked to the star-littered sky, as if wishfully longing back for the days Krystina was still alive. "I was born far from here, and brought up by the notorious Pricecats."

"Who are they?"

Astra gnashed violently. "I will be forever annoyed at how human's erased the entire history of talking animals, magic, and especially the old gods. The stories I could tell you ..."

"Then do!"

"But I must leave." Astra blindly painted a few eggs, even as she hopped away and talked to me. "Preparations for next year's Easter must begin already. And there is always someone in need of help, somewhere. Always the Anti-God, which some call Satan, with his evil plans, trying to make God look bad. I wish you good luck, Noah."

"That's all? No instructions?"

"Look on the bright side: crucifixion is not a punishment anymore anywhere in the world."

Within a few hops, Astra Buni was far away. 

I sunk to my knees, looking at the stars. Then I closed my eyes and folded my hands in prayer.

"But Krystina, where do I go now? What is my mission? What am I to do? There is so much suffering in the world. Wars, dictators, famine, child labor, bad systems of education, high chocolate egg prices, povert---"

"STOP WAKING ME UP FOR SILLY QUESTIONS. APPLY YOURSELF. YOU CAN SEARCH THINGS ON THE INTERNET NOW."

After talking to animals and splitting a river with a magical egg, honestly, the booming voice that appeared didn't even faze me. "I can ... choose?"

"OF COURSE."

"What if I say something God doesn't want said?"

"GOD GAVE YOU THE MAGIC OF THE PROPHET BECAUSE HE SAW YOUR HEART, AND IT WAS THE BEST HE COULD FIND. AS YOU HAVE FAITH IN GOD; SO DOES HE HAVE FAITH YOU WILL DO RIGHT. THEY LEARNED FROM THEIR MISTAKES IN THE PAST."

"What if I ... go on a holiday first?"

"THEN YOU PROBABLY NEEDED THE REST."

"What if I ... believe heaven and hell don't exist, and are just invented to make children scared and obedient?"

"HEAVEN RECENTLY PASSED FREEDOM OF SPEECH LAWS. YOU'RE FINE."

"What if I ... am not willing to sacrifice my life for this mission, like you did?"

The voice didn't answer anymore.

We have a choice. We always have a choice. And we can choose to be good, without the threat of hell or the dangling carrot called heaven. We can choose to update religious beliefs, because that's what happened every time a new messiah was chosen.

Easter is a reminder of all that. We can be forgiven, we can be humble, we can forego violence. And we can start again, let Spring come once more, and be fresh and joyful.

It seemed, to me, that the world was ripe for another messenger. And oh, I believe this time he might be sixteen, and a reluctant hero, and very handsome, and very tall, and very wise, and---

_Translater's Note: The original Gospyll continued for seventy more pages, listing more positive adjectives to describe himself. We cut it off, for your sake. God also might reconsider his choice of messiah this time._

These were the Tales of Easter. Ten of the most important Gospylls, discovered by archeologists after lots of digging and piecing together parchment. Some things might have been lost in translation. Many details might have been made up. But all the Gospylls agree on the general story, such as that Krystina existed, and that something really significant happened in Jerrysalum around 30 years after the Soulsaw. We might never figure out exactly what it was. At least, not until God shows their face and explains it, or in the unlikely event that we find video footage from ancient times.

But this new God was smart. They didn't show their face. They only worked through messengers and minor interference. Let's hope they survive longer than the old godchildren, Ardex and his siblings, who walked around the lands themselves and interfered with _everything_. Whatever happens, I am sure Timothy will see the event as another cause for celebration. And maybe, one day, you will get a visit from Astra Buni too, as you wander off and get lost during the egg hunt. And she might give you a magical egg, and confirm or deny the whole story, and your life will never be the same again.

But one thing was for sure: Noah would have to tell a pretty big unbelievable story to replace some more big unbelievable lies.

Happy Easter.
