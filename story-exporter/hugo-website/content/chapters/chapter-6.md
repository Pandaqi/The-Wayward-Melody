# The Lines Upside-Down

The corridors inside The Wall were narrow. Jenna barely fit. She imagined an adult would have to crouch, and probably still bump their head against the ceiling a million times.

Jenna followed them for a while. They confirmed just how huge The Wall had been. But now it was being broken down, bit by bit, and they had already cut it down in half.

And then she arrived at a dead end. She imagined this was the center of The Wall. Its beating heart, its magical core.

She found ... papers. Lots of papers. Sketches. Notes written by Basco himself. His handwriting was barely legible; he had a habit of mixing uppercase and lowercase letters at random. 

But Jenna didn't need to read his notes to know something was wrong with the sketch. A large scroll, easily five times as large as Jenna, contained the blueprint for The Wall. Every section, all measurements, and instructions for the dwarven builders.

Then it dawned on Jenna.

Why _her_ letters were on the wrong side, and the Other People's glyps were on the wrong side. Why Basco's town was barely large enough to be called a town, while he'd given the Other People a huge plot of land.

It was a silly mistake. 

Basco had, accidentally, drawn The Wall upside-down. Inside-out! It seemed Bibi, his granddaughter, had been right. He wasn't a very good builder after all. He should've been called Basco the Barely Capable. Perhaps Basco the Architect of Disaster.

Footsteps sounded behind Jenna. She yelped, then covered her own mouth. The footsteps quickened. Strong hands were placed on her shoulders, dragging her away from the blueprint.

"Finally!" yelled the voice behind her. It was Bibi herself. "Now you can all see the truth about Basco! As the dwarves were finishing The Wall, they realized Basco's mistake. But it was too late now. The Other People got stuck on the _good side_, which Basco had wanted for himself. And we ... us ... got stuck on the tiny bad side."

Jenna relaxed. "You snitch! So you finally agree with me? You're not arresting me again?"

Bibi grinned. "Having you arrested was the best thing that happened to us, wasn't it? How else would you have figured all this out ..."

Jenna playfully slapped Bibi's shoulder. "You did it on purpose! You gave me away because you hoped I would break down The Wall!"

Bibi shrugged. "You are _so_ predictable."

Many of the papers weren't that interesting. Just lots of numbers and sketches. But at the bottom of the pile, they found the most important note of all.

The final one that Basco wrote, before The Wall was sealed and completed.

_The Wall is done. All seventeen sections. Taller than the tallest mountain. A true masterpiece! And now ... finally! No more loud talking. No more noise and disruptions from the big mouths. Calm ... and silence. I can finally think again and enjoy my final years in peace. Signed: Basco._

Jenna dropped the paper. "That's all? The Wall was built just because some people talked too loud?"

"The Other People just happened to have large ears and big mouths. This made them talk loudly, yes. But they also talked _a lot_, because they loved using language, and they were very creative with it. Coming up with poems, inventing better words. Even worse ..." 

Bibi revealed even more papers, which were previously hidden in some dark corner. It contained hundreds of poems and stories that the Other People had written. "The word _Wall_ was invented by _them_! At first, Basco wanted to call it _The Silent Fence_, because that was the only word we had back then. Not very intimidating, is it?"

Jenna snickered. The Silent Fence. It would not even keep out sheep!

They had to leave the treasure room. Because, well, a few minutes later there _was_ no treasure room anymore. They had reached this part of The Wall and broken it down too.

It was now, truly, no more than a low _fence_.

Families from one side ran to the other side. They looked for their lost children, or parents, and even pets.

Jenna's parents risked their lives to reach her as quickly as possible. They held her in their arms and raised her while cheering.

Not everyone was immediately convinced. Bibi needed to reveal the notes they found, providing _evidence_ of the silly reasons The Wall was actually built. In due time, this made most people accept The Wall was gone. And that this was good. Except for that one guard who still desperately wanted to shoot _something_.

For a while, Jenna was the only one who could translate. She used all the material from The Wall whenever needed. The glyphs to spell _letters_, their sounds to say _phonemes_, which created _syllables_, which she could connect into larger bricks called _words_. And by simply placing brick after brick, the words combined into _sentences_, which could communicate _anything_ she wanted! Language was easy!

She used them over and over, practicing every day, until they became second nature and she didn't need them anymore. She had learned the Other's language and could speak it fluently.

Within years, their languages merged into one. They found phonemes they could both _speak_, and glyphs they could both _read_.

The Other People agreed to talk with less volume. Because they didn't _need_ to do it anymore. Everyone understood each other just fine. 

After reading the notes, Jenna also knew exactly how much they still had to pay the dwarves. And she knew they could never pay it. Because the dwarves wanted precious diamonds, and they had none of that. Basco had even lied to the dwarves and claimed they had diamonds to spare.

When the Dwarf King visited their lands one day, she was frightened and stuttered an apology.

"Nah, don't ya worry about that," said the Dwarf King. "You've paid us in entertainment. It was really funny seeing Basco make mistake after mistake! Haha! Building a massive wall the wrong way around! _Hilarious!_"

Jenna breathed a sigh of relief. She held a nice feast for the dwarves and asked the big-mouthed people to sing their pretty songs and recite their poems.

When the Dwarf King left, he made fun of them and said: "If you ever need us again, let us know. Maybe you want a home built upside-down? A door that can't open? A catapult that only shoots downward? A chair that falls apart as soon as you sit on it? Bye bye!"

Jenna didn't really laugh along. Because she had realized she'd made the same mistake as Basco. And she didn't tell anyone about it, ashamed as she was.

The Other People? They didn't really speak a _new_ language. Why would they? All of them used to be _the same people_. All of them used to speak exactly the same language no more than a hundred years ago. 

No, the Other People were just _playing_ with language again.

One night, as Jenna looked a bit longer at their glyphs, she realized the truth. They ... they were just _her_ letters, but upside-down. When the big-mouthed people spoke, they made the _same sounds_ as her, just _backward_. A very playful way to communicate. Something very simple, yet she missed it completely. And so she forgave Basco for building The Wall inside-out---she didn't forgive him for building a wall in the first place.

And now all traces of The Wall were gone. Dead, boring silence was also gone. The two peoples had become one again.

And they lived happily ever after, with no wall to separate them ever again.
