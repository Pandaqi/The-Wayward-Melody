# Paused Pirates

![Pirate Map for Chapter 1](images/chapter-1@0.25x.png)

_Round and round the rovers go. Their ship was special, you must know. It had no steering wheel, it had no fins. It could NOT MOVE at all, and so the story begins._

Some kids, when bored, decide to go outside and play. Some bored kids will play a game, or watch a movie, or go to bed early. And if they're _really_ bored, they might even start eating vegetables and do their homework.

And then there are some kids who are _so_ bored ... that they become _pirates_.

"It's a beautiful day, is it not?" said Rovy. 

He stood on a raft. A bunch of wooden logs combined with rope, which now drifted away from the island. It had taken him a year to build the raft. And still, it was only big enough for two people. Even worse, it drifted wherever it wanted. They had no way to turn it around. No steering wheel, no paddle. Even if they knew where treasure was---and they did not---the raft could not get there.

"Aye!" yelled his friend Pyra.

"I ... what?" Rovy looked confused. "I thought you were going to say something."

"Aye! As in, you know, Pirate-speak for _yes_."

Rovy nodded. "Aye, aye."

"Oh. Are you hurt?"

Rovy sighed. They needed to work on their Pirate-speak. 

And then he saw it. A ship. An actual ship. A proper ship!

It bounced on the waves. It shimmered in the sunlight. It probably did not see the pirates coming, because it stayed where it was and did not flee.

"As I said," yelled Rovy, "a beautiful day for some _pirating_! Arrr!"

"Are ... what?" Pyra saw the ship too. She grabbed her sword in excitement. "I thought you were going to say something."

"Never mind," said Rovy. "Get ready to steal their ship!"

With every minute, their raft drifted closer. With every wave, the ship became bigger. Rovy was a bit disappointed: it wasn't the biggest ship he'd ever seen. But it was better than a raft, that's for sure. Much better for finding treasures.

They were close now. So close that the ship cast a shadow over their raft. For a second, Pyra even thought that night had fallen.

"Ready?" whispered Rovy.

"I guess."

"ATTACK!"

They jumped off their raft. The ship, like most ships, had ropes and rails on the side. This made it easy to climb upwards, until they rolled over the deck.

Rovy scrambled to his feet. His sword swung all around him. Then, and he probably should have done this first, he opened his eyes.

The ship was empty. Him and Pyra almost hit each other in confusion. There was not a single person here. Not a single sound. It might as well have been a ghost ship.

"A bit disappointing," said Pyra.

"But the ship is OURS!" 

Rovy pulled something from his pocket. It was his personal pirate flag. He'd drawn it this morning, right after he decided to become a pirate. As a flag, dear listener, it was ... fine. He'd drawn an angry face surrounded by fire. But as a pirate flag, it wasn't great. It looked like a sad smiley surrounded by seaweed, and that simply won't scare your average fisherman.

Still, Rovy hoisted his new flag. Soon, it fluttered in the wind, at the highest point of the ship.

Pyra had been searching for a steering wheel. And she found none.

"I don't understand," she said. "All the stories say that ships have a steering wheel right there!"

Rovy stood still in the middle of the ship. Yes, there _was_ something weird about this ship. 

When he was on the raft, he kept losing his balance. He kept falling off, because the ground beneath his feet tilted and changed. And, even worse, his socks kept getting wet.

But this ship ...

"It's not moving _at all_!" he yelled. "Look!"

Both of them hung over the side. They looked down at the water. The ocean tried to push and pull the ship. It sent its best waves, the one who finished Wavy University, but it was not enough. The ship simply _did not move_.

"A bit disappointing," said Pyra again. "I don't suppose treasure is coming _to us_, no?"

"No," said Rovy, scratching his chin. "No, I've never heard of a treasure chest with legs."

"Aye."

"Aye. This is a problem. But we're experienced pirates! We've seen it all! We can solve this---"

"We have only been pirates for a day."

The two of them fell silent. Then Rovy had an idea. 

"The ship probably belongs to someone else. Someone who fled. Or fell overboard. Or for _some_ mysterious reason left the ship behind."

"So ... there is probably a _map_ somewhere!" 

"And food!"

"And a map!"

Rovy went looking for food. Pyra went looking for a map.

As evening fell, only one of them returned happy.

Pyra smiled as she placed a piece of paper on the deck. She carefully unrolled it, until it was flat on the floor. The map was so big that it could have been a carpet.

Most of all, the map had a few marks. Red crosses that said "something important is here". It also showed the island where Rovy and Pyra lived, which was still nearby: _Isle of Diro_.

"Great work, fellow pirate," said Rovy. "We'll set sail for that first red mark ... as soon as we can move."

"How did your search go? Did you find any food."

Rovy's lips turned down, his face sour. He patted his pockets and showed a gray blob. "Just this thing. I don't even know if it's food or not! It could be a stone---"

Suddenly, the deck below them turned. Both of them fell down, their faces pressing into the map. That hurts, dear listener, make no mistake. But Rovy was already back on his feet, and smiling.

Because this could only mean one thing: their ship had started moving.
