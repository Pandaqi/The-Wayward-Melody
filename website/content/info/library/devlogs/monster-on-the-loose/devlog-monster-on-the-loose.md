This game had a bit of a strange trajectory from start to finish. That's why this devlog will be rather short and leave some gaps.

* I really wanted to create a "hidden movement" game for _years_.
* And then, a week before Halloween, I conceived the idea of combining this idea with hunting _monsters_. One player would be a monster with specific powers/weaknesses, the others had to track them down. Not only find _where_ they are but also _who_ they are.
* This felt very promising, so I wrote a complete plan, then started work. Except ... it was a _lot_ of work, and I got distracted, and then Halloween arrived when I was only 50% done. I had prepared all the illustrations, the final rulebook, the general design elements, all the "preproduction" stuff---and then I had to quit.
* So, one year later, I picked the project back up. This time, I had 3 weeks left until Halloween! I would finish it! Hooray!
* Except I got sick for a week, then had money troubles for a week, and then I only had a few days left for what was still a _massive_ pile of work.

And that's where this devlog starts :)

## Downscoping

My plan was too ambitious. I had to simplify, which is good for creative projects/products in any case, and in a way that reduced material and drawings needed.

So ...

* PROBLEM: Before, each monster (16 types) had their own unique Map Tile. You picked that monster? Then include their Map Tile in the map. Easy-peasy. But ... 16 unique map tiles is a _lot_. And if you decided to include many monsters, you would have a MASSIVE map too.
  * SOLUTION: Map tiles are attached to _two_ monsters. Fewer tiles needed, random map size more controlled, easier to find the one you needed.
* PROBLEM (related to the first): I didn't have enough illustrations for Locations to let each one appear only once. Growing the deck would be problematic anyway because then the monster would have to search 50+ cards each time to find the one with the location they moved into this turn.
  * SOLUTION: Reduce # map tiles, reduced locations _per map_, so each location only appears ONCE guaranteed. Also, each location card shows the _icons_ of the map tile where it is.
* PROBLEM: My initial plan had map tiles cut into nice grids, with locations following the grid. This felt necessary to allow map tiles to connect _in any way_. This, however, made random maps look both boring and not organic enough to allow the monster to "slip away" in interesting ways.
  * SOLUTION: Let go of the grid, except for _dots_ placed at the edges of maps. As long as you line up those dots, any map tiles will connect properly, while looking organic and having a lot of variance.
* PROBLEM: I've become increasingly annoyed when playing my own print-and-play games at flimsy paper pawns being too hard to grab and too easy to knock over.
  * SOLUTION: Make them CONES! Make the map spaces circles.
* PROBLEM: Many ideas for monsters or special actions were just a little too complicated. They didn't fit nicely on a card. They needed five sentences to explain instead of at most two.
  * SOLUTION: Simplify, simplify, simplify. Whenever possible, make something either a core rule of the game (so several monsters don't need to repeat it), or split it up and assign it to other places. Most of my ideas from a year ago are pretty good and fun, but I still had to cut 50% away because it wasn't simple and streamlined enough.

## Design

I would love to have the time and resources to make these designs absolutely amazing. But, alas, I have to compromise and pick a few simple (efficient) ways to dress up the game so that it's "professional enough".

In this case,

* I combined some free textures and AI-generated textures into something resembling an old, Halloween-y, brown, vintage map. I reused this absolutely everywhere. Sometimes very obviously, like the entire map tile background. Sometimes very subtly, like a 5% opacity overlay on top of the entire Monster Card.
* The fonts I chose a year ago were _awesome_, they do a lot of heavy lifting.
* I chose three simple pieces of imagery: a skull, a grunge cross, a curly border decoration. These were reused everywhere.
* With designs like these, a subtle glow around pretty much everything makes it all come together much better and adds useful contrast. Again, sometimes the glow will be obvious (when I want it to be), sometimes it's so subtle you might not register it as glow around the object.

And then I just used all illustrations I already had to create the Monsters, Hunters, Locations, Maps, etcetera. I forgot to create anything for the _clock_ in the game (and some other parts), so I had to be creative and combine existing elements and textures to get close to the look of the other elements.

## Rules: Checks & Improvements

Reading back my rules from a year ago, I immediately noticed some odd choices and potential cuts.

### Monster vs Hunter Balance

For example, the rules said:

> On a Hunter's turn, they MOVE and INVESTIGATE. Investigation means asking the monster if they've been at any of the locations adjacent to yours ("yes/no" answer)

Let's do a basic count here. First game. Three map tiles. 24 locations, most connected to 3--5 others. Let's assume 4 players: 3 hunters, 1 Monster. If investigation works like this ... they will find that monster in no time :p 

In one round, they can check 10--15 locations. In two rounds they've checked the entire map. Not great, eh?

I changed investigation to only be about two spaces: your current one (completely forgot that) and one adjacent space of choice. Still strong, but not overpowered.

Another example were the Monster powers. I was afraid of making them too _strong_, so my original ideas added lots of little safeguards and disadvantages. 

* Now that I knew the size of the maps and the final rules ... I basically removed all those little constraints. We _need_ Monsters to have one very powerful advantage or move, otherwise it's still a bit too easy for Hunters to find them.
* Similarly, the uncertainty of _which_ Monster someone is, plays an important role in leveling the playing field. Hunters will take the first few rounds to figure out _who_ you are, in most games, to effectively chase you down afterwards. Without that uncertainty, it becomes too easy, because Hunters know exactly where your neighbourhood is and how you can (or can't) move.

### Deadlocks & Insta-Wins

The biggest issue with board game rules is accidentally creating deadlocks or instant wins.

* OLD RULE: The old rules said a Monster draws 3 Locations at the start and picks the one _furthest_ from their hideout to start.
  * PROBLEM: If they're unlucky, their starting Location is right next to a few Hunters, and they're caught immediately. If they're unlucky, they physically CAN'T get home in time. If they're lucky ... they spawn right next to their hideout :p
  * SOLUTION: They draw 4 locations and pick whichever one they want, but it must be at least 4 moves away from their Hideout.
* OLD RULE: The Clock moves 1 Hour forward for **every turn**, even if you did nothing.
  * PROBLEM: Why the heck did I do that!? The clock only has 12 hours. With 4 players, a "day" only last 3 rounds, or 3 Monster Moves :p
  * SOLUTION: The clock only moves after the Monster's turn. Only Special Actions (which the monster can use for some big power/advantage) can advance the clock further (as an easy way to balance it against having too much time)
  * (Also, this was the moment I realized how important the clock was, and how it would be fun to add multiple alternative clocks to the game that you can play with.)
* OLD RULE: The "basic monsters" (to use for your first game) simply had the goal of "get home to your hideout". 
  * PROBLEM: The nice thing was that the rulebook could just say "Monster tries to reach their goal" and its true for the whole game/all expansions, no further note. The _downside_ ... is that this is WAY too easy to achieve once advanced rules are enabled. There's a massive gap between "black cat just needs to take a few steps and get home" and "grim reaper needs to survive 5 days"
  * SOLUTION: I changed it so the base game _overrides_ the goal with "get to your hideout without being caught". Only once you play advanced modes/expansions, does the rule simply become "check the monster's unique goal".
* OLD RULE: Some Action Cards were way overpowered, while others were pretty useless if you happened to be a Monster/Hunter that could already do the same thing pretty easily/cheaply.
  * SOLUTION: A rewrite of actions to focus more on things that people would always _want_ to do ... but _can't_ in most cases. It meant more duplicate actions (like, the same card appearing 5x times in the deck), but that's fine.

Once this was done, I did a quick "sanity check" by doing a fake 4-player game. The issue with hidden movement is, of course, that I can't test it alone ... because I am both Monster and Hunter and obviously know where I am :p But I can test for objective things like deadlocks, insta-wins, unbalanced numbers, etcetera.

This test showed that the map was about the right size and Hunters/Monster about equally powerful now. So I finished this "base game" so I could print it and test it at Halloween.

Because, yes, I still had too little time and had to push 100% completing the "expansions" (2-star and 3-star monsters) to _later_.

## Playtest & Polish

Okay, so, we tested the game on Halloween. Well, the lobotomized version of the game missing the expansions, some polish, and some clarifications in the rules. 

As expected, this made the game way more of a _test_ than an actual _fun game_. (Usually my games are at least functional and pretty fun to play already before I let family members waste time on it :p)

Below is my long list of notes that I wrote down as we played.

### MONSTER

* CONFIRMATION (good!): The Special Action cards are definitely needed for game balance and variation. A 12-hour clock also seems the perfect length.
* CLARIFY: Going back to a previous location _ends your turn_. (You can't move afterwards.)
* CLARIFY: If playing multiple monsters, the clock only moves _once_ per round (after the last monster in turn order).
* CLARIFY: Change the explanatory image for rooftops to use actual spaces instead of abstract dots. (They can be misconstrued to mean the smaller dots on the map.)
* GHOST: Goal is too hard. "Survive 5 days" takes too long.
  * SOLUTION: Make it 3 days.
* GHOST: Improve image to add a _hunter_ icon. (So it immediately reminds you that you can only walk over rooftops _if_ there's a hunter next to the same one.)
* SCARECROW: Getting home is too restrictive. "Visit 5 spaces in your neighborhood" is way too easy to block once players are (pretty) sure you are the Scarecrow.
  * SOLUTION: "Visit 5 spaces from one and the same neighborhood."
* SPIDER: Catching Hunters too hard. Used to be "If someone starts + ends on a space in your Path, you catch them."
  * SOLUTION: "If someone stands on a space in your Path" (so start OR end, at any time)

### HUNTERS

* SIMPLIFY: Turn the main action into "you do ONE thing: MOVE or INVESTIGATE or CATCH"
  * Used to be "you move and then investigate/catch", where investigation is 2 spaces and catch is 1 space.
  * Not only is the old rule less streamlined, it also turned out to be overpowered. (Moving _and_ investigating 2 spaces, when hunters block monster movement, is just too strong.)
* MR HOLMES: Investigate/catch all spaces of the same type is overpowered.
  * SOLUTION: I probably meant "IF on a special space, Investigate all such spaces". (Which excludes blank spaces + doesn't allow _catching_ too.)

### MAP

* BUG: A few roads somehow half-disappear in the exported PDF? Should just tweak them a little bit until the error goes away.
* CLARIFY: When two different road types meet (one normal, one tunnel), it counts as a _tunnel_.
* SETUP: The map should really scale with the number of players, not necessarily number of monsters.
  * SOLUTION (simplest): "Recommendation: include at least one monster per player"
* DAY RESET: I completely failed to consider what to do with Hunters when a new day starts. The Monster resets (Path gone, pick new start). But what about Hunters? Do they stay in the same place? Force them into random locations?
  * SOLUTION 1: Simply allow Hunters to pick new locations --> this is too freeform and too powerful if Hunters already have a strong sense about where your hideout is.
  * SOLUTION 2: Force Hunters apart with some simple rule. 
    * "Each Hunter must be on a different map tile" -> too freeform still OR impossible if playing with smaller map
    * "Each Hunter at least X spaces apart" -> too much work to figure out + discussions about _who_ moves and what way
    * "Each Hunter must be on a DIFFERENT type of space (or all on the SAME type of space)" -> this one is the best "rule", but the randomness of the map doesn't guarantee this is possible, and it's still too much discussion and figuring out (which takes too much time between days)
  * SOLUTION 3 (really thinking out of the box here): Instead of moving the pawns, _move the map tiles_. Everyone stays where they are, you just shuffle the map -> I like this, but only as an addition to a better core rule.
  * SOLUTION 4: Hunters simply draw random locations at the start of each day :) (These locations are _not_ available to the the monster, to balance it: you have no choice where you start, BUT at least you know your starting location is _not_ where the monster is.)
* BALANCING: Forcing a monster's hideout to be in their "neighborhood" is a really nice rule. But ... if the other players are very sure about what type you are, they can basically just lock down that neighborhood and you're done.
  * SOLUTION: Either your _hideout_ OR your _starting location_ (each day) must be in your neighborhood.

So, to be clear, the "DAY RESET" is solved with a combination of solution 3 and 4.

* Each day, the map randomly shuffles around. (To get variation + the monster/hunters can't be stuck in cycles walking the same routes.)
* Then, each Hunter draws their starting location.
* Then, the Monster picks their starting location from the remaining deck.
  * They draw 4 cards at a time and pick the first one that is _valid_.
  * If their hideout is in their neighborhood, any single one is valid. (So they just pick one of those first 4, as the old rules say.)
  * If not, only locations that are in their neighborhood are valid. (So they must _start_ every day in their neighborhood.)

That last bullet point actually was the hardest one to figure out. How to make this as streamlined as possible? How to explain it with the fewest words and fewest friction? Eventually, keeping the "draw 4 cards at a time" from the "first game rules" and extending it to "keep doing it until you get a valid starting Location" did the trick.

And it's an important trick. The only reason our test games fell apart was because of that 100% fixed requirement that your Hideout is in your monster's neighborhood. Now you have the uncertainty about which of the two it is + the monster has some variation and flexibility in how to approach it, _without_ losing this nice piece of information that links Monsters to their Neighborhoods.

Quite proud of that one. I think it brings the whole game together.

## Conclusion

It took about 1.5 hour to handle all this, update all relevant places, refresh images, and fix any extra issues/polishing that came up. This first "iteration" after a playtest is always the most bang for your buck. In 1% of the time it took to make the game, you make the game 10x as balanced and playable :)

It surely doesn't mean it's perfect. Way more playtests are needed, many monsters have never been tested, etcetera. But I unfortunately don't have the resources to do that kind of long, professional review of my games. At least, with these improvements, the game is playable and balanced. Whether it will be fun and amazing in every possible combination ... I don't know, but I hope my players will tell me over time.

At least I got this project _done_. Completely finished, tested, and updated, just in time for Halloween. I honestly mostly did this to get it out of my head and prove to myself that I _can_ finish slightly bigger projects like this. But the final game ended up being pretty good too, so that's nice.
