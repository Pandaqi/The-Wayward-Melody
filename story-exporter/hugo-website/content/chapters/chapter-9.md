# House of Hearts

Zara sat on the porch and played a game. A game with cards. She'd taught it herself and it was all she ever did on most days. She even had a notebook with all her scores, drawings of all the cards, and ideas for better rules. She could get lost playing her game for hours. Today was no different: the sun was setting and she didn't even notice.

The game was called _Zolo_. You started by placing a single, random card. Then, the next card---

"Are you playing that game _again_?" interrupted her mother in a loud voice. She towered over Zara and picked up the cards. Before she could protest, her cards had landed in the bin. "Go meet some other people! Go do something else!"

Zara rummaged through the garbage to get her cards back. What was wrong with her parents? They _bought_ her these beautiful cards! For her birthday! "I don't want to. Other people do stupid things. Like throwing away someone's cards."

Mother gently lifted Zara away from the garbage bin. She took out the cards herself. "I'm sorry, dear, I shouldn't have done that. But please start going outside more."

"I _was_ outside."

"You were one step away from the front door. Sitting on the floor for _eight hours_, alone with your cards!"

"So ... so what?"

Zara took the cards and walked out the door again. She dropped on the stone tiles, _two_ steps away from the front door, and started a new game of Zolo. You see, she kept getting better and better. But she still hadn't _won_ the game, she hadn't beaten it. So she kept trying.

The cards were so pretty, showing all these kings, and queens, and fancy colors. Cards never annoyed her. Cards never broke the rules. Cards and numbers didn't throw her favorite toys in the garbage! 

Most nights, she even dreamed about strategy for---

"Hey!" said a voice right next to her ear. 

She jumped in surprise, almost hitting a plump boy in his face. "Who are you---"

"Can I play?"

She vaguely recognized him as her neighbor. The boy picked up her notebook with scores, and painted half of it pink in doing so.

"No. It's for one player only."

"Oh. Can you explain the rules then?"

Zara stood up. What did she have to say to make him _go away_? She couldn't think of anything. She felt nervous with him around and suddenly couldn't find the words.

Her father saved her. Just then, he came home from work at the Rainbow Center. Zara's mother ran outside to greet him. And hug him. And kiss him. And kiss even longer. You'd think her parents hadn't seen each other for _years_. Hand in hand, they walked back inside and started cooking.

"Eww," mumbled Zara. 

The boy seemed to agree, as he frowned and walked away. Then he stopped and turned around.

"Can I, erm, borrow a card or two?"

"For what?" At least he _asked_ before touching her cards. Zara thought it was an improvement. Her game could be played with only half the cards.

"An, erm, project? For school?"

Zara shrugged. Just give him what he wants, then he goes away. So she gave him two of the ugliest cards and watched him go.

She played Zolo on the porch, alone, until mother had to drag her to bed.

___

Zara yelped in surprise when a shadow fell over her. Ugh. The neighbor boy again.

"Can I, erm, borrow another card?"

"School project?"

"Same one. Yes." He gently touched a few of the cards, painting them pink in doing so.

"_Fine._" Zara picked the next ugliest card---someone called "jack"---and gave it away.

She saw it as an extra challenge. Winning the game of Zolo without _all_ the cards ... well, if she did that, she would be a genius! She should win an award or something!

As the boy walked away, Zara's mother sat down next to her. "Why don't you play with him, dear? You'd only need to take a few more steps away from the door!"

"So what?"

Mother groaned. Father ran outside, probably thinking mother was hurt. When he saw she was fine, he sat down too and hugged, and kissed, and---Zara didn't even want to see it. 

"We're trying to help you," said father. "Believe us. We've lived longer than you, little miss. We know you'll be much happier with a few friends. Running through the woods, building sand castles, playing tag---oh, the things we did when _we_ were young."

Her parents looked at each other and grinned, in a way that just confused Zara.

"I _have_ friends. They're called cards and---"

Mother's face darkened. As if Endless Blackness had arrived on her eyes. "That's enough, Zara. We've been _very_ patient with you. But we're your parents, and we want the best for you, and so we're doing this for your own good."

"Doing what?" asked Zara in a small voice.

"You will _not_ play this game for a _month_!" said mother. Her finger pointed at Zara, nearly cutting her with her sharp nails. "And if you break that rule, then, then---"

"Just listen to us, please," said father. "We love you, little miss---"

"No you _don't_!" screamed Zara. "You're mean! You're bullies, like all the other kids! Taking away all the things I like!"

"Zara!"

She started crying. By the time she was inside, she was out of breath and sobbing. She went upstairs to her room and locked the door, writing in her notebook how much she hated her parents and they clearly did _not_ love her.

___

Zara was never going to listen, of course. She just found sneaky hiding places to keep playing Zolo. In the attic. Behind the big tree in the backyard. She even played it inside a cupboard, when it was empty because of her mother's "spring cleaning". Which seemed to be another word for "oh no I dropped all the dishes and now they're broken".

She was _so close_ to beating it. Her parents clearly didn't even care about her, or they would have discovered her hiding places long---

"Do you mind if I borrow some more cards?"

"Waaah!" Zara nearly fell out of the tree. Somehow, the neighbor boy had seen her and climbed the tree without her noticing. She could be _really_ lost in her own thoughts. "How did you find me? Are you conspiring with my parents?"

"You're, like, clearly visible from my bedroom. I'm sure your mother knows where you are."

"She does _not_. I am _very well hidden_. And you---you need even more cards?"

"Yep. School project. Big one." Whatever the boy touched turned pink. Zara hated it. A pink tree was _surely_ going to stand out and give her away.

She looked at her cards and frowned. She could lose a few more, she guessed. But that was _it_. You couldn't play Zolo when more than half your cards were missing.

"Here. Take these. They have your ugly face on them."

"Thanks!" said the boy. "Now I can, erm, finally finish that project."

He smiled, but it wasn't a _real_ smile. Zara thought he looked disappointed.

Zara wondered what the project could be. Why did he need _so many cards_?

More days passed, and Zara played Zolo and nothing else. The boy also asked to borrow a pen, some pages from her notebook, a rubber duck, and even one of her shoes. 

It was at this point that Zara realized she never asked his name. Now it felt too late. He owned more of her stuff at the moment than she did. When was he going to give it back? What _was_ he making? A tree house? His own card game? Dolls that looked like kings and queens?

See, this was why she played _alone_. She kept getting interrupted while playing Zolo. And now she was losing her things, and worrying about things, such as if the boy was going to give her away.

This had to end. Whatever his big "school project" was, it was _not_ worth it. Zara wanted to be alone again.

And so, the next time he visited, when she was hiding behind some farmer's sheep, she was ready.

"Hey! Mind if I borrow some pillows---"

"Go away! You've borrowed enough!" screamed Zara.

The boy blushed. The pink he usually spread around was stuck to his cheeks, as his face looked frozen.

"You heard me!" she kept screaming. "With your stupid face. And your ugly ears. And your whiny voice, always asking for---"

"Yeah, yeah, it's clear," said the boy. His shoulders slumped as he turned around. He weaved between the sheep, who were probably just as surprised as him, and went home. 

Zara waited a while before returning home too. Because, well, they lived right next to each other. But she had _finally_ been able to play a game of Zolo again. Alone. In peace. And ... and ...

Boring.

It wasn't the same.

She actually looked forward to hearing him ask for even weirder things. She _wanted_ to explain the rules to him. He had enough cards, by now, to play it himself too. She had even started drawing her _own cards_. She told herself she was just practicing art. But now she had to admit she had wanted to give those cards away, because mother explained that his birthday was coming up.

Marco. That was his name, mother said.

She didn't know what to think anymore. What to feel. The cards looked lifeless without the splotches of pink paint Marco left on them. Dead.

With a worried mind, she got home, didn't even notice her parents having fun on the couch, and went to bed.

___

The next morning, she woke up to a great surprise.

Mother and father stood around her bed. They smiled as they revealed---ooh, that was a nice smell. Cake! They had baked a cake! 

And they had cleaned her room. And was that---was that a gift? She rubbed the sleep from her eyes. Once she could see properly, the gift became clear too. It was a small bicycle, barely wrapped in gift paper.

"What ... why?" croaked Zara.

"We're your parents, little miss. We love you. We just want you to be happy."

Oh, how could she ever be angry with them? She hugged them both, crumpling her pajamas.

She smiled as she rolled out of bed and received a slice of cake. A bike! A ... bike. For someone who never leaves home. Wait a minute ...

Then she noticed her nightstand. The empty spot on top, next to the photograph, where she _always_ placed her cards. In exactly the same way.

The cards were gone.

This was all a trick! 

"Where are my cards?"

"Oh? Dear, we can look for them later."

"_Where are my Zolo cards!?_" demanded Zara.

Her parents gave up the act. "They're gone, dear. For good. We just want the best for you, and the best is to go outside and make friends---"

"I _hate_ you!"

Zara left the room. Her parents reached for her as if she were a pet that was about to escape. She dragged the heavy bike along, destroying a part of the staircase.

She came downstairs. She walked through the front door, onto the porch. 

And there was no neighbor boy waiting for her. Marco wouldn't come, not after how she yelled at him yesterday.

She had ... she had kind of expected it.

What now? What was the plan?

She heard her parents come down the stairs behind her. She had to move! 

And so she ran down the street, to her neighbor's house. She rang the doorbell. She knocked on the door, then resorted to _slamming_. Her parents were crossing the street too.

The door opened. Marco was still in his pajamas. His hair was a mess, and he painted the front door yellow in surprise.

"Let me in! Let me in!" said Zara, who didn't wait and let herself in.

"What is---"

"I want my cards back," said Zara. "Here, you can have this bike. Or, well, I haven't unwrapped it. But it's probably a bike, right? Anyone can see that."

A fair trade, she thought. He'd get a shiny new bicycle for free, and she could continue playing her game alone.

"What happened to your own cards?" asked Marco.

"My _mean mean mean_ parents threw them away! They must hate me!"

"The same parents that are standing behind you now?"

"Sorry to bother you, Marco," said mother sweetly. "But can we please speak with our daughter?"

"No!" Zara slammed the door shut.

"They look nice enough," mumbled Marco. "Your ma used to give me free candy whenever I came by!"

"Well, well, that's---" 

"And they gave you a bike. Is that ... are those cake crumbs on your cheek?"

"Yes. They did all that. But only to hide---"

"Your mother has started crying." Marco's front door held a glass window. Him and Zara's father had looked at each other all this time, painting the door an awkward mixture of colors.

Zara fell silent. Which meant the entire house was silent. Come to think of it, she didn't even know what Marco's parents looked like.

"Where ... where is everyone?"

"They're working. Father will be back in six months."

"You're alone?"

Marco shrugged. "Regularly."

"B-But ..."

"Of course I don't like it. But they need the money. I like being able to eat, you know."

Zara had a bad feeling about letting him borrow her stuff now. "Where are my things, Marco? Did you sell them for money?"

"What? You really don't understand, do you?" Marco blushed pink. His hand briefly touched Zara's hand, and sparks of pink shot away like fireworks. 

Marco stomped to the kitchen. He opened a drawer and took out a simple basket full of ... everything he'd borrowed. It had all been stacked carefully. Not a scratch or dust anywhere.

"You didn't need any of it?" whispered Zara. "I thought you were making the biggest house of cards ever, or something!"

"I just ... I just wanted to ..." Marco looked away. "Talk to you."

Zara's heart sank. Her parents pounded on the door. Father angrily stared through the window.

She grabbed Marco's hand. For the first time, she was the one painting everything around them a bright pink.

"I'm sorry. I ... quite liked ... talking to you."

Marco smiled. "Your parents love you, Zara. Just open that door."

"Yes. Yes," said Zara, looking over her shoulder. Her parents hugged each other and blew a kiss. She melted. Yellow-green splotches of guilt dripped onto the wooden floor. She wanted nothing more than to throw open the door and join in the hug. "Yes, I guess they do."

"Also," said Marco, unwrapping the gift. "Thanks for the free bike!"

___

Zara stood in her parents' bedroom, talking with her mother. 

"I'm sorry. Again," she said. "I love you too. And you clearly love each other too ..."

Mother smiled. "You can disagree with our rules. We can talk about it. But if you can't convince us, well, then you have to listen. It's ... you'll understand when you're older."

"I can understand ... now?"

"It's impossible for a parent to see their child make a mistake," said father, playfully messing with Zara's hair. "And _not_ do everything they can to fix it."

"I know," said Zara. She had painted all the pillows blue, from fear of what she was about to say. "I want to promise I'll go outside and ride my bike, but I, kind of, gave it away to Marco."

"That's okay. You've made your first friend."

Zara had done some thinking. With Marco's help, of course. Her parents didn't hate her. They bought those cards for her, they _knew_ she was secretly still playing, and still they let her free.

They would _never_ throw away the cards _for real_. That was a lie. Just to make their point. They must have hidden the cards somewhere.

After days of secretly searching, she figured it out. The cards were in her parents' bedroom. The one she was, usually, _not allowed to enter under any circumstance_. Especially not at night, for some reason.

This was her chance. She had already spotted one of the King cards. In the top drawer of mother's nightstand. 

She had also spotted a doll they'd taken away from her a year ago. Because she had hit her _other_ neighbor on the head when they interrupted her Zolo gama. She didn't remember.

"We just hope you've learned your lesson," said mother. "Go outside. Go play. Make friends. Fall in love."

"Eww," mumbled Zara. All that kissing just looked messy. She was _never_ going to do that, uh-uh.

She shuffled one more step to the right. She pretended that it was _very_ hot in here, so she could open the window.

"Well, dear, what would you like for dinner?" asked mother. "Your favorite---"

Blindly, with her hands behind her back, she pulled open the drawer. She could find her cards anywhere. The perfect size, and weight, which fit her fingers like a glove. With a bit more effort, she also grabbed the doll.

"Oh no, little miss, don't you---"

She _threw_ both objects out of the window. Then she climbed out herself.

"ZARA!"

Her parents' bedroom was on the ground floor. And there was even a soft shrub below it. Zara rolled into the garden unharmed, where someone was waiting for her.

Her father stepped through the window as well, but stopped halfway through. One leg dangled outside, one still inside.

Marco rang his bell. He'd gathered the cards and the doll in a bag, then moved the bag so that Zara could sit on the bike too.

Laughing and waving, they cycled away.

"I'll be home for dinner!" yelled Zara.

Her parents held each other as they watched them drive circles around the neighborhood. Wherever they went, the streets were painted pink, as well as a few unfortunate dogs that were being walked. 

Of course, it was the parents' duty to tell Zara _not_ to jump out of windows. They couldn't let this go. They would punish her by _not_ cooking her favorite meal, ha!

But they were happier than ever to see Zara like this. She had opened her eyes to friendship, and in doing so, the world outside her front door.
