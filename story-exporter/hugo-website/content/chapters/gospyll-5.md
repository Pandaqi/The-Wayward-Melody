# The Gospyll According To Jamus

There are many times when you'd use the word "good". A school grade can be good. A performance, such as a reenactment of these events at your local theatre, can be good---if you're lucky. A story can be good, and Easter festivities can be a jolly good time, especially when chocolate is involved.

But dying a terrible, painful death on a wooden cross is generally not a time to use "good".

So why did that happen on _"Good" Friday_? 

This is a good time to realize words can change meaning over time, or receive more meanings. When I called Krystina a "the worst sister ever", when I was younger, I obviously meant "you are very wonderful". And when I told her "take over the woodworking company or we never want to see you again", I obviously meant "you are free to follow your heart and we support you".

When Krystina carried her cross on Friday morning, the word "good" meant "holy". Because to all the Jurads, anything good had to be the creation of their God. If God wanted something to be a certain way, then it must be good. Nowadays, as most animals stopped thinking about Gods at all, this meaning isn't often used anymore.

The crowd told themselves this lie, over and over, as they watched my sister walk up the hill. To Golgotha, the Place of the Skull. Where so many criminals had found a terrible death after being nailed to a cross.

_They_ didn't cause this, no, no. It was God's will, surely. This had to happen. It was Good.

Krystina was too weak to carry the cross herself. Some things never changed. When we were young, I already had to open jars for my sister, or fetch objects from the highest shelf.

I could have never imagined that I had to carry a cross destined for her.

The journey to the top of the hill was a long one. Especially when you're tired. Two more criminals joined us, sentenced to crucifixion days ago, but they barely received any attention. In fact, their loneliness in their final moments made Krystina cry still.

All this time, the Amor soldiers had treated my sister like garbage. Shoving her around. Binding her body with ropes too tight. Placing a crown on her head, mocking her for being called _Queen of the Jurads_, as it was a crown made of thorns.

She had not lashed out once. She looked even more like the simple, frail platypus that I grew up with. In line with her own lessons, she didn't use violence and didn't fight. She accepted God's will at last.

And God's will was to turn her into a story big enough to replace all their lies.

Once the sun had fully risen, the cross arrived on the hill.

I will not describe the process of crucifixion in detail, dear reader. It is a barbaric practice. Which is ironic, given that Bar-Bar didn't invent it and wouldn't wish it on his worst enemies. The old sheep, set free, had already left the city at this point.

Needless to say, a living body is not meant to be tied to anything. Especially not a rigid cross. Especially not using sharp nails driven through the body in terrible places. Life is meant to _live_. Life is meant to be free, and to move.

They nailed Krystina to the cross, then used rope and several strong wolves to hoist the cross upright. There was nothing more to it. Many bystanders already left. The ten remaining disciples were among the first to go.

Krystina could have easily saved herself. She could have evaded capture in the first place. She could have used one of her miracles to flee. If you can walk over water, then you can surely walk over walls and buildings too. Turn one loaf of bread into thousands, and you have a wall of dough to keep your pursuers at bay.

It is my firm belief that she _chose_ to make this painful sacrifice. And it was that moment that made me doubt for the very first time. That made me see my sister as more than "just a sister".

Although we must admit that a range of strange curses and magic flowed through our family.

"Remember mother?" Jamus asked her. "How she claimed she laid eggs without any male platypus ever touching her?"

Krystina used her final energy for a faint smile. "And father, who claimed he could build two identical tables? Such that you couldn't tell the difference?"

"_I_ can do that too," said Jamus. "All your brothers can, because they all have identical eyes and identical beaks."

"And mother would sing such beautiful lullabies," said Krystina. "I remember them all."

"Because it wasn't just mother," said Jamus. "All your sisters can, because they all have identical voices and identical memory."

In her eyes, Jamus could almost see dream-like memories of their family. Playing in the sand as toddlers. Annoying each other, splashing each other, as they traveled using the rivers. Just normal brothers and sisters. No hint that she would turn into a savior.

"It ... it would have been nice to come home," whispered Krystina. 

Jamus had to step closer to still hear her. The Amor soldiers begrudgingly allowed it.

"We will bring you home," said Jamus, nodding.

"If God's plan succeeds, then my home will have changed," said Krystina. 

Jamus could not hold back his tears, and he held his sister one final time. "Is it really worth it? All this pain and suffering to get into the Heavenly Palace, which might not even exist?"

"That's not why it must be done."

"Tell me honestly," said Jamus at last, as he saw Krystina fading away. "Have you truly never sinned? Not a single time, in all your life, you were tempted by evil? Are you the first messenger to achieve this?"

Jamus hadn't seen it happen. A playful splash of water in his face was the most violence Krystina every gave him. A grumpy response was her worst behavior on her worst day. Jamus had seen every other brother and sister of his make mistakes and do hurtful things, as those things tend to go. 

I am sure you, dear reader, can tell many spicy stories about wrong behavior from your siblings. Your brother might be the pope now, for all I care, but _you_ know your brother stayed up late (when told not to), stole his parent's horse carriage (to sneak out with his new girlfriend), and ate all the cookies in the jar (then pretended _you_ did it).

But Jamus knew his sister as a completely kind and sinless animal. Then again, they'd been apart for so long that he didn't know what Krystina had done all those years.

"I don't believe I have sinned," said Krystina. "I have certainly made mistakes or said hurtful things. But it is God who decides what is _sin_ and what is just the _imperfection_ of living."

Jamus spoke in whispers. "You have ... never ... been with someone?"

"No. But it ..." Krystina stared at the horizon, her eyes slowly losing vision. "It would have been nice to share this life with someone else. To have children. To ... grow old on this beautiful world."

Krystina lost consciousness. She would be alive for a few more hours, during which her most devout followers circled the cross and cried for her. At some point, a dove descended from the clouds and kept her company too, albeit in silence. As opposed to the other criminals on the hill, she did not have to die alone.

When she died, the entire world turned black. Three hours for a sunless place.

She died as any other animal would die. Just a platypus after all, Jamus told himself. And they took her off the cross and buried her in a tomb.

___


At this point, you might have wondered how Krystina and the disciples survived without jobs, money, food, or even a home. The area around Jerrysalum could be a harsh place, with scorching heat, deserts, and a generally high probability on any day of being conquered by Amor soldiers. How did they do it?

A special woman called Marya Magadal gives us part of the answer.

She joined Krystina long ago, after the messiah drove "evil spirits" out of her, they say. A phrase which her means "gave her a pat on the back when she almost choked in a loaf of bread". I can know; I was there. 

Marya was a beautiful fox, as graceful and healthy now as she was then. But she was a rare kind of fox. One with a purple skin and very long whiskers. This strange color is why she believed evil spirits lived inside her.

Despite not being a disciple, Marya followed them everywhere they went. She often spoke for them or aided in tasks. Most importantly, my research indicates that they paid for most things out of _her_ resources. 

How she came to be so wealthy is unclear. In these times, women were often regarded as lesser than men. Especially the Jurad faith did not see them as reliable witnesses, reliable workers, or reliable anything. Rumors traveled around about a criminal past. Rumors about jobs Marya did, which I don't dare write about, and which she always denied.

What's undeniable, however, was her loyalty to Krystina.

She was the last of the followers to leave the cross on the hill. She was the first to keep vigil at the tomb, lighting candles and saying prayers to keep out any bad spirits. 

She was the only witness brave enough to show up at the trial and speak _in favor of_ Krystina.

Yes, dear reader, this is a part that the Gospyll of Astra left out. The things the previous Gospyll wrote were all true, I believe. But there's a difference between telling a lie, or simply not telling something at all. And if you ask me, the second one is worse. It's the line Krystina talked about, between mistakes and sin. Telling an accidental lie is simply a mistake. Leaving out information on purpose is a sin. 

I have often requested this Gospyll of Astra to be stricken from the record. Unfortunately, the record these days is maintained _by Astra herself_. She has, strangely enough, ignored my request.

At the trial between Krystina and Bar-Bar, many "witnesses" were called forth. And when I say "witness", I mean "somebody who had no connection to Krystina, but was eager to lie about all her sins".

There was a lion who claimed to have seen Krystina grow up. Let me clearly state: I had never seen that lion before in my life. They probably didn't even live anywhere _near_ our strange family. But the lion told wild stories about how my sister accidentally killed all her playmates, then revived them out of guilt.

An ox also showed up and claimed to have taught Krystina in platypus school. He showed all the math questions that Krystina got wrong, and concluded she was obviously not right in the head. I am not sure how useful this was, as half the crowd could not read or count beyond ten themselves. Leaders often forget that poverty and hunger don't create a very well-read populace. It was hard enough to find literate animals to write down these Gospylls in the first place!

Even the birds got involved. A large crow spun up a tale about Krystina being a bird-hater and a thief. She was supposedly stealing bird's eggs, juggling with them, using them for sport, and using them to feed her followers. Once she started claiming Krystina used the eggs as a musical instrument, hitting them with palm branches, she lost the crowd though. There's a limit to believable lies, and it is somewhere between an egg drum and magical sandals.

And here we get to the reason why these witnesses weren't in Astra's Gospyll. _She_ stole those eggs. For no good reason! All she would do was paint them in pretty colors, until the baby birds broke out. Astra was a thief and a sinner, but made herself look like a victim.

I was hidden in the crowd too. It didn't matter how often I yelled that they were lying. To ignore these eye witnesses and not take their story as Gospyll. I was overruled by the other yelling in the crowd. It's the only thing on which I agree with the Gospyll of Astra: it was strategic crowd manipulation that led to my sister's end. 

For my sister had, and I truly believe this now, never sinned once in her entire life.

And then Marya Magadal entered the balcony, as the final witness.

"Not a day goes by that I haven't seen Krystina or spoken to her," she said. "I have heard all her lessons, even the small ones, even those in privacy. I have seen all her actions, even the minor ones, even when nobody else was looking. If Krystina is punished here today, then I assume the entire world is punished. If Krystina is guilty of any crime, then everyone else is guilty of that crime ten times over. I will not stand for this. I will never leave her side."

It was no surprise, then, that Marya was the first to visit Krystina's tomb every day. And the last to leave, as the moon already hung in the sky, and she sometimes imagined seeing Krystina among the stars.

She imagined the wrong thing, dear reader.

On the third day, Sunday, she arrived at the tomb and froze in horror. The tomb was _open_. The large stone rolled before the entrance, so heavy they needed six wolves to move it, was gone.

The sun was yet to rise. The darkness inside the tomb mirrored the darkness outside. In total silence, Marya looked over her shoulders and hesitated before the entrance. 

"Please forgive me," she mumbled, and dared step inside.

The tomb was empty, not a sign of a corpse.

A disappearance is not that unusual. Things disappear all the time. Things you desperately needed, such as your wooden keys or your favorite sandals, have a habit of disappearing exactly then. A magician makes it _seem_ as if things disappear. And an elephant could have easily passed by and accidentally destroyed the stone before the tomb. Then some squirrels in ninja outfits might have entered, found Krystina, and simply carried her away in the dead of night. Or maybe the stone was never a stone, but just a skilled chameleon, and they were tired of sealing the a cave and walked away.

All very plausible explanations, as you will surely agree.

Disappearance is not the impressive part. It was what happened afterwards that made me write this Gospyll.

___

The expression "to walk on egg shells" means that you need to be very, very careful. Marya had to be careful when informing other of the miracle that had happened. The disciples had to be careful about meeting each other and sharing this news. If not, they might all be arrested anyway and the cycle would repeat.

But it also applies literally in this case. Marya had walked over literal egg shells, into a cave she thought was empty. When she reached the end, however, she heard a suspicious sound behind her and instantly turned around.

There, in the opening, outlined by sunlight, stood none other than Krystina. Alive and well. Or, at least, alive.

The same kind face. The same bright eyes. The same body of the exact same size and color. The same angelic, sweet voice as she said: "Good morning, Marya."

Marya stuttered and stumbled. Her body sought the cave wall for support, as she forgot to blink.

"Is it ... it is really you?"

"Well, yes, who else would it be?"

"I can't believe ... you were ... you are _dead_!"

Krystina danced around in the opening, then beckoned Marya to step into the light. "Do I look dead to you?"

"This is ... you have conquered death!"

"No. I have conquered life. And I have paid for all of your sins, so we can start again. Properly this time. Go forth and tell everyone."

A speechless Marya remained rooted to the spot, clinging to Krystina. "Tell them yourself! With your return, you can continue your mission!"

"No. I have completed my mission."

Marya ran away. And the first one she told was me. As she said it, I didn't believe her. As we walked to the tomb, I didn't believe her.

When I saw Krystina again with my own eyes, I could no longer deny it.

My sister had performed her final miracle: a resurrection. Even Ardex, God of Death, had not managed to revive himself.

No normal animal could do this. Not even the best magician in the world, the most powerful demigod of them all, could perform a resurrection.

My sister was no normal animal. She was the messenger of God.

In the hours that followed, everyone held their breath. The news was bound to reach governor Pillar at some point, as well as the High Priest, or just the nearest Amor soldiers.

Would they arrest her again? Would the cycle repeat? Or would things be different now?

The first signs were promising. The first Amor soldiers at the scene did not look angry and did not bring out the handcuffs. They bowed to Krystina when they saw the resurrection with their own eyes. They laid down their weapons for her, and asked her forgiveness a thousand times over. Forgiveness which Krystina gave easily, as always.

She met up with her disciples. They shared a final day or two, in which they asked many testing questions to confirm that it was really her. 

"What useless present did you give me, an ape, for my twentieth birthday?" asked Bartholo.

"A nest for holding eggs," said Krystina with a smile.

Wamez the Wolf wanted to verify her identity too. "When we were chased by lions, because I taunted them by calling them _just large cats_, what did I ask you?"

"Whether there would be any lions in the Heavenly Palace," responded Krystina.

Krystina was sad that Astra and Judash were not there. Her disciples were sad that she had to leave again, and they didn't understand _why_.

She gave the explanation that she had to claim her place in heaven. That, soon, they would see her ascend and share her place with God.

I was the final animal to speak with her. And when I did, she hinted at the actual truth.

"The longer I stay around," she said, "the more you will all see me as a normal platypus again. The more my resurrection will fade from memory and seem ... normal."

"I can't believe that," I said. "You are the only animal to ever come back to life. This will be remembered forever."

"Yes, let them remember _this_ forever, as the end of my story. And you, Jamus, will spread this message." Krystina kissed her brother on his forehead. "You were sent to Jerrysalum to uphold Jurad rules---Momo's Law. They call you Jamus the Just, because of your knowledge of the rules, and never breaking one. But it is time for new rules. Better rules. So go forth and start a new religion in my name."

"I don't think that's how religions are start---"

"I think it will work out just fine."

The next day, as a large crowd watched, she ascended to the heavens. Or, at least, that was her final trick. In reality, a dove carried her into the clouds, until nobody saw her anymore. Once she had left behind her friends and family, she traveled to some faraway corner, took up math classes, and started a candy shop.

To all who lived in Jerrysalum, Krystina was no doubt the messiah. They were inspired, awed, star-struck, and eager to follow in her footsteps. She had shown that, after all this time, and so many sins, God still believed in them and would give life on Somnia a second chance.

Many were passionate about grabbing that second chance with all four paws.

Judash is said to have used the money to complete Krystina's original mission. Feeding the hungry and taking care of the poor, without ever saying his name or asking credit for it. In fact, if anyone asked, he would say Krystina's name. And he'd say _daughter of God, the promised Messiah_ in the same breath. That's why only I know that it was really Judash, and nobody else!

Astra, if her Gospyll is to be believed, had banished herself to a faraway island in guilt.

The remaining ten disciples obeyed Krystina's final command. To travel the world and spread the miracle of her resurrection. Her victory against death, and the celebration of new life.

As Passover ended, they all left Jerrysalum going in different directions, with a head full of inspiring stories.

From this day forward, many minds would be changed. Like my mind was changed, and that of Judash, and that of the High Priests and Amor Empire. Many who thought about taking up their weapons, heard Krystina's story and sought peaceful solutions instead. Many who saw Krystina as a heretic now saw that she had simply improved the old Jurad rules.

It is said that Marya Magadal visited the Amor Emperor at the time, Tiberios. She brought with her a very normal, ordinary white egg. She greeted him with "Krystina is risen!", and Tiberios replied: "A platypus rising from the dead is as likely as that egg turning red." Then the egg miraculously turned red in her hand. And Tiberios looked more kindly at Krystina's followers ever since.

It always takes a big, almost unbelievable story to replace a big lie and change people's minds.
