# Home Is Where The

The Shape War had been going on for a year now. And King Triangle had mostly _won_. The Rebelshapes had annoyed him, sure. They had kept secrets, hid scared shapes, and fought against him the whole way. But all of Shapesong---except one flowery village---was terrified and had rebuilt everything to look like a Triangle. Triangle eyes. Triangle wheels on their bicycles. Triangle paper to write on with a Triangle pen, as their teacher wrote their lessons on a Triangle blackboard. Surprisingly, most of their lessons were just about how cool Triangles were and nothing else.

And all this time, animals were asking: _Why!?_

Why did he want everything to be a Triangle? Why did he hate everything else? Didn't he see how unhappy this made everyone? Didn't he see how ugly Shapesong had become? Everything looked _the same_. Every town you visited looked _the same_.

Two Bearchitects had tried to help. When they realized you could make all other Shapes out of lots of Triangles, they were able to make some pretty buildings again. Some trees that actually looked like, you know, a tree. But it was too little too late.

And now they had made King Triangle angry. He was coming for Ballhouse Beaucoup. He couldn't even allow a _single_ magical building to _not_ look like a Triangle!

And so all the rebels kept asking each other: _Why?!_

"Come out now!" yelled King Triangle. "You can't hide in there forever!"

"Yes we can!"

"No you can't!"

"Yes we can!"

This continued for a while, until King Triangle was distracted. His eyes caught a certain someone. A beautiful Heart-shaped animal, red and powerful. She walked past, smiling at him. 

The King could feel his own heart beating faster than ever before. He opened his mouth to say something, but no words came to him. No words that were good enough to speak to this beautiful woman.

You might have wondered, dear reader, why King Triangle didn't have a Queen by his side. He has a son, Prince Circlesnake, so who is his mother? Well, you will find out soon enough where his mother went. The truth was, that King Triangle was without a Queen at the moment ... and he wanted to change that.

"My lovely lady," he said, his voice higher and more gentle than ever. "What brings you here? This battlefield is no place for---"

"But I live here," she said. She smiled still, inviting the King to come closer with her eyes.

"In the Ballhouse?"

"No, no. Just a small shed in town."

"A small shed?"

"Triangle-shaped, of course."

"That's no place for a lovely lady like you!" said King Triangle. He had suddenly forgotten all about the Rebels. Who were glad to see it, because they could continue preparing their big plan for tomorrow.

"I agree," she said, smiling even more.

"Why don't you and I take a walk?" asked King Triangle. He offered his hand. This would be a fine Queen, he told himself. A kind Heart, but there was an edge to her. Something dangerous, something powerful. Now that he had shaped Shapesong to be like himself---that is, Triangles everywhere---it was time to have a Queen next to him again.

"I would love to," said the woman. "But now is not a good time. How about tomorrow?"

King Triangle hid his disappointment. "Fine. Fine, fine."

___

King Triangle hadn't slept. He had dreamed of his new Queen all night. He would walk with her today, impress her with his jokes and deep knowledge of the history of the Triangle. She would fall in love with him. Happily ever after.

The next day, the red Heart waited for him. He had struggled to see what kind of animal she was before. Now, walking side by side, it was obvious: she was a lovebird. 

"Tell me about yourself," said King Triangle.

"My name is Amoralia," said the Heart-shaped lovebird. "There isn't much more to say. I lived a simple life. No, tell me about _you_, oh great king!"

King Triangle needed no encouragement. "Obviously, I am the greatest King that ever lived. Look around you. Everyone wants to be a Triangle, like me!"

"Your son isn't a Triangle, right?"

The King's face grew darker. "No. And I will punish him for it."

"Excuse me, sire, but what happened to his mother?"

They turned a corner, heading into the jungle. It was early morning. The sun rose and made the Ballhouse glimmer. Even from deep in the jungle, you could always see the magical home. It was like a guiding star, but, well, shaped like a big Circle.

For a moment, Amoralia thought the King wouldn't respond.

"Sit down with me, please," he whispered. They sat down on a nearby mushroom. After the King made sure there were no suspicious Shaeprints on it.

"I used to have a nice neighbor," said the king. "A good friend. We went to school together. We practiced swordfighting together. The first time I had my heart broken---because I fell in love with someone who did not love me---he was there for me."

"Must be nice," she said sweetly, stroking his cheek. "To have such a good friend."

"It was! He was a Heart-shaped lion. Strong. Nice to look at. Kind. Some people looked at us and thought _he_ was going to be king!"

Amoralia laughed. Loudly. An echo through the entire jungle. 

It was the sign she had agreed on with the Rebels.

"We traveled through the desert together," said the King. "He was the kind of shape who'd stand in the sun all day, just to keep you in the shade. A big Heart, he was. But even he let me down."

"How so?" asked Amoralia. She shuffled on the mushroom, getting closer to him.

"What happened to the Prince's mother? The previous Queen?" King Triangle bared his teeth, his face an angry mess. "She ran away with that friend of mine. Liked him better, I suppose."

Amoralia gasped, covering her mouth with her hands.

"You see," said the King. Loudly. An echo through the entire jungle. "Squares have let me down. Circles have disappointed me. Stars have betrayed me, and Hearts have stolen my Queen. _All shapes have hurt me in the past. All except the mighty Triangle!_"

That explained a lot, thought Amoralia.

That explained everything.

Her work here was almost done. 

"I'm so sad for you, my king," she said. "You were right to force everyone to become a Triangle. Yes, that is a _very_ normal response. Very smart and not at all mean and stupid. Absolutely what you should do when a few shapes let you down in the past. No doubt about it."

"Thank you," said the King. "Finally someone who understands me."

Amoralia turned away and rolled her eyes. She obviously didn't believe a word of what she just said. But any moment now.

"Sir!" yelled his guards. "King! We have a problem!"

___

Prince Circlesnake gave it one final try. He gave the Rebels one more chance. Tessella had told him to ask some cat called Penny. She started the whole thing when she couldn't find any other Pentagons, apparently. She was still one of the most important Rebelshapes.

He approached Ballhouse Beaucoup from the back. It was hard to get over the broken furniture around it, at least without being noticed. He didn't want to be squashed by a falling piano, or kicked away by the living home.

But as he rolled towards it, nothing happened. In fact, the door opened. As if ... as if the home invited him in. Was it a trap? Or did the home understand he could be trusted?

The Prince decided not to wait. He rolled even faster and soon entered the Ballhouse.

The room was mostly empty. The Rebelshapes had already moved to the next room. From the sound of it, they were busy planning something big.

In this room, however, sat a single cat. A Pentagon cat. The Prince couldn't believe his luck.

"Penny?" he asked, his voice small.

"Yes?" said the cat. "You are ... you are the Prince! What are you doing here? How did you get---"

"I want to join," said the Prince. He bowed before Penny. Which, if you're a Circlesnake, just meant flattening himself against the floor. "I already asked Tessella, and Piqafox, but they turned me away, saying you hated me. I was never a spy. I actually confused King Triangle and made sure you were never captured."

"_Unbelievable!_" yelled Penny. Her hairs stood up straight, and she walked to the other room.

"Please! You have to believe me! Please!" pleaded the Prince.

Penny was gone for a few seconds. Then she returned while _dragging_ Tessella and Piqafox behind her.

"Explain yourself!"

"I ... I just did," said the Prince.

"Not you! These two!" Penny stared them down. "You turned away the Prince? Told him everyone hated him?"

"You know it's true," said Tessella. She was a Hexagon shape today, and rubbed her sides where Penny had pushed her.

"We can't trust a spy," said Piqafox.

"Is that why we started the Rebels? Is it?" asked Penny. "I believe I had _one rule_! One rule! It was that _every_ shape was welcome!"

Penny's voice rose higher and higher. The rebels in the other room stopped talking for a bit.

"You should be _ashamed_ of yourself!" said Penny. "That kind of thinking is _exactly_ like King Triangle! Just because some shape hurt you in the past, you hate them forever!"

"Don't talk to me like that---"

Tessella bumped Piqafox. She stopped talking.

"We're sorry," said Tessella. "You're right."

She gave the Prince a nice, long hug. 

Then she searched her Toolbox, and came back with a cake. Again.

"A ... welcome cake? For our new rebel, the Prince!"

Prince Circlesnake laughed. He accepted the cake, but didn't eat any of it. "I just hope you can see me for what I really---"

Laughter. It echoed through the rooms. It bounced off the walls, harshly into their ears.

The sign! Amoralia had made the sign!

They looked outside. Their plan had worked. The King was distracted, as were his guards.

"Move out! Move out!" yelled Penny.

The Ballhouse opened _all_ its doors, windows, and even some hidden pathways that Beau never knew about. This home was truly a magical, living being. Older than they knew. Full of secrets.

The Headmeaster of Bearchitect Academy had explained that the home came from another planet. One where living homes were standard. One where you were weird if you _didn't_ have a living home. But, also, one where living homes were forbidden too, and people fought to have one.

The Rebels thought about all this as they escaped the Ballhouse. As they shut off all the lights. As they left all the rooms for good, sat in the chairs one final time.

Hundreds of Rebelshapes flowed out of Ballhouse Beaucoup, like a river of all shapes and sizes. Nobody had seen them leave. Those who did, pretended they didn't see. 

Some of the rebels wore Triangle uniforms. That's what they made from all the silk the weasels had stolen. Uniforms to _pretend_ they were Triangle guards. And so, anybody who saw them leave might have thought they were being arrested. That the King had won, and there was no reason to tell anyone.

By the time King Triangle returned from the jungle, Ballhouse Beaucoup was empty. Silent. Dark. The rebellion had flown away, to his castle in the capital. Amoralia would join them there.

"Blasted rebels! Filthy shapes!" King Triangle yelled every two-word combination he could think of. Big fishes. Red balloons. Poisonous bananas. Crumbled cookies.

None of it was nice. All of it was mean. Except perhaps the balloon thing. 

And when he was finally done, he raced back home immediately.

"Time to show those Rebels who is more powerful! Time to end them once and for all!"
