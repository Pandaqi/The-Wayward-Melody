## Any Questions?

{{% section-foldable header="What is an escape room?" %}}

An **"escape room"** is a collaborative puzzle experience for a any group of people. It differs from just "a bunch of puzzles" by having a story, unique material, surprising connections between the puzzles, and (sometimes literal) out-of-the-box thinking. 

The name comes from physical locations in which you literally get locked in a room and need to find the key. That's where the hype started.

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="Why should I play one?" %}}

A **printable escape kit** is far cheaper, faster and more accessible than a paid location or almost any other activity. Buy it once, play it whenever, with whomever, however many times. _You_ decide if you want to put a timer on it (to see if you escape "in time") or not.

Nothing beats **sharing an experience with others**. Nothing beats our human desire to solve problems, that _Eureka!_ moment when you suddenly see what's been staring you in the face all the time. Young or old, male or female, everyone can enjoy puzzling and playing their part in a fun mystery.

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="What do I need?" %}}

The precise properties and requirements of each escape room are listed on the product page. In general, though, we aim for the following types of experiences.

* **Minimal setup**: as few pages as possible, no other requirements (such as glue or a phone), printable in both grayscale and color.
* **Everyone can play**: the one who prints/prepares the game does _not_ see the answers and can play along. There are always multiple puzzles to do simultaneously.
* **A full experience**: escape rooms take 1 to 2 hours. There's a clear theme, art design, story, and more than enough puzzling.
* **Family-friendly**: as little text as possible (to make them language-independent), friendly themes (instead of solving bloody murders all the time), colorful and vibrant on the table.

In practice, you only need to prepare the material and read/explain the introduction page. After a few minutes, even young kids understand what to do and are off to a hot start.

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="What do I get?" %}}

The entire escape room is packed in a **single PDF file**. Inside it, you'll usually find,

* **Introduction Sheet**: this explains the puzzle setup, the start of the story, and anything else to know beforehand.
* **Answer Sheet**: tips, hints and answers to the puzzles in case you're stuck. This is usually _obfuscated_ to prevent accidentally reading them.
* **Puzzle Material**: these can be standalone puzzles, or general material of which _you_ have to figure out where to use it.
* **Bonus Material**: sometimes, there are fun bonuses like a "reward" for escaping, or just some fun thematic elements to place around the table.
