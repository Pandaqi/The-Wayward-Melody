# The Gospyll According To A Chicken

On an island far away, a bunch of chickens planned to overthrow their leader.

Their leader had not locked them up. They weren't shut into a tiny, suffocating chicken coop, like many of their unfortunate brethren. Their chicken coops were large structures to use voluntarily, to shelter against storms or get some much needed privacy. Otherwise, the chickens had free reign of the island. They could even leave, if chickens were better swimmers.

Their leader had not been unkind to them. She was taught by none other than the mythical Krystina. And so she was sweet, and caring, and would not hurt a fly. Unless she accidentally hopped into one, of course.

Their leader had not even called herself their leader. She called it a "collaboration" between her and hundreds of chickens.

Yeah, sure, uh-huh. A collaboration where _she_ gets all the eggs, and the money, and the magic. And what did the chickens get? _Nothing._

And so at midnight, hidden in the largest chicken coop, they planned their chicken coup.

"I can't believe a disciple of Krystina would be so evil!" yelled one chicken.

"She isn't evil," responded another chicken, the one writing this Gospyll. I would like to remain anonymous though, so I will refer to myself as El Chicko. "As Krystina would say, she is _misguided_."

"What she is doing to our eggs _is_ evil!" responded the whole den of hens.

"Not all of it," said El Chicko. "Remember when she used an egg to create the beautiful stone statues?"

"What good are stone statues?" responded El Henno, my husband. "Now I have to gaze upon an ugly head every morning when I wake up!"

"Okay, but, our leader also used the eggs to build us these lovely coops," said El Chicko.

"Stop calling her our leader," yelled El Henno.

"Stop talking so loud!" said El Chicko. She heard noises outside the coop. A dull thud, then silence, then a dull thud again. With every _thud_, the coop rattled, and several eggs rolled from one wall to the other.

If you have never been a chicken, dear reader, and I hope you haven't, this might sound as if a drowsy giant approaches your home. The chickens, however, recognized it immediately as something more fearsome: their hopping leader.

Thud.

Silence.

Thud, close enough to rattle the doors and open one of them.

Silence.

The chickens held their breath. It would surely look suspicious to see all of them cooped up in their coop planning a coup. Especially because of the whitepapyrus containing their details schematics and plans for how exactly to conquer the island.

Thud; further away.

Silence.

Thud; even further.

They released their held breaths and placed the eggs back where they belonged.

"That was close," whispered El Chicko. 

"Too close," said El Henno, shivering from head to bend toe.

"We must speak in whispers from now---"

The doors to the coop flew open. A much larger animal than they lowered their head until it got stuck in the opening.

Hundreds of eyes stared at a much larger set of only two eyes.

"Well hello there," said the mysterious figure. He was covered in darkness, but his hands and his eyes pointed to an ape-like creature. "I hope I am not interrupting, like, an important chicken party."

Nobody knew how to respond to that. El Chicko and El Henno worked together to try and hide their schematics.

"Anyway," said the figure, "I am looking for Astra Buni. I have traveled the world and spoken to many creatures. Until finally one of them told me that this island was called _Astra Island_."

"See!" whispered El Henno. "Our leader is so vain she names the island after herself!"

"I was told there was _nobody_ on the island until she arrived," said the figure. "What else should she have done? Call it Empty Island?"

"Anyway, we mean, erm," stuttered El Chicko. "No, she is not here. Never heard of that bunny."

The creature grinned. "You lie! Ha! I never told you she was a female bunny!"

El Chicko looked for a way to salvage the situation. "Salvage" is a word which here means "get the monkey off the island and not give them away to Astra Buni".

Then she had an idea. An incredibly smart idea, if I may say so myself. Chickens are much smarter than they get credit for. Other animals have all these weird names for their babies. A baby sheep is called a _lamb_, for some reason. Hard to remember. A baby dog is called a _puppy_, which is, like, an entirely different word. But a baby chicken is called a _chick_, and they're cute and yellow to boot. That's smart naming, if I may say so myself.

Anyway, El Chicko approached the monkey and said: "We didn't reveal we know Astra ... because we are _scared_ of her. Oh yes. So scared."

"Scared? She's the nicest bunny ever!"

"How long has it been since you met her?"

"I have never met her. But I heard of her reputation. All over the world, people talk about the _Goddess of Fertility_ that lives on this island. A Goddess of new life, and new beginnings, and, erm, a lot of eggs."

"Well, well, she has _changed_. She holds us hostage! And she steals our eggs! Yes, you must help us now, while you still can. Help us get rid of evil Astra!"

The monkey frowned. Then he laughed and jumped a hole in the sky.

"So she _does_ live here! I have found her! Finally! You know what this calls for?"

"Erm, no?"

"A _celebration_!" The monkey did a little dance. "From now on, this day shall be a holiday. And I, the Great Timothy, will call it _Astra Is Found_."

"Isn't it already a holiday?" asked El Chicko. "It's almost Resurrection Day, isn't it?"

"Oh. Yes. I forgot. I've been traveling for so long."

With the monkey dancing around their coop, there was no use holding a secret meeting anymore. The chickens slowly walked out and joined him. In the distance, they could spot Astra Buni's silhouette. Always painting, always painting. The moonlight faintly illuminated her next masterpiece, drawn on the egg of a Dodo. She was whistling, too invested in her work to see what was happening.

El Chicko asked: "So? Are you helping us or not?"

"If you speak the truth," said the monkey, "then I will surely help you escape. That is my duty as a good Krystian. But I need to, like, talk to Astra first."

The monkey probably hoped for a calm, easy conversation. But the chickens knew this was never possible. Not with Astra and her egg decorations. Because as soon as Astra finished her painting, several parts of the island exploded, gravity turned upside-down, and some of the stone statues came to life---and they were angry.

___

A stone statue, of a very long head with very big lips, almost flattened Timothy. He dodged aside at the last second, only to forget gravity had reversed and float higher and higher. 

The chickens quickly grabbed each other's wings and created a chain of feathers, until El Chicko could grab Timothy before he was forever lost in the clouds. Or, until gravity was normal again and he'd come crashing down. Which, of course, not many animals survive.

"Get out!" screamed Astra. "Get out, intruder!"

"But ..." Timothy struggled to find the words. Her voice sounded deranged, like a mad witch, and her anger boiling over.

"See what we mean?" said El Chicko with satisfaction.

The satisfaction soon disappeared when a stone statue hopped onto her.

El Henno let go of the chain of feathers. He clucked in a panic and dragged El Chicko away just in time. Now they were both floating higher and higher, holding hands---or, well, holding _wings_.

"Help us!" yelled Timothy, upside-down and walking on his hands. Another stone statue chased him, and he was forced to walk straight into an explosion of grass and dirt. "Stop the magic, Astra!"

"I told you to _get out_!"

Timothy blinked, frozen on the spot. This was not the Astra he remembered. This was not the Astra Buni for whom he traveled half the world. What had happened to her?

It's a well-known fact that chickens can't fly as well as other birds. But we can fly _a little bit_. Fifty chickens came together to fly _a little bit_ and drag their friends---El Chicko and El Henno--back to safety. The other chickens surrounded Timothy.

"Now you've seen for yourself. Our leader is insane! Help us get rid of her, before she uses even darker magic!"

I am sad to say that several chickens didn't survive the attack. One stone statue fell over at an unfortunate time, while another chicken had tried to swim to safety.

Timothy's face turned grim. "I have seen, yes. This madness must stop. Give me ten of your best eggs!"

It must be noted that Timothy had no magic himself. He was not a demigod, a child of the old gods. He was the child of a very regular mother and father monkey who still celebrated his invented holidays back at home.

But you might also remember, from his Gospyll, that he understood the magic of eggpainting.

After some hesitation, El Chicko delivered their best eggs to Timothy. There was some discussion about what "best" meant, but it usually meant biggest and unbroken.

He didn't have the luxury of a calm painting session. Timothy had to clutch the eggs and hope none of them broke. He ran for his life, dodging explosions, grabbing branches when gravity turned upside-down again, and slowly got closer to Astra.

"Why are you doing this!?" he yelled at her. He had to yell, dear reader, to surpass the grating noise of stone statues sliding around as if on skates.

Astra ran farther away. She seemed not to care about the destruction she brought. El Chicko grew even angrier, noting that the other side of the island---Astra's home---_never_ had any surprise explosions or other magical mishaps.

Timothy followed her there.

"What happened to your heart, Astra?" cried Timothy in disbelief. "Listen to me! Explain yourself, or ... or ..."

He didn't dare say it. But from what he'd seen, it seemed dangerous to let Astra do whatever she wanted here. He'd have to help the chickens escape, at least. He might even have to arrest Astra, causing her life to end in the same sad way as all the other apostles.

They arrived at Astra's home. A beautiful maze of wooden buildings, stone statues, and garlands of eggs, slithering through the hills, underground, and in the sky, hanging from tall trees and solid clouds. And every single bit, every last inch, was painted in mesmerizing colors and patterns.

Astra duck into a slanted wooden building.

Timothy sighed and started painting an egg. The exact pattern he'd used years ago, on accident, to cause an explosion. As he finished the painting and threw the egg, he said.

"No, no, you can't hide from me---"

And Astra returned with her hands full of paintbrushes.

"Oh thank you, stranger, for bringing more eggs!" said Astra. Her voice was sweet again, and she quickly took a few of the eggs he brought.

But Timothy couldn't stop his movement anymore. He had already thrown the magical egg, which was starting to send out fireworks, straight at Astra's home.

Oh no. Oh no.

Astra's eyes went wide open. Within a heartbeat, maybe even less, she drew a pattern on one of the eggs. And she threw it on the ground around her.

A _shield_, of light and feathers, briefly appeared around her. The exploding egg sunk into it, as if dipping a marshmallow in chocolate, then bounced out an continued the explosion safely between the clouds. Although several birds migrating there did not like the decision as much.

Because of the crude pattern, hastily drawn, the shield didn't hold for long.

And because of the time they had wasted, El Chicko and the other chickens had run to Astra's home too. The stomping stone statues had destroyed everything else and were chasing them now. Timothy felt himself grow lighter already, as the gravity started to reverse here too.

Astra turned around, and around, taking it all in. And then she sunk to the floor.

"Astra, you are under arrest for---"

"I know, I know, I'm a failure, I'm a failure," she mumbled. Her long ears bent to cover her eyes, which were filled with tears.

"Surrender yourself and stop your magic now, Astra, before more animals are kill---"

"I told you to _GET OUT_!" she screamed.

The hundred chickens circling her fell silent. El Chicko noticed, however, that she wasn't looking at them, nor at Timothy.

She looked _past_ him, straight at the first of the stone statues. As the statue had come alive, so had the face. It had a wicked grin, eyes that blinked, and those big lips moved as if about to say something.

But after Astra's scream, the faces fell still again.

The statues returned to their original shape and stopped moving.

"I am sorry about that," said Astra more calmly. "Evil spirits are attracted to my magic. They like to inhabit those statues from time to time, and it takes some stern lecturing to get them out."

She stood up and walked away. "Doesn't change that I'm a failure. Don't waste your breath on me, apostle."

"You're ... you're still learning to, like, control your magic, that is all," tried Timothy. 

"That is all? Have you not read the Gospylls? Have you not studied scripture?" asked Astra, her voice cracking. "I _failed_ Krystina. I denied her three times. There is no place for me in heaven, and if so, if so ..."

Her voice became small, and she dropped to the floor again. It was a particularly sad sight against the colorful, cheerful backdrop of her painted home. "... then what am I even to do with my life?"

"Don't listen to her!" yelled El Chicko. "She's manipulating you! She is evil!"

Other chickens, floating in the air, mostly upside-down, said: "Don't forget to undo reverse gravity!"

"Grab her, Timothy," said El Chicko. "You have the power to bring her to justice! For stealing our eggs! And giving us nightmares about stone!"

But Timothy _had_ read the Gospylls, and the scripture, and preached it to many. He knelt down besides Astra and took her paws. He removed her ears from her eyes, so that they could see each other clearly.

"What do you do? You make it right! That's what Krystina would have said. Few animals are strong enough to resist the temptation to sin. But Krystina died for our sins. You can be forgiven---you _are_ forgiven---as long as you lead a life away from sin, from now on."

Astra's glassy eyes could hardly focus. She trembled from teeth to fluffy tail. "I ... can be forgiven?"

"I am absolutely sure," said Timothy, "that you can earn back your place in the Heavenly Palace. But you need to get off the island and start living a life of faith again. Or, more simply, you need to start _living a life_ again."

___

Now, dear reader, you might think this sounds all wonderful and nice and it makes you warmful inside. But she was still the tyrant of Astra Island! She still needed our eggs to do her magic! And so El Chicko, and her band of chicks, were not entirely pleased with how events unfolded.

Timothy took Astra to Doveland. The Grapi tribes used to live there long ago, when the Amori Empire still exist. Then the animals there called themselves Germani, before the doves outnumbered them again and renamed it. I tell you this to help you understand the timeline. There was talk of a Second Conflict brewing, but it had not yet arrived, and would not for some years. But the events that would _cause_ this new, massive war around the world---they _were_ happening now.

Timothy and Astra were old beings, I am sad to say. The Magic of Longlife was still in effect, which is the only reason they were still around. It also meant they had seen history repeat itself over and over.

And so they arrived in Doveland to _try_ and prevent history from repeating itself. Because Astra needed eggs, in case she had to magic her way out of a situation, El Chicko and a dozen other chickens were brought along too.

"The animals here are losing their ways," said Timothy. "They claim to be Krystian, but they ignore Krystina's teachings. They fight. They are greedy. They hunger for war, and I fear they're about to get it."

"What do you want me to do?" responded Astra. "I have not found the egg pattern yet for _mind control_."

"Hmpf. I find that hard to believe," said El Chicko, wings crossed.

Timothy shook his head and smiled. "It doesn't need to be so big. You can do good in small ways. It might even be the only way to do good, because the bigger your act, the more likely there are, like, bad consequences!"

El Chicko looked around. They had arrived at a very regular, normal street in Doveland. Which meant that the animals were poor, the houses were shoddily built, garbage lay on the street, and every second you could hear someone cough their lungs out.

"Right now," said Timothy, "there is a boy inside a home, _angry_ about being poor and hungry. If we don't interfere, they might become a criminal and steal something tonight."

"Or take the girl inside that home there," said Timothy. "She actually grew up with Krystina's story in Le Bjib, but drew the wrong conclusion. That good people are punished and bad people get money and an empire. She is planning to become a powerful general and start a war when she gets older."

"What ... why are you telling me this?" asked Astra.

"Krystina did not believe in _punishing the bad_," said Timothy. "That never works. It just creates a downward spiral of violence and punishments. No, she believed in _rewarding the good_ instead."

Slowly an idea dawned on Astra. She hopped onto the roofs of many homes, and listened at their doors and windows, and found Timothy was right. Many of them had lost their way, praying for selfish reasons, or not praying to Krystina at all. They were eating meat during Lent. Children were already whispering of revenge against injustices, as their stomachs rumbled.

But there were also children living faithfully. There were children praying not for themselves, but for their sick mother, or their sad friend. She saw families spending time together in joy and laughter. 

Needless to say, the next morning those children woke up to a present. A magical egg that gave them a new toy, or food, or beautiful drawings on their walls. Large families received multiple eggs. One that healed broken bones, for that sick mother, and one egg that was all warm and cuddly, to lift the moods of that sad friend. Astra did what she could with the magic she had discovered thus far.

The children she considered "bad" did _not_ get such a thing.

Timothy smiled as she returned. "Well well, we could make a holiday out of this!"

There were some issues to iron out, of course. Astra had done this on the wrong night, so Timothy invented that the holiday would _move_ every year: on the first Sunday after the Full Moon after March 21.

The feast had now changed so much from Passover that they needed a new name. Timothy named it Easter, in honor of Astra and how many animals pronounced it wrong.

Many witnesses also reported seeing a large crowd of chicks walk around that night. Either that, or the streets had turned yellow. That's how chicks, and eggs of course, became even more firmly associated with the holiday.

Within a few years, Astra was visiting hundreds of towns during Easter. Rewarding the children who had been _good_. Families were expecting it now. Faithfulness returned, albeit slowly. Because Astra had such little time, however, she stopped dropping the eggs neatly on their table. More and more, children had to search for the eggs themselves upon waking up. They could have been hastily deposited over the garden wall. They could be stuck in their chimney. They could be precariously balanced on the top of a door, such that if someone were to open that door with force, they'd get hit with an egg on the head.

To make this possible, of course, Astra needed even more eggs than before.

El Chicko had been hesitant at first. She had helped Astra that first night, but with reluctance.

Upon seeing the results, though, she became committed. Within a few years, Astra trusted El Chicko enough to give her important jobs, such as counting the eggs and growing the garden of Painter's Plants.

"If this is what you use our eggs for," El Chicko had said, "then I can stand behind that."

"If this is our purpose," El Henno had said, "then we believe heaven has a place for us."

And for the first time in centuries, Astra left her island and believed so too.

"Tok tok tok tooook tok," said a newborn chick, "tok tooook tok."

Well said, well said.
