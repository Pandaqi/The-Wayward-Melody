## The Rat Who Returned

Once upon a time, in a great forest, there lived a little rat named Remy. He was clever and quick, but he had one big problem—he never planned ahead. Every day, he ran through the woods, looking for food, playing with the fireflies, and exploring new places. But when the sun went down, he was always in trouble.  

Sometimes he had no food for dinner. Sometimes he got lost in the dark. And sometimes, he was so far from home that he had to sleep under cold, wet leaves.  

One evening, after another long day of wandering, Remy was far from his burrow when the wind began to howl. A great storm was coming! He ran as fast as he could, but he had no idea which way to go. The trees all looked the same, and the path was muddy and dark.  

Then, through the rustling leaves, a voice called out.  

“Lost again, little rat?”  

It was an old owl, sitting high in a tree. His feathers were speckled with silver, and his big golden eyes glowed in the dark.  

“Yes,” Remy sighed. “I always get lost! I never know where to go, and I never have what I need.”  

The owl hooted softly. “Then you must learn the secret of the *Returning Path*.”  

“The Returning Path?” Remy asked.  

“Yes,” said the owl. “A path you follow every day, so you always know the way back.”  

Remy’s whiskers twitched. “But I like to go wherever I want! I don’t want to follow the same path.”  

The owl chuckled. “Ah, but if you make a habit of coming back the same way, you will never be lost again. And if you always gather food along the way, you will never go hungry.”  

Remy thought about this. It sounded...boring. But he was tired of being cold and lost.  

So the next morning, Remy did something new. He picked a small stone and placed it at the start of a trail. “This will be my Returning Path,” he said.  

As he explored, he placed more stones along the way. Whenever he found food, he took a little extra and hid it under a log. Then, when the sun began to set, he followed his trail of stones home.  

For the first time in a long while, he was home *before* the sky turned dark. He had food, he had a warm place to sleep, and he felt safe.  

Remy did this again the next day. And the next. At first, it felt strange. But soon, it became easy. He no longer worried about being lost. He no longer worried about being hungry.  

Then one evening, another little rat ran up to him, shivering. “Remy! I’m lost! I don’t know the way home!”  

Remy smiled and pointed to his trail of stones. “Follow me,” he said. And he led the little rat home safely.  

And from that day on, Remy was not just the rat who loved to explore—he was the rat who *always* returned.  

And he never worried again.  

**The End.**
