# The Gospyll According To A Chocolatier

Coco Cadibori shut off the light in his chocolate factory and wondered if he'd ever turn it on again.

Centuries ago, explorers like Olombos discovered faraway lands, and brought back Sigri and Cacao. Since then, animals loved to eat the sweet stuff. They tried to put it in everything! Chocolate bars, chocolate bats, chocolate hats, chocolate hares, and other words that only differ by a few letters.

Business had been going quite well. In fact, this candy shop had existed for _millenia_ at this point. Coco didn't know who their mysterious founder had been, but a plaque on the wall said the store started just after The First Conflict. A plaque that was rusted now, missing a piece and half covered by vines. 

The shop had been handed down from generation to generation. Coco's father had run out, and his father, and so on. At some point, roughly around the discovery of Floria, they were so famous that animals from all over the world came to visit.

Then disaster struck. The Second Conflict broke out.

Suddenly, animals were busy running for their lives, instead of eating candy. Money went to weapons, not luxury items.

The biggest problem? Coco couldn't blame them. He often wished their mysterious founder had started a supermarket instead. Or trained new Bearchitects. Or started a massive library. Animals would _always_ need those three necessities: food, shelter, and good stories.

But it was too late to change anything now. Some time ago, steam power and machines were invented, and he'd invested all their money into building a large Chocolate Factory. If you entered the shop, walked to the left, broke into a secret door, and used the slippery slide, you would arrive inside this hidden factory. It was massive. It could create thousands of chocolate bars in a day.

But nobody was buying anymore. And now their money had run out.

Coco was not alone, of course. The cuckoo bird had an associate, his wife, who usually came up with the new ideas.

"I'm out of ideas," she said with a sigh. "We've had quite a run. But this bird has flown. Maybe we should just quit."

"Never," said Coco.

"Easy to say, harder to do. We can't pay for the shop anymore, or enough food in our nest---"

"We have hundreds of chocolate---"

"My body is sick of only eating candy all day! A bird can't survive on lollipops, marshmallows and chocolate! Besides, the more we eat ourselves, the less we can sell."

Coco flew to the window. He looked out over the town square, as his neighbors placed the first Easter decorations. Yes, the Eggcelent Egg Shop next door was having the time of their life. In these dark times, especially, people wanted a symbol of hope and new beginnings. Suddenly, every monkey wanted some cute chicks or bunnies as a pet. Indeed, the Pet Shop next door was doing great business.

"There should always be room for festivities. For tasty food, for rituals, for entertainment. No matter how grim the world gets. But we need to ... we need to _capture_ it. We need to invent a new product."

"Then invent it before tomorrow at noon. That's when the bank wants their money, or they'll kick us out."

His wife gave him a quick beaky kiss on the cheek, then went to sleep in the nest. This was their name for a gigantic circle of twigs and leaves, which hung from the high ceiling in their factory. The machines would normally run all night and provide background noise, but they couldn't spend the money on electricity now, so the wonderful chocolate factory was dead silent.

In the darkness, Coco closed his eyes. He placed his wings before him, covering half his beak. And he prayed.

"_Oh Krystina, I beg for your help,_" he whispered. "_We have endured. We have suffered for months, barely eating, barely sleeping. But we see no way out. The situation is not improving. Give us a sign. An idea. Anything. I beg of you._"

"I HAVE ALREADY GIVEN A SIGN, ARE YOU BLIND?" a voice boomed, echoing against the factory walls. His wife slept soundly; had she not heard?

"Erm, excuse me? Who is this?"

"KRYSTINA. YOU JUST CALLED FOR ME. IS SOMETHING WRONG WITH YOUR MEMORY TOO?" 

The voice was so _full_, so all-encompassing, that Coco felt the need to shield his ears with his wings.

"I ... I didn't know you would answer. So quickly."

"I HAPPENED TO BE IN THE AREA."

Coco's tone of voice radiated disbelief. "Why?"

"SHOPPING."

"I am not worthy of your visit," murmured Coco, "please forgive my response. But can you, maybe, perhaps, mayhaps, _repeat_ that sign you gave me?"

"IN TRUTH, I DO NOT REALLY GIVE SIGNS. IT IS YOU WHO HAS TO _SEE_ THE SIGN."

"This is confusing, oh Lordess of Light."

A booming _sigh_ echoed against the dead machines. It even blew some chocolate bars from the shelves.

"YOUR SHOP HAS GROWN STALE. OPEN YOUR EYES AND TRY SOMETHING NEW."

Coco opened his eyes. The first thing he saw, still behind the window, was his neighbors placing eggs to finish the Easter decorations.

"Could it be ... would it be possible to ..." he whispered to himself.

"I MUST GO. BINGO NIGHT AT THE HEAVENLY PALACE."

Coco's mind was churning. He absently thanked Krystina for answering his prayers. He didn't even notice the rumbling and bolt of lightning that signaled her holy departure. Nor did his wife; she had slept through the whole ordeal.

Chocolate. Eggs.

It was mad, wasn't it? They were hardly able to shape the chocolate. They only had a few molds, and most of them were _rectangles_. Their most complicated product, a chocolate here, required _hours_ of manual work to complete. They had to put the chocolate in one container, then another, then another, and then manually cut out the whiskers and the teeth and the eyes using their beak.

And nobody bought it. Too large, too expensive.

But an egg? An egg was small. You could hold it in your hand, or your paw, or your wing. _Any_ animal could reasonably buy it and eat it. Or, well, except for dogs---allergic to chocolate---and insects---still too small. But those weren't their target audience anyway, and bees could create their own sweet candy in any case.

Chocolate. Egg. How would you do it?

Coco grabbed a sheet of metal and started a fire. By holding the metal over the fire, it would heat up. And at some point, when metal is hot enough, it becomes _malleable_. It becomes soft, squishy, and you can change it. Using his beak---which could withstand the temperature---he started bending, pushing and scratching the metal.

The first attempt failed. Of course it did. Eggs should be perfectly round and smooth. But they're also not a circle. Come to think it, eggs have a really weird shape, hard to imitate.

The second attempt was better, but he had accidentally created a perfect ball instead. That was no use. Additionally, how would they pour the chocolate into the mold? A ball was closed on all sides!

Coco had a divine realization then: to create _two molds_. One for the left side of the egg, one for the right side. This made it easier to shape them without mistakes. It also meant he could pour hot chocolate into them first, and then _combine_ the two to close off the egg.

And so he did.

He worked all night, until the rooster crowed, his wife looked at him funny, and the sun rose.

He had created the right molds for an egg. He had manually---or wingually, I guess---coated them with cacao butter and poured hot chocolate into them.

"The chocolate should have cooled off by now," he said, grinning. "Let us see."

He grabbed the first two molds. He held them over the workbench, then freezed. Afraid of failure? Maybe. Or just to dramatically build the tension.

He broke the two molds apart.

From inside, an egg fell down on the table. Chocolate. Egg. Perfectly smooth. So smooth, in fact, that it rolled off and landed on the floor.

"Five second rule!" yelled his wife, who scooped it up and immediately started it in colorful wrapping paper.

Until noon, Coco and Caca stood side by side and prepared the first fifty chocolate Easter eggs that ever existed. Then the bank manager arrived to demand payment---or take their factory away.

___

Caca took the eggs and quickly set up a stall in front of the shop. It was far easier to sell something if people could see it from afar, and they didn't have to step inside.

The problem, of course, was the bank manager standing right there. 

"Welcome sir," said Coco, "please, come with me."

"Don't play tricks," said the pig gruffly. "Pay up, rent for the last six months, or it's over."

"Oh yes, we have the money. But it's in our very safe safe, very safely, you see?"

"What?"

"Please follow me to the hidden room of our factory," said Coco. "And we can carry the bags of gold to your bank together."

"Hmpf."

The bank manager looked at Caca and the wrapped eggs in her wings. His tail curled more severely, but he didn't say anything of it.

As soon as the pig was inside, she started screaming.

"A new invention! A special Easter treat! Buy it now: Chocolate Eggs!"

On a sheet of paper, she quickly made a calculation. How much chocolate had Coco used per egg? How much did that cost? For how much did they need to sell it to make a profit?

"Only one Soliduri for one egg!"

Soon, their neighbors crowded around the stall. They picked up the eggs, turned them over in their paws, and even unwrapped a few of them to check if it wasn't a trick.

Now, let me be clear, not all eggs were God's perfect creation. Some were chipped. Some were more square than circular. Some hadn't been completely cooled when she took them out, so they were half-perfect and half-oh-god-what-is-this-monstrous-shape.

But the customers didn't seem to care. They marveled at the new creations---and bought them.

Caca struggled to keep up. One wing would accept money, the other would give them the egg, while she wished for a third wing for bookkeeping. 

"What is this nonsense!?" yelled the banking pig, somewhere behind her. "You don't even _have_ a safe safe!"

"Oh no!" lied Coco. "Then it must have been stolen!"

"I am done with this. I'm taking your shop and anything inside, and you will never---"

"No! Wait! Please, I just remembered where we put the safe instead. We moved it for security reasons. If you would please follow me back to the front of the shop ..."

Caca wished she had a fourth wing with which to slap the pig in the face. Or herself, because she realized she had made a grave mistake.

Selling each egg for one Soliduri was very profitable ... but not enough to pay off their debt.

She had already sold forty of their eggs. She couldn't suddenly raise the price, could she? No, her neighbors would feel cheated. They'd never buy from them again!

"You can, erm, also order eggs in advance," she invented on the spot. "We're preparing more as we speak. Our machines are running tirelessly!"

"Well, I guess they'd be really nice to have for our Easter dinner," said a mother.

"Yes! Yes! Chocolate eggs for dinner!" yelled a baby fox.

Caca felt the looming presence of the banking pig at her back. Coco was out of excuses and had to watch, awkwardly, as he hammered a sign onto their shop saying "SOLD TO THE BANK".

Families placed extra orders. Animals living in other neighborhoods had even heard of the Chocolate Egg invention and came to visit. 

She mad a quick calculation.

Then, as the pig finished nailing the sign onto their window, she turned around. She smiled so much her beak hurt.

"Good sir, here is the money you were looking for," she said.

She gave him all the profit of that day. Exactly how much they owed, not a Soliduri more or less.

"Hmpf," said the pig gruffly, and they wondered whether the word actually meant something in Piggish.

The pig stomped off. "You can take off the sign yourself. Hmpf."

The Cuckoo Chocolatiers did just that. Then, when they had completely sold out, they went back to the factory to quickly make _a lot more chocolate eggs_.

That Easter was a large success. So large, in fact, that their competitors were eying them with an evil eye. They wanted to copy them, but didn't have Coco's skill in creating the right mold, or Caca's skill in wrapping them as cutely. Other candy shops had to create each egg fully by hand (or paw, or fin), as they hadn't invested in those machines.

Next Easter, as their machines worked tirelessly to create thousands of Chocolate Eggs, there was a massive break-in.

___


When I read the previous Gospyll, three things stood out to me. 

First of all, I found it hard to believe that the chickens could hate Astra Buni so. It seemed unreasonable. More likely, I think El Chicko is a criminal and they just wanted to make it seem like they are noble and had good reasons. You will see why I think so lowly of El Chicko soon.

Second of all, I found it hard to believe that Astra Buni would not get her place in heaven. She has been a good being, better than most, for longer than most. In fact, last time I saw her, she seemed just as young as she was a few millenia ago. Perhaps painting eggs is the cure against aging after all. Or there's some secret magic she isn't telling us about. Maybe if you paint an egg like a mirror, it becomes a lotion for eternal youth or something.

And Krystina must have seen this. If she had the time to hear my prayers in the chocolate factory, her spirit must have watched over Astra Buni many times. I personally wouldn't even know what to do if I were dead, but still hanging around in the real world. After a while, scaring people by hiding in their closet must get stale too.

In any case, the Easter Bunny---as she was now known---deserves a place in the Heavenly Palace when she dies. But I, my wife, and basically everyone, hope she never dies.

Third of all, it doesn't take a math wizard to know that some thousand chicks are not enough. If Astra Buni is to visit _the entire world_ during Easter, and hide her eggs for _good_ children to find---well, she's gonna need more eggs than that. And thus more chickens. But the rumors about Astra Island being too full already, I am sad to say, were true.

Now let us return to the break-in.

Glass shattered. A light turned on, then off again. Then on, then off, like a disco, until the pig realized their fat butt was accidentally triggering the light-switch. 

The door was forced open, but the blast was barely audible over the hum of the busy machines. Coco woke up, but didn't know why, at first. His wife heard nothing.

Shady figures crawled through the factory. The footsteps grew louder, until they were right underneath The Nest hanging in the air. Coco held his breath, then dared turn around and peek.

"Steal what you can," whispered one of the dark silhouettes. Coco thought it was probably a chicken.

"Destroy the rest," whispered the pig. The pig! The bank manager! "We don't want them miraculously finding money again. This shop has caused enough damage, and I want it to be _mine_! MINE!"

"What part of _we must be silent as the grave_ did you not understand?" said the chicken, annoyed.

"Maybe my grave won't be silent," said the pig. "I have enough money to pay a troubadour to play songs on it forever."

Coco frowned. Once the pig started destroying machines, he could safely leave the nest without being heard. He flew to a spot more nearby. But what for? What could he do against multiple intruders? 

At that moment, another group of shady figures entered from the other side.

Glass shattered. A disco. Door blasted off its hinges. You know the deal.

Only this group did not walk. Their leader hopped. And it apologized for destroying our door.

"Get out! This is our burglary!"

"_Burglary_? Well have I ever---"

"All of you get out!" screamed Coco, descending on them like a bird of prey. Of course, a cuckoo is _not_ a bird of pray, so besides sticking some feathers into a pig's mouth, he didn't achieve much. "I have already informed the police!"

The pig shrugged and continued destroying machines. The chicken jumped into shadows, as Caca reached the light-switch and turned it on.

Coco didn't recognize most of the burglars. But on the other side stood Astra Buni, holding a massive basket and looking for a place to put an egg.

"Oh, oh no," said Coco to Astra, "sorry for calling you a burglar."

"What? But she _did_ break in!" said the pig. His behind crushed a machine and the chocolate it just poured into a mold.

"No when I do it, it's _different_!" Astra had found a good hiding spot and placed an egg. "The cuckoos have been very good. I came to hide a few magical eggs. And to talk to them."

"Talk to us?" said Coco and Caca simultaneously. His breath caught; she blushed. 

"Yes, yes, no need to be like that," said Astra, fumbling around in her oversized egg-shaped pockets.

"I'm not having any of this," roared the pig, mistaking himself for a lion. He had brought four henchmen---which means accomplices---which means other strong and wicked animals dressed in black clothing---and they attacked Coco and Caca at once.

"_I_ will get rich from selling chocolate eggs! And _you_ will be in jail and without your precious factory! Ha ha! Ha ha! OINK!"

Coco shrieked and folded his wings around his wife.

The first blow hit.

But not the cuckoos.

"I will _not_ have any of this in this holy factory!" yelled Astra.

She was done fumbling. She had thrown an egg with purple-pink swirly patterns going in five directions. Which, apparently, meant a levitation spell.

The pig and his henchman were forced to leave the floor. They flew higher and higher, bumping into lamps, extended machine arms, and The Nest, until they were stuck on the ceiling.

The spell also took their final henchman, the chicken, with them.

And it was El Chicko.

"_You?!_" said Astra.

"Oh don't be a saint," responded El Chicko. "You know you pay us far too little."

"I don't pay you at all," said Astra with a frown.

"Exactly!"

"The payment is knowing you did the right thing," she proposed. "Money should be of no consequence for a true Krystian." 

This never seems to be a compelling argument, though, for thieves to stop thieving.

After the police was actually informed, they came and cleaned up. Many chocolate eggs were ruined or broken, and the damage to machines was severe, but they'd manage. Especially with the massive new order they just received.

"The chickens are not enough anymore," said Astra seriously. "Besides, I think we need to stop using real eggs, as much as possible. They start to rot if not found in time. Throwing them creates such a mess. I want to replace them with chocolate eggs completely. _Your_ chocolate eggs, from this holy factory."

Astra later admitted that her magic was far less powerful and controlled on chocolate eggs. She needed more practice with it, and some spells were changed entirely. But she never told this to the public, as it is a serious weakness, so I also won't.

"Why ..." said Caca. "Why do you keep calling our factory _holy_?"

Astra hopped around, as if searching her own eggs, until she found what she was looking for. The other half of the broken plaque.

The top half told them the date. This factory was founded some years after the Resurrection of Krystina.

The bottom half told them who did so. Krystina herself.

Coco immediately fell to his knees again---or what went for knees in the sticky legs of a bird---and folded his wings before him in prayer.

"Oh, Krystina, we are not worthy. We hope we did right by your ... candy guidelines. We will make this factory the shining beacon of the chocolate egg market again."

A booming voice rattled some machines, and the glass shards on the floor. "WHAT? PRAYING TO ME AGAIN?" 

"Yes? I want to thank you for the factory. And I am sorry if I interrupted you---"

"DON'T SWEAT IT."

"I will try not to invoke you for every prayer."

"YES. THANK YOU. THIS IS HURTING MY VOICE."

Coco Cadibori soon invented all sorts of different flavors, such as the Cadibori Creme Egg. Astra Buni noticed this changed her magic too, sometimes in dangerous ways, and decided to stick mostly to basic dark chocolate eggs for now.

What was certain, though, was that Krystina's Kandyland had become world-famous once more. Animals visited from all over the world. Tourists trying to view the holy site. Chocolate lovers to try the new varieties of Easter egg. And Astra Buni herself, of course, to collect millions of them for hiding during Easter.

The relationship between Astra and El Chicko was never restored. The chicken continued trying to plan chicken coups from inside chicken coops. The obvious solution, it seems to me, would be to simply build a different type of housing for El Chicko. Astra forgave the chicken, but also banished all unruly chicks and chickens from Astra Island. They say that the chickens still aimlessly swim around the Sultry Sea to this day. Some claim they eventually turned into a species they call _duck_.

This was my Gospyll. The next time you eat a chocolate egg, remember old Coco and Caca, who continued to make them for years and years to come. And if you ever have the time, visit Krystina's Kandyland. Those who have faith will always find its location.
