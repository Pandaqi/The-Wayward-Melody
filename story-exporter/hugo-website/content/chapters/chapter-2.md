# The Dwarven Walls

The next day, Jenna went to visit Bibi the Buildersdaughter. As her name suggested, she was the granddaughter of Basco the Builder---the man who built The Wall. Jenna told her mother they were great friends and would just be playing together. Of course, as you will now find out, the two of them had never spoken to each other in their life.

"Could you help me?" asked Jenna.

"Help _you_?" said Bibi, turning up her nose. "The stranger that is _destroying_ the great Wall? Uh-uh."

"It was an accident," said Jenna quickly. "I want to make it right."

Bibi grabbed a hammer and unleashed her anger on a chair. If you were to visit her workshop at night, you would always hear her grumpy mumbling. About how grandfather was famous, but she was not. About how Basco built an impressive Wall, while she was stuck repairing people's chairs and tables.

"You can make it right by repairing the hole."

"I can't. I'm not a great builder, like you. Can you, erm, tell me something about how the Wall was built?"

Bibi narrowed her eyes. "Why do you want to know that?"

"As punishment, I now have to learn everything about our mighty wall," lied Jenna.

Bibi sighed. She dropped her hammer and gave the sturdy chair back to a satisfied customer. "What is there to know? Basco drew up a plan, he ordered stone to be delivered, and then they build a wall."

"Just like that? Where do you get the stone? Where do you _start_? It feels overwhelming."

Bibi grinned. She saw her chance. "Honestly, Basco was more of a ... how do you say it ... _manager_. Yes, he managed the project. But others actually did all the hard work."

"Like?"

"Dwarves."

"_Dwarves?_"

"Tiny people. Live underground. Cut out lots of stone. Can wriggle into small places to fix things. Believe me---_many_ things went wrong when building that Wall."

Bibi casually opened several drawers with her feet. She found an old map of The Wall. Sketched by Basco himself, long ago, stained and turned yellow over time.

"How do you build a big wall?" asked Bibi, then answering the question herself. "You build a bunch of smaller walls."

She pointed at a section colored red. Then she pointed at another section colored blue. 

Jenna realized the entire wall had actually been ten smaller walls. They were built one after another, instead of at the same time.

"They don't even use the same kind of rock," said Bibi. "Because Basco's favorite rock ran out after only three weeks. One part of the wall is made of _wood_. Another part is made of _sand_, and that red section is even made of _strawberries_."

"Interesting, interesting," said Jenna, stroking her chin. 

"It is, isn't it?" said Bibi with a smile. She felt glad that she could talk to someone about her passion. And reveal just how terrible of a builder Basco had actually been.

But there was still something Jenna didn't understand. "Then why does The Wall look the same on the outside? To me it just looks like _one wall_."

Bibi shrugged. "If you don't know what to look for, then you don't know. Also, the dwarves did a great job painting over it."

"Well, I hope we paid the dwarves enough for their work."

"Basco did not," said Bibi. "Why do you think we're all forced to do the same boring work all day? I _hate_ building a thousand chairs! But we need to pay off our dwarf debt."

Jenna wanted to leave this place now more than ever. Surely, the other side of The Wall would be _better_ than this?

"Thanks a lot, Bibi the Buildersdaughter. I must be going now!"

Bibi frowned. She'd wanted to tell even more fun stories about The Wall, but now she froze with her mouth wide open.

"Say, what was your name again?"

"Jenna. Don't tell my mother I was here!"

And Jenna ran off. 

With a Wall this large, you can see it at all times. Wherever you are in the town, you can turn around and find the wall again. But what _part_ of the wall you can see, is of course different.

Jenna walked around town for hours, looking for the best spot. She tried to look ... normal. As if she had important jobs to do in that part of town.

And then she found the spot. On top of a grassy hill, close to the shoemaker, she had a great view of the Strawberry Section. The weakest part of the wall.

Jenna realized now that it was not _one_ mysterious, overwhelming Wall at all! It was made of smaller parts. And those smaller parts she could understand more easily. She could focus on destroying _one_ dwarven wall at a time, instead of the whole thing. If she looked closely, she thought she could see the squashed strawberries through the layers of paint.

That night, Jenna snuck out again. This time, she had made a better plan. She left behind a straw doll of herself. She had "accidentally" blocked her bedroom door. And she left behind her torch to find her way in complete darkness.

After some stumbles and scraped knees, she reached the Strawberry Section.

She looked around one final time. She held her breath---was she really going to do this? If she was caught now, they'd throw her over the wall and left her behind. Nobody ever returned.

But she _had_ to know! 

Through the thin strawberry layer, she could hear more sounds from the other side. They sounded more crisp, instead of muffled. They sounded louder.

It allowed her to make the next discovery. Whenever they paused for a while, it meant the end of one sentence. But when they paused for _a really short moment_, would that not be the end of _one word_?

She memorized the words she heard. Soon enough, she thought she heard the words in _other_ sentences. Someone said the word at the end, but then another responded and said the word at the beginning. Someone said the word, but with the final sound changed a bit. Like saying _"eavesdropping"_ instead of _"eavesdrop"_.

Then the monsters on the other side walked away. Within a few steps, they were so far away from the wall that Jenna could barely hear them anymore.

"No!" she whispered. "Don't go!"

She took her hammer---a torch with a stone---and started destroying the wall again. This time, it barely made a sound. Just a _squish_ and a _slab_ as she cut away the fruity stones. It was much easier than before, and the hole grew faster.

But it was still a _big wall_ and Jenna a _big human_. She worked for minutes, which turned into hours, which turned into---oh no, how long had she been here?

Sweat dripped from her brow. Her arms were too tired to swing the hammer again. She stumbled away from the wall and felt hopeless again. She had barely slept the past few days, and there was something ... magical about The Wall. 

It seemed to repair _itself_.

Any time she almost had a hole large enough to step through ... she would take a break and the hole would shrink again.

Afternoon had arrived again. Jenna heard some monsters say goodbye again or wish each other good night. But which one was it? Did that word mean "good" or "night"? Was that sound she heard even a word, or something ... even smaller? She tried to mimick the sound herself, but found she could not do it.

She suddenly realized the next pattern.

The Wall repaired itself, yes, but not like humans repair wounds. It didn't keep going until the hole was completely closed. Fortunately, it also didn't leave a scar.

Instead, the Strawberry Wall was made out of even smaller parts.

Suddenly, Jenna felt two strong hands on her shoulders. She had completely missed a figure who watched her all this time, hidding behind some shrubs, and sneakily approached her from behind.

"You're so predictable," said a familiar female voice.

Jenna smiled. She had feared it was a guard, but it was Bibi instead.

"Look! We can almost see the other side! But how does the repairing work? What are those smaller parts?"

"Well, how do you build a small wall?" asked Bibi, then answering the question herself. "You place one brick, then the next brick, and so on. Strawberry Bricks, in this case."

Bibi pointed it out. Wherever Jenna had removed an entire brick, the hole remained. Where Jenna had only cut half a brick, or damaged it a bit, the brick would fully regrow. The bricks were red, of course, and shaped like a box. Square on all sides, placed in neat little lines.

"Thanks!" said Jenna. Her mind formed a plan: tomorrow night, she'd only take out entire bricks. The Wall wouldn't repair her hole, and she'd be able to see and hear the monsters completely! All from the safety of her own side! "Oh, and don't tell anyone I was---"

"I am sorry," said Bibi. "I already told them hours ago. You're _so_ predictable."

A group of guards popped out of their hiding places. One struggled to break free from his shrub, but the others had Jenna in handcuffs in an instant.

"We don't like doing this," said the guard, "but you forced us. Look at what you did! A massive hole! The punishment is clear: Wall banishment it is!"

Jenna was too scared to say anything more. She only mumbled goodbye to her very disappointed parents. She was placed inside a ball of twigs and leaves, then placed in a catapult that Basco the Builder had invented too. With all the power in the world, it _barely_ shot high enough to cross the wall. Sometimes they had to try several times. Bibi told everyone that her grandfather had made some bad calculations.

With Jenna being a small girl, lightweight, it had no trouble shooting her over The Wall.
