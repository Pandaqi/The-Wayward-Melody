# Good Riddance

The phrase "good riddance" is an interesting one. It means it was _good_ that you _got rid of something_. You're happy you lost something. You're overjoyed that someone left.

Isn't that strange? You might not be able to imagine a moment when that is the case. You're usually sad when you lose something, or when your birthday is over. 

But what if you were attacked by a lion? You'd be glad if the lion was distracted by a butterfly and left. Or what if you were holding a prickly cactus? You and your hands would be glad to lose it. Or what if monsters attacked your cute little town? You'd be glad to have a Wall that keeps them out.

There are certainly moments where one might say "good riddance". The events in this next story are, unfortunately, not among those moments.

Jenna crashed into a tree on the other side of The Wall. A very soft tree that caught her like a green pillow.

At first, she thought she had been very fortunate. What are the chances! Not even a scratch!

Until she opened her eyes and stood up.

She stood in the middle of a clearing. The tree that had caught her was the only one here. Everything else was either grass or a wooden stand. A stand, yes, holding many spectators. Some of them clapped; others trudged away in disappointment. One of them pointed at her and made a comment, which made the spectators nearby laugh.

Jenna felt uneasy. She stepped to the left, then to the right, then turned around, but she didn't know where to go. The stands circled her. As if she stood on a stage and tens of monsters had come to watch her arrive.

But these were no monsters. They were people!

Sure, they looked a bit strange. These people had huge ears and huge mouths. As if their head was too small.

And when they spoke, it was _loud_. Speaking for them was as loud as Jenna's loudest scream. As if they were more afraid of not being heard than of annoying others.

She noticed two people---a man and a woman---standing in the clearing with her. They seemed to be a mixture: their mouths were normal size, but their ears were on the large side.

They looked Jenna over, head to toe, toe to head, and back again. They took out a faded drawing on paper, and compared her to it. Then they shook their head. They had a look of pity, but they didn't come to help her.

Slowly, the stands emptied. Jenna was left alone in the dark. It was night in here now, and day in _her_ world. Her world. Would she ever return? Or would she have to live here now, with people she could never understand? Was it really that bad that she tried to break The Wall?

Jenna sat down and cried. Until morning came.

Light returned to this place. The people left their homes to go eat, or work, or sit in the stands and hope someone else was catapulted here today. They greeted each other with a wave and some spoken sound---

Jenna bolted upright. She _recognized_ the start of that sound. It sounded like---like one of those words she heard before.

If they were saying "Good Night" before ... then maybe they were saying "Good Morning" now? 

She couldn't make the sound herself. Her mouth was too small, she thought. I just _can't_ say that.

But she could recognize the sound, and she now thought it meant "good". But she couldn't be sure. She had to test it.

And so she finally stood up and left the arena with the stands. She walked into a town that looked very similar to hers, only the walls were thicker---more soundproof---and there was _far_ more space. The town seemed to go on forever. This side had an entire jungle, and mountains, and a lake. Compared to this place, Jenna's town looked like a miniature dwarven town.

She passed some people playing with a ball. They effortlessly kicked it to each other over a long distance. Jenna could never have played this back home---there wasn't enough space. Of course, they were shouting to each other as they played, and Jenna felt inclined to cover her ears. Why did they all talk _so loud_?

And how on earth did she hear _none of it_, all those years, on the other side!?

She wandered straight into their game. The ball narrowly missed her head.

A tall boy asked her something, loudly. She didn't understand. He held up the ball.

Of course. He was asking if she wanted to play! So Jenna made her best attempt at saying _good_ in their language. 

She hoped, very much, that she wasn't saying something terrible by accident. She would look really silly if the word actually meant "night" or "sleep". It would be even sillier if the boy asked "could you please get out of the way?" and she said "good!", and then just kept standing there. Utmost silliness would be achieved if the boy asked "who are you?" and she had accidentally said "sleep!".

The tall boy looked puzzled. Then he smiled and passed the ball to her.

She was ... accepted? Jenna couldn't believe it as she played along with their game. Her first kicks broke a window and a vase that hopefully wasn't one of a kind. But she soon got the hang of it and they all yelled the same happy word when she scored a goal.

These were not monsters, far from it. They were very nice! She had already had more fun _here_ than she ever had on the other side of the wall!

But ... if these are not monsters, then why was The Wall built? Why did Basco shut them out---while giving them way more space---and lie to everyone?

She played with the children all day. Though they soon got tired of this game and played other sports with other balls: a small yellow one, a big orange one, a very heavy one with three holes in it. Whenever they grabbed a new one, they would say its word.

The word was different each time. Of course it was! It would be silly to name all round objects the exact same thing.

But the _end_ of the word was the same. Something something, blabla. Something else, blabla. They weren't saying two words. There was no pause. It was always one word, just with the last part switched out. 

It took Jenna all day to remember the word for it: a syllable. And that it was the same in _her_ language: foot-_ball_, basket-_ball_, base-_ball_, and so forth.

She thought words were the smallest you could get. Like how she thought bricks were the smallest parts of The Wall.

Now she knew better. 

As night fell, and they all said "Good Night" to each other, she returned to The Wall. For the first time, she noticed that not all bricks were the same. Some were longer than others. Some were higher than others. Some started as one type of rock, then changed to a different type of rock. All the bricks in the Strawberry Section had strawberry _somewhere_. But it was often mixed with some wood, or sand, or a precious ring that a dwarf lost and had never found again. In that sense, The Wall was a huge lost-and-found for objects that belonged to those who built it.

Bricks were heavy objects. Hard to carry, hard to break.

But a chunk of strawberry? A small pile of sand? _That_ Jenna could understand. She could carry it in her hand. She could practice playing with it over and over, until she understood every inch of that Wall.

Something else was different too. She couldn't quite place it. The Wall definitely looked _different_ from this side, as a whole. But why? She looked and looked until her eyelids drooped and she finally fell asleep.

When she woke up, morning had come again. But that's not what woke her. It was a man and a woman standing nearby, hugging someone who was probably their child. They had a backpack, and eyes set on the hills in the distance.

And they made that same sound again! The sound that meant _good_. This time, however, it was part of a word. Only a syllable. It was only the _start_ of a word.

So what could they be saying? Could it be ...

Jenna realized the answer as she saw the child leave, with tears in his eyes. They were saying _goodbye_.

In the days that followed, Jenna listened like a dog. Of course, it wasn't hard to hear these big-mouthed people said. But it took a while to hear patterns. To hear a syllable used here, and there, and then in another word. To get a list of five or ten syllables, and what Jenna _thought_ they might mean. 

She figured out _foot_ after a kid had gotten something sharp stuck in their shoe. She figured out _ing_ because they made that sound whenever someone was _doing_ something. She figured out _hair_ when someone had accidentally shaved off all of it and ran around town in a panic.

She repeated these syllables over and over until she found ways to say them. Until she could pick them out even when someone talked really quietly, or slurred their words, or modified them into a longer word like "foot-ball-ing".

Jenna believed she could learn their language. And she believed The Wall could disappear, and everyone could live together again.

She was about to ask her first question. By combining the syllables she understood, and speaking them in a way that was mostly wrong but close enough, she wanted to ask the nearest person for help.

As she entered town, however, the large-eared people had all gathered in one spot. And they did something that changed Jenna's mind completely.
