# My First Stories I

**Advances:**

**Description**:
With a few words and letters under your belt
An apprentice can quickly start to appreciate a tale
This resource has fables, creativity and a magical whale
Until even the youngest learners can tell a story heartfelt

**Extra:**
This is the final teaching resource for Language in Level 1. There's a reason humans evolved to tell and enjoy stories. Putting information and language into a wider, practical context---one that creates mysteries or stirs up emotion---helps learn that information much faster.

Of course, with only a seed of language comprehension present in kids at this age, the stories are incredibly simple. Once they've seen a few good fairy tales, however, they'll be hungry for more. The best you can hope for, after using this resource, is a child who soon devours any books they see!

As the saying goes: "Parents just need to read, read, read until their little ones are ready to read, read, read"

**Contents**:
* "Listening Comprehension". Have Guardian read a simple story about someone moving around a forest, or following a specific sequence of events. The kids get a drawing/path/map with holes cut out. As they listen to the story, they must place the cut-out events at the right location in the story.
* **Repeat Back**. Read some stories, then ask questions about it and have children repeat back part of the story?
* **"Fill in the story blanks."** => In the simplest version, the parent/teacher just reads the story to the kid(s). Possible inputs for the blanks are given, and children must point at one and say what it is. ("So he paid money to the ..." "COW!")
  * At the start, to help find the right answers, maybe all these blanks RHYME.
* **"Game of Telephone"**: You are told a story/given a story. You must then tell it again, but certain key words are forbidden, so you must find synonyms/other ways to phrase it. (Can start more simply with just single sentences.)
* **Read and Mark** => Read a very simple text with lots of images. Keep a word cloud next to it, and cross off any that you find. 
  * Maybe it's a story about stealing or magic or whatever. And it ends with the question "Who stole the cow?", and the answer to that is in the word cloud if you solved it properly!
  * **At this stage, it's extremely simple, and the Guardian reads it with them.** (As children can't recognize letters themselves yet.)
