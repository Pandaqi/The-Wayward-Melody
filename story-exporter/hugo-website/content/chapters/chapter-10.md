# The Home of Hereborn

Comm the pear looked everywhere. He was a living fruit, more like you than you think maybe. He needed friends, and playmates, and a loving family. And what had he been doing? He'd been gone for weeks! Maybe his daughter had already been born, while he was studying mountain peaks!

And so he raced to find a home before night. Today, yes, today he would decide. 

It would be nice to have a garden, but it didn't need to be very large or very green.

It would be nice to live near water, but it didn't need to be the only thing she's ever seen.

The home needed to be large enough for three fruits at least. But any taller or bigger was not a great need.

Noise is annoying, but silence is too. He'd come to like birdsong and waking up to the sounds of moo, ewe and mew.

And sure, Comm liked summer, but not when it got _too_ hot. Rain and wind were needed too, with the balance that they brought.

You could say more toys are better, but is that really true? You only have two hands at a time; only one body to visit a playground or a zoo. 

A few toys was enough, it was more than Comm had ever had. And they didn't need five tables and four chairs, as long as his daughter had a soft bed.

You need food to survive, of course, but nobody eats an entire grain field a day. If he could find a place with bits of different food nearby, that would already be okay.

If he could find a home with all of that, his search would be done. And as afternoon arrived, he actually found one!

A little hut near the beach, at the edge of the woods. He compared everything with what he wanted, and decided all was good.

Unfortunately ... it was missing a final piece. There was nobody around, except some angry selfish geese.

"What are you looking at!?" they said to him. "You're a strange pear! With ugly hands!"

Comm felt that, most of all, it was important to live among family and friends.

With a sad face, he left the perfect home and searched. And searched. And searched. And searched.

Night fell. In the darkness, he could barely make out the difference between home, tree, or animal. He stumbled forward, desperately hoping to find the greatest place to live. But every comparison failed now. Every home had something that was _worse_ than somewhere else. A big home was noisy. A home with a veggie patch was built upside-down for some reason. And a fabulous hut in the mountains didn't have a garden anywhere in sight.

Until ... could it be? 

He couldn't see in the darkness, but he could feel. He found a new home, and he felt, and listened, and looked around, and checked the inside. And everything seemed right. Just large enough. Not too noisy. Didn't he pass a farm and some grain fields along the way? And he bumped into his best friends too, what a coincidence.

Then Comm suddenly froze. His pear hands touched a bit of wood, he knew it like the back of his toes.

He'd come back home.

He'd traveled the whole world and was never satisfied. Whenever he found a good place, there was always _something_.

And now his wife yelled: "Comm! You're back! Oh, just in time, the baby is coming!"

He ran inside, having made up his mind. 

He sat on their small bed and hugged his wife, tears down his pear face. And not long after ... his newborn daughter lay in his place.

Comm had seen the world now, dear listener, that was truer than true. But he'd never seen something as beautiful and right as his daughter Pearaboo. He could compare her to anything, and she would always win. And as he held her in his arms, he knew where her life should begin.

Here, right here, where they had just enough and they had each other. The pear family lived happily ever after, and Pearaboo would soon also get a brother.

Comm had compared every home, upside-down, noisy, big or overgrown. But now he truly realized: there _is_ no place like home.
