# Word Awareness

**Description**:
The next step to learning language
Is to break it down into individual parts
This simple resource brings awareness
To the words that are sentence's beating hearts

**Extra:**
Children intuitively (or "naturally") learn language through speaking and sound. That doesn't necessarily mean they understand what a word is or how they build their own sentences.

This resource marks the first step to "breaking down" whole sentences into smaller pieces. Words come first, of course. After this resource, you can continue by building _syllable_ awareness, and eventually _phonemes_ (the individual sounds when speaking). 

All of this is necessary to build a strong foundation for jumping into actual alphabet letters ("_graphemes_") when Level 2 comes around. Always remember: counting words seems very basic to any adult, but is a new and challenging skill for those who just entered our beautiful world.

NOTE: Start with compound words, as those are just two _whole words_ smashed together. Then move to 2-syllable, 3-syllable, etc. (See that syllable-word-list PDF)

* Explanation
  * Good Explanation: You can compare lack of word awareness (in a child) to listening someone speak a foreign language---do you know where one word ends and another starts?
  * Define: "Every time we pause shortly / finish one block of a sentence, it's a word"?
  * Define: "Every _thing_ is one word."?
* Count words in sentence (clap, move disks, jump, etc).
* Say which sentence has more words.
* _Sort_ sentences by number of words?
* First/Last word?
* Odd one out: identify the sentence that has a different #words than the others.

ACTIVITIES:
* Filling in blanks
* Jumbled-up sentences => Apprentices need to put back in the right order
  * You can do this by merely _saying_ it.
  * Or you can involve them physically. For example, give every kid their own WORD. Now they stand in a line, and you must rearrange the line until the order makes sense.
* Just label/name everything throughout the day.
* Scavenger hunt (for labelled pictures around the (class) room).
* Jump/Clap/Do a dance/wiggle when you hear a new word in a sentence.
  * "Word Stepping": create circles/spots on the ground. For every word said in a sentence, they step to the next circle. (Can be a bit more fantastical/adventurous.)
* "Word Wall": around a specific topic/image, collect more and more related words over time. (Like a mind map thingy.)
