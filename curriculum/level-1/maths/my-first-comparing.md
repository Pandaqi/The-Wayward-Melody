# My First Comparing

**Description:**
Once an apprentice recognizes shapes, colors and more
They are ready for the next step in the lore
Instead of saying things are equal or not, that's that
This resource teaches many ways to compare, group and contrast

**Extra: @TODO: This is important info, but should it be here? Explained elsewhere?** 
In general, we can distinguish two different ways of learning. Everyone possesses both, but usually has more natural affinity with one than the other.

Some learn _analytically_: they start from abstract ground principles and build on that. For example, they first learn what a _line_ is, and then a _triangle_, and then a _square_, and so forth.

Some learn _holistically_: they start from groups of meaning in the real world and work backwards to uncover its ground principles. For example, they first learn about homes and work backwards to understand they are _squares_ with a _triangle_ roof.

That's why this resource mixes them both. Some exercises go analytically, some holistically.

**Contents:**
* Two groups are given. Write ">" or "<" (or "=?") between them to mark which one is larger. _Unique Twist: When done, this reveals some sort of smiley? ><_
  * Once in raw numbers/quantity.
  * Once in literal size/dimensions.
  * _The Teaching Resources basically teach these symbols and their meaning._
* A number of things are given on the page. Draw a line through them that puts them in the right order. (That is, the line starts at 1 animal, then goes to 2 animals, etcetera => all of this without actual counting/numbers yet!)
* Cut out a collection of basic shapes. Now _sort_ them by size, then #edges.
* Cut out a collection of funny beings. Now _sort_ them in many different ways (length first, then width, then #eyes).
* (Cut out a collection of animals. Compare their WEIGHT on some balancing sheet? Can we assume children know that elephants are much heavier than ants? If we illustrate their respective weights with images first, they might ...)
* Have a bunch of puzzles/exercises around characters with preferences. It's your job to mark the option they'll pick, at every decision punt/turn, until you get a path to their home/the secret hideout. (Which is hinted at with the hint? Obvious once you get there?)
  * "Feasting Fox": always picks the side which has MORE food or BIGGER food.
  * "Lazy Lion": always picks the side that is MOST NEARBY or EASIEST TO REACH.
  * "Eww-Height Elephant": always picks the side that is LOWEST / LEAST TALL.
  * "Bluesight Bull": always picks the side with COLOR RED OR BLUE
  * "Mathy Mouse": always picks the side with a HIGHER quantity of stuff.
  * "Scared Spider": always picks the side WITHOUT scary beasts/predators
  * "Circle Cat": always picks the side with the ROUND SHAPE over the SHARP SHAPE (Circle vs Square)
* Combine COMPARING and GROUPING. With the things you cut out ...
  * First group them by type.
  * Then sort the _groups_. (Each type appears a unique number of times, so that group size is different and sortable.)
* ADVANCED VERSION of GROUPING: instead of circling, you _connect_ all animals of same type with lines. This gives definite shapes + requires slightly finer motor skills.
  * **In these things, we really want a HINT at the end about what should magically come out.** (For example, if connecting groups should yield the petals of a flower, then we show some grass and flowers with question marks around it + open space in the middle to draw the flower yourself once you see it.)
  * (IDEA: Once you've gone through all these GROUPING versions, another code or result reveals itself? Because of all the groups an animal has been part of?)
