{{% section-foldable header="How To Teach It" %}}

Below are some general notes about teaching this subject as a whole.

* Our brains have evolved to use **speech** as the primary method of communication. That's why _sounds_ and _saying words out loud_ should always be a massive part of any linguistic education. Students who vocalize words, and are surrounded by a lot of spoken language, will pick things up much faster.
* As a consequence, the best thing you can do is **read stories to your Apprentices**. Read to your kids until they are able to read themselves. Even if they can already read, you can still read to them _more complex_ stories (for which they're not read yet). Stories should be fun. Convincing a child to just _listen_ is much easier than making it do boring exercises.
* Learning your first language is very hard. Learning a second language is a bit easier, but still hard. Once you know two languages, though, learning new ones becomes much easier because you can associate those concepts with what you already know. If possible, **consistently use _two_ languages** with your students from a young age.
* The fastest way to improve your command of a language is to simply **use it a lot**. Read a lot, write a lot, speak a lot. No school exercise can beat that. As such, your most important job is to make students excited to _play with language_ all the time. Whether they memorize all the rules or do all their exercises is absolutely secondary.
* Research clearly shows that **people read worse (and memorize less) from screens and typing**. If possible, make Apprentices read from _physical books_ and make them _write by hand_. 

Our resources have been designed to encourage all these good practices and make it easy to stick to them.

{{% section-foldable header="What Is (Not) Included?" %}}

This store contains **many books**, but they aren't exclusive to the Language subject. _Every_ topic has one or more related stories. As such, the Language resources often recommend books (a "reading list") that are classified in a different category on this store.

Resources are focused on English. **Foreign languages** are planned for inclusion at a later stage, but aren't available at this moment. Many of our **quizzes** and **games**, however, work in all languages that use the same alphabet. Their rulebook might be in English, but the game itself is language-independent.

We deviate from the standard curriculum (in most parts of the world) by putting spoken communication first and putting a much (much) lower emphasis on getting the nitty-gritty details right. We focus on teaching a deep intuition and understanding of communication, and using fun tools (like puzzles and games) to expand vocabulary.
