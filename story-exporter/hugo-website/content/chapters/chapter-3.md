# The Hut of Improbable Height

Comm the Pear looked everywhere. He was a fruit much larger than you know, and had a baby expected tomorrow. He and his wife lived in a tiny ugly hut, and always bumped their head against everything. He wanted something better for his daughter, something she could easily fit in.

Soon he found a hut, and it was like he had dreamed. Twice as large as his current home, or so it seemed. Happily he went inside, enjoying the rooms so tall and wide.

But then he made a big mistake. He looked through he window, across the lake.

Another hut stood right there, and it seemed even taller! A bigger home was always better, right? Even if it was an ugly color.

And so he found a hollow watermelon and used it as a boat. As the afternoon arrived, he stood with a lump in his throat. The new hut was enormous, as if it was made for a giant beast. It had four kitchens and bedrooms for twenty children at least!

It took him all afternoon to walk up and down the stairs. Though you must know, dear listener, they're quickly tired, those living pears.

Comm smiled from ear to ear, believing he'd found his perfect home right here. But then he made a big mistake. He looked through the window, across the lake.

Another hut stood right there, a purple magical house. It reached so high that it disappeared in the clouds!

And so Comm found another watermelon ship and, as the sun set, made another trip. The closer he came, the more he was sure. This was an even bigger house than before.

He walked through the front door and couldn't believe his eyes. This hut seemed tall enough to fit a million peary guys. His old hut felt like a dark tiny box now, he could never go back. Yes, his daughter would be much happier here than in his old wooden shack.

It took him all night, however, to walk up and down the stairs. To reach every room, to find all the chairs. It was so very tiring, he had to say, that he couldn't see himself doing this every day.

He didn't _need_ all that space, maybe. Perhaps a smaller home would do, especially for a baby. And so he sailed back to the first hut he found. But as he arrived, he was startled by a frightening sound.

Right next door lived a lumberjack. He was sawing trees and hammering planks with a neverending CRACK and a CLACK. The loud noises scared him so that he almost fell in the water. Surely, he thought, noise like that won't be good for his daughter!

Maybe the height of the hut isn't important, said his inner voice. Maybe it's better to find a place with less noise ...
