# My First Words

**Advances:** [my-first-letters-i, my-first-letters-ii, my-first-letters-iii, my-first-letters-iv]

**Description**:
Once you know a few symbols of the alphabet
How they sound and perhaps also how they connect
You can start forming words, ones of the simplest kind
These resources help learn and memorize all words to mind

**Extra:**
This resource takes knowledge of individual alphabet letters, and perhaps a few syllables already, and advances that to reach recognition of _words_. Children are able to recognize "this is a word" (or, more generally, "this is a standalone chunk of a sentence") surprisingly early. Attaching meaning and reading/writing the actual word are, of course, a much bigger leap. 

This resource aims to bridge that gap. Once satisfied with this, we recommend continuing to _My First Stories_. This _applies_ the vocabulary knowledge in the practical and curious context of stories, which is a far more effective way to learn. (Once someone has a somewhat solid understanding of the alphabet, the general recommendation for improving their command of language has always been to _read, read, read, and read even more (diverse) stories_.)

**Contents**:
* **REBUSES! OF COURSE!**
* Just a sheet with images and their words beneath.
  * => A more advanced sheet where you have to _connect_ the words, or fill in one missing letter.
* Teach **Vowels (long/short) + Consonants, Blends, Digraphs, Magical Letter E (silent after consonant at end; vowel before is always long; bake)**
* **Draw around the blend/word** something that contains those letters. (So you get the empty blend letters as starting point for a drawing.)
* Have a wizard conjure some spell, but the end result is empty. Teach that "if you say the word, the object becomes it" (so the COW spell creates a COW). 
  * Which can later be extended to "if you say the word in CAPITALS, or with a ! after it, etcetera".
* Maybe structure it like a "fight"? Two groups of words/blends before each other. Eliminate options until you can create a word from one group.
* Gestalt Learning => something that presents letters in a wider context/bigger chunks, asking the parent to use them in that larger format too? => This is just what STORIES do, is it not?
* Give two images. => ISN'T THIS JUST THE ALPHAKEY PUZZLE, BUT WITH IMAGES?
  * They must find two matching words, where the last letter of the first word = first letter of second word.
  * Or they must find two words starting/ending with the same sound. (Maybe RHYME?)
  * => Can be extended to even more image, maybe a CROSS of them in the center
* **Word Wheels**: cut out several circles with different word "endings". The top circle has the beginning + a hole (so only one is visible at a time). Now you can rotate through words, which can be used to give quiz answers or communicate or whatever, by simply rotating that wheel. (Might want a pin or something through the center).
  * https://www.superteacherworksheets.com/phonics-word-wheels.html
  * Of course, can also be done in reverse: top part has a word ENDING, rotating parts have START
  * In general, they work a lot with **word families**. Because it allows associating words through the same sounds, it allows teaching a lot of words at once (knowing only a few letters/sounds).

**Advanced Spells:**
* (Explorers)
  * The Names of Nick => About coming up with funny nicknames for little heroes in a story or something
* (Quizmasters) Wordy Birdy (Name first/last word in sentence, Distinguish Words in general)
* (Puzzlepages) 
  * ANA GRANNY( = Anagrams themed with some cozy grandma or something) => Does this make sense yet if they can't WRITE? IDEA: Cut out all letters once, then the puzzles just give you an _order_ in which to put them + a _hint_ at what has to come out!
  * Word Search With A Twist (parts of the word search are empty, you must _fill in_ the letter that should be there to complete it) 
  * CROSSWORDS with IMAGE HINTS 
  * IDEA: "Bookworm (PopCap)" like game, where it's about linking letters to create words in a grid. (Either just to score "as many points as possible", or to reveal some unique solution.)
  * IDEA: "Pigeon Hole Puzzle with Common Blends". That is, add "rules" at the top that say "after an L can only come an E". (Or, more generally, it just gives a list of blends you have learned/are available now.) This allows you to fill in the puzzle and spell words from very barebones beginnings.
  * "Code Breakers" stuff? => Recognizing that certain words are misspelled. Or certain words don't match the image/meaning attached.
* (Runaways) Very simple ciphers, word puzzles, alphabet substitutions, simply writing down the word for an image you see. The "code" at the end is just a simple word like KITCHEN.
* (Gamiverses)
