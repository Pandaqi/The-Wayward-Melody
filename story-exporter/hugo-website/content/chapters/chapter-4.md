# The Song of Birds & Banjos

Comm the pear looked everywhere. He was a living pear, you see, who was expecting the birth of his baby. He had always lived in a terrible place, a small wooden hut with birds screaming in his face. The noise kept him up at night, with their BEEP and their BLEEP. His daughter, he was sure, would want a better place to sleep.

And so he ran away again, his ears wide awake. He listened and he listened, but the first he heard was: "Get out of my lake!"

He'd been so busy listening that he forgot to open his eyes. He'd wandered into some penguins diving into the ice.

"I'm sorry," said Comm, startled at his own loud sound. But then he realized ... it was because of the silence all around. This icy place was far from everything, and it seemed calm and nice. Maybe he'd found his perfect home, right here on the ice.

He stayed with the penguins for a while, visiting some igloos for sale. He made friends with a polar bear and asked questions of a whale.

But when the morning was done, he wasn't satisfied. He kept hearing scratching, like SKSH and KSSH, from the penguins sliding over the ice.

No, that wasn't good enough, still too much sound. So he said farewell and kept searching all around.

He traveled into the mountains, where he was sure no fruit or animal lived. On snowy peaks high in the sky, he discovered he was right! From up here he could see the world below him, see the busy bees, bananas and more. But most importantly, he couldn't _hear_ any of them anymore.

"Yes!" he shouted. "I've found the perfect home!"

A second later, his voice somehow returned with: "Ome---ome---ome---ome."

Surprised, he tried another sound. He yelled: "Up here I'm all alone!"

Soon his voice returned, though softer, with: "Lone---lone---lone---lone."

It was his echo, dear listener, bouncing against the stone. Whatever he said, the mountains would repeat it right back.

It was annoying! He felt like going mad! He soon stopped talking and climbed down the mountains instead.

He visited a city, but everyone was talking too loud. He found a nice road, but too many traveling strawberries and apples were out and about. 

He visited a cozy town, but exactly on the hour they would bang a gong. And when he found a Fruit Villa far away from it all, they had hired musicians to play a banjo song.

He could not find complete silence, only places that sounded unalike. Would his daughter live next to piano players, or would she rather hear fruits driving their bike?

He wanted to cover his ears and go away. And so he ran into the rainforest at the end of the day. High up in the trees, a thousand hidden birds sang their melody. But he couldn't hear most of it, because of the pouring rain falling foreverly.

Maybe, he thought, there will always be some noise. Just like the rainforest will always get wetter. Maybe what his daughter really needs, is a place with much better weather ...
