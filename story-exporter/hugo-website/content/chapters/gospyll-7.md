# The Gospyll According To Timothy

Welcome. This is my Gospel. Or however you spyll it. I like telling stories. This is good story. I am hero, you will see. You can thank me for holidays. And candy.

It all started with the death of my mentor, Paulo. He was burned by Emperor Empero, one of his final acts. Very sad. By that time, he'd already written thirteen important letters. Krystianity had grown so much that large parts of the Amor Empire were accepting it now.

When I heard, I obviously yelled: "This calls for a celebration!"

"Celebrate that your teacher is dead?" asked Timothy's mother in disbelief. She was a monkey, like me. Animals within the same family tend to be the same species. Just thought I'd explain.

"No, like, _remember_ him. In celebration." I tapped my chin and looked around. "Hmm. We could make a _holiday_ out of this!"

"Oh, please, we already have enough holidays," said his father.

"One can never have enough holidays." 

Just then, however, the final letter from Paulo arrived. His final words to me and my town. He knew he was probably about to die. So the letter contained the largest wisdom of all.

_You are on your own now, Timothy. Continue the spread of Krystianity and its important rituals. But remember life can't just be a string of holidays. If you're always eating candy, it will stop tasting nice after a while. If you're always celebrating, it will stop feeling special. Most of all, Krystina led a simple life, and it's a simple life in which you shall be happy._

I threw the letter into the Serpentine River, as I crossed the wooden bridge. Confusing language. Weird advice. Timothy knows best!

I talked about it with friends.

"You're right. Silly advice," said one of my monkey friends. "So we need to be _sad_ and _angry_ half the time? So we can be _happy_ during holidays?"

"No, like, we should first _suffer_, like Krystina suffered," I said. "Then we can have a holiday as the _reward_."

"We have to _die_ first, every year?"

"No, not that part."

"We have to come back to life, every year?"

"Not that part either."

"We have to learn magic tricks like her? They say she could move mountains without breaking a sweat!"

"Don't think they expect us to do that part either."

All monkeys sighed and grabbed their wooden sticks in frustration. "Then _which part_ of her suffering must we copy?"

I remembered the Gospyll of Lent. How Krystina spent forty days in the desert, fasting---which means eating big no no. That seemed a good idea.

"I announce a new ritual!" yelled I.

They all rolled their monkey eyes. "Here we go again."

"Before Passover," I said, "we shall fast for forty days! Our minds shall be with Krystina, not with food---"

"You're mad!"

"I can't go two hours without food!"

"Yes, like, that's why it's _suffering_, dummies." I stole someone's stick and wrote my new ideas into the damp Serpentine River banks.

"But won't we _die_ without food?"

"You raise a good point there."

I tapped my chin. Always do that when I want to look like I'm thinking deeply. I looked around and saw sheep grazing our fields, cows being milked, and chickens laying eggs. This new invention of _animal domestication_ was really nice. We monkeys barely had to move anymore, and we had all the food we'd ever want.

Maybe Paulo was right. Not enough suffering. Which means not enough faith and devotion to Krystina. Which means ... 

I shivered at the thought. It would mean that, the second the Second Coming would come, I would be denied entry to heaven! 

Thus I added a stipulation to my new ritual. "Stipulation" is a biggie word Paulo taught me that means "addition". Because saying that I _added an addition_ feels very silly.

"We shall eat no _animal products_ for forty days, leading up to Passover!"

"What does that _mean_?"

"Like, no animals."

"But we barely eat animal meat as it is. We want the animals alive to produce our milk and eggs. We mostly eat fish."

"Then it means anything that _came_ from an animal too."

"_What!?_"

By now, the entire town was listening to this conversation. I became nervous. But I felt this was the right ritual. 

"You heard it right. We should not eat any eggs, or milk, or meat, for forty days. And it shall be called Lent. And it will prove our devotion and faith!"

All my friends responded by saying how much they hated eating only nuts and berries. 

The adults just returned from another day of fishing. They had a different view.

"Well ... I think we can all agree ... that fish is _not_ meat, right?"

"What? Of course it's meat," I said. "It's the body of an animal."

The Serpentine River started to flood and washed away my words. So I looked around for a clay tablet to make my ideas permanent. 

"But, you know," tried the adults, "is it _really_?"

"Yes?"

"No, no, I don't think eating fish should be forbidden."

"Indeed," added another adult ape, "fish are clearly not meat. They are, erm, fake meat. A test from the devil."

"Yes, I mean, how long have fish been around? Forever, I say! They were there before monkeys, before any other life."

"Then it is decided," they said. And they forced me to change the final line of my clay tablet. Which is annoying. Do you have any idea how hard it is to punch letters into hardening clay? "Fish meat is not meat."

And so it was that Krystians started fasting for forty days. But only in a way that meant they didn't really have to suffer. 

They were slightly annoyed they couldn't eat scrambled eggs for breakfast anymore. Or chocolate milk, a new invention of which nobody knew the origin. Besides that, they kept eating what they were eating anyway. Only now they told themselves they were suffering and showing their faith.

That was when "The Egg Problem" started.

___

The first time we celebrated Lent, one of the disciples visited our town. Bartholo the Ape. Many of his nephews and cousins lived here anyway. But he heard about our rituals and wanted to see it for himself.

All that he saw were baskets and baskets full of eggs. Like a city wall made of eggs! Meant to keep out intruders. If Amor soldiers were particularly allergic to eggs or had a fear of all things vaguely white and round.

"Bartholo is here!" I yelled. Me and my banana-eating friends ran over the bridge to greet him. "What a wondrous event! This calls for _celebration_!"

"You can't start a holiday inside a holiday, dear Timothy," said Bartholo. He had a long gray beard now. His face was as kind as ever, but sadness about failing Krystina had never left his eyes. 

He'd grown older. Tired from travels, I guess. When I was young, I believed Kina didn't really exist. But Bartholo actually went there and preached the Krystian faith.

"I take that as a challenge," I said.

"Oooh! Timothy challenged an apostle!" said my friend.

"That calls for a celebratio---"

"What is the deal with the eggs?" asked Bartholo.

"They are, like, left over?"

It's strange how nobody saw this coming. Normally, we use and eat many eggs a day. Now, during Lent, we suddenly _stop eating eggs at all_.

The eggs were still coming. It's not like Krystian rituals change reality. But we didn't have a purpose for them anymore. All of Lent, the monkeys were busy weaving baskets---from reed growing in the Serpentine River---just to store all the eggs!

"We should do something with them," said Bartholo. "Eggs, they ... symbolize life and new beginnings. Some even say that Krystina was reborn from an egg. To let them waste away is absolutely unheard of!"

My thinking brain immediately fired up. I had _so_ many ideas for fun holidays and rituals left! I was just waiting for something to apply them to.

"We can turn _egg throwing_ into a sport!" I said.

"Egg _throwing_?" said Bartholo, taken aback. 

"Is _egg kicking_ better?"

"Absolutely not. These eggs are not to be harmed."

"Ugh, like, I _really_ think kicking a round object could become a very popular sport someday." 

I shook my head and proposed my second idea. "We could build statues or sculptures from eggs? Like, like, a statue of Krystina herself! Right in the town square!"

"A fine idea," said Bartholo, delicately stroking his beard. He could do that because monkeys had _fingers_ now, yeah! "But dangerous. What if it falls? What if the wind starts blowing? There's a reason we don't build our homes out of eggs."

The chickens entered the discussion too. "Don't _we_ get a say in this!?"

"They are _our_ eggs!" cackled another.

"I suppose so," I said. "What's your idea?"

"We can sell them to animals who _do_ want to eat them."

"I don't think you will find many," said Bartholo. The old ape allowed himself a smile. "Everyone in these parts has converted to Krystianity. I came here because they're celebrating Lent in the neighbor towns too. You did a great job, Timothy. Now let me help by solving your Egg Problem."

"A compliment from an apostle? That calls for a celebratio---"

Bartholo picked up a few eggs. He walked to the Serpentine river.

For a moment, I thought he would baptize the eggs. That felt silly to me, though it also felt like a great cause for celebration.

But no, he used an old trick to create _paint_. After the Color Wars many centuries ago, which left us with far fewer colors than before, the Painter's Plants escaped under water. You could find them anywhere near a river or lake, but only if you looked for them. By grabbing one of them, and squishing them, a colorful liquid would drip from it.

Bartholo did this expertly. He grabbed a plant and squished it for a line of red paint. He reached deeper into the waters and came back with a line of green paint.

"I had a friend once," said Bartholo sadly. "Another disciple, the kindest being there ever was. Great eye for colors. Loved eggs. Knew all the tricks to make paint. Unfortunately, she disappeared the day Krystina was crucified. Let's start this new ritual in her name."

Then he started painting the eggs. Simple patterns. Zigzags. Spirals. Up, down, left, right, circles, squares, eyes, fingers, platypi, anything he could imagine. Soon he had five eggs that looked beautiful, and magical, and as if they came from a chicken in a technicolor dreamcoat.

"Now us! Now us!" yelled the monkey children.

They all grabbed their own eggs and ran to the river. Bartholo showed them which plants to use, adding more and more colors over time. 

All day long, we decorated these eggs. We created portraits of ourselves. We had a competition for the best drawing of Krystina. We put twenty eggs in a row and depicted the entire story of the Gospylls.

It was a good day.

Soon, we started decorating eggs in _secret_. Just so we could surprise family and friends with a gift. On the final day of Lent, all the towns visited each other and exchanged pretty eggs.

Though, let me say, that _our_ eggs were prettier than _theirs_. Then again, we had more experience. And more paint from the Serpentine River.

The festival of Passover had never been this colorful. We had never shared our food, and my celebrations, with this many different animals. 

It was a good time.

It was also very different from the Passover of old. With all these new rituals, this new story of Krystina, the new dates. One might even stop calling it Passover and turn it into a new feast entirely.

I though about this on Resurrection Day: the Sunday after Good Friday. That's how we called it, for now. I also made some Jurads very angry by suggesting we move the Sabbath from Saturday to Sunday. Yes, it broke, like, thousands of years of tradition. But nothing of importance every happened in Krystina's life on a Saturday!

With my mind wandering, I was painting my hundredth egg. 

It was becoming a real pretty one. Like, prettier than prettiest. I tried all sorts of new colors. I drew things upside-down, calling it "abstract art".

Until, all of a sudden, everyone was screaming. 

Oh. Okay. What? Why? Where am I? Huh? Why is there a duck in my arms?

It took me a while to realize that my tent, and the buildings around it, had mysteriously _exploded_.

___

At first, the village had assumed that our celebratory fireworks had gone off too soon. A fine assumption. We barely understood how those new inventions from Kina worked, and bonfires were everywhere.

But the fireworks were untouched. So they blamed _me_. Can you imagine! I've never done a rebellious thing in my short life! 

The problem was, of course, that I didn't know what had happened either. But the explosion certainly came from _me_. All broken pieces, which included my favorite clay holiday calendar, could be traced back to where I was standing. The explosion happened where I was.

"All I remember is painting an egg," I told them, over and over. We even found the pieces in the wreckage. My beautiful abstract art, using all colors of the rainbow. 

Like a jigsaw puzzle, we reassembled the egg. Of course, a bunch of broken pieces glued together is _not_ the same as an actual egg. Some of the paint was gone too, burned away or scraped off.

Which is why only a _minor_ explosion happened this time. Just enough to set someone's tail on fire and lose a chicken coop.

"This is cause for a celebration!" I yelled.

"No! We don't celebrate explosions!" said mother.

"No, like, I figured it out," I said. I ran around town holding up the broken egg. "If you paint the right things, the egg actually _becomes magical_!"

I had accidentally figured out the magical pattern for "explosion". Accidentally, I used the right colors and lines to create the spell. 

At least, that's what I thought at the moment. I wasn't able to test it again, because my explosion had made one unfortunate victim.

Bartholo the Ape, also the Ape-ostle, was severely injured. 

We placed him in the comfiest bed available. We give him our best food, and our prettiest eggs. But there was nothing to be done. He was dying, we could all see it. We could feel it. We could almost imagine the spirit of Krystina coming down to take him.

His final words to me: _Go and find Astra Buni, the lost disciple. She must know more._

"More about what?" I asked, and "Where do I even start?", but he didn't give further answers.

He stayed with us for several days. Then Bartholo the Ape was gone. And it was my fault.

In that moment, I felt like Astra Buni. Overcome by tears and guilt, for leading someone we liked to their death. 

I felt I had no other choice. I had to take over Bartholo's work, and travel the world to find Astra. Maybe she knew why this pattern caused exploding eggs. Maybe she knew _other_ patterns that created _other_ magic.

And now it's time for my obligatory bashing of other Gospylls. I was looking forward to this!

The Gospyll of Saulo says that Krystina was a ghost. His panda paws went straight through her body as he tried to touch her. I find this hard to believe, but I think I understand. In his final days, Bartholo the Ape was thin, and frail, as if he hadn't eaten in ten years. He was sick, and old, and his body done. In that moment, touching his forehead, or grabbing his hand, almost felt _like_ touching a ghost.

I think Gospyll of Saulo is good story. But his talk of Second Coming was wrong. The text didn't say it was _this generation_. He mistranslated the old word _genea_, written in the language of the old gods! The correct translation is _family_ or _heritage_! 

When Second Coming comes, all those who had faith will remain, and all evil will be purged. We all agree on that, right? But _when_ this will happen ... nobody knows. I think it just means that Krystina will appear first to her _family_. Or the next messiah is someone _from her family_.

Saulo made many animals believe they would soon be saved. That they should endure their suffering, for Krystina would soon come again. But when she didn't, and everyone waited for generations, this all fell apart. Krystianity stopped being _one_ idea. It split into _multiple_ different ideas about what Krystina and her words meant. Some were, understandably, done with being treated badly and took matters into their own paws.

If only Paulo had translated the words correctly ...

Like that, his talk about how my grades plummeted are a _lie_. In these days, dear reader, some big system of education didn't exist. If you were lucky, and rich, you had a mentor or personal teacher. They'd stay with you as you grew up and teach you all sorts of things. If not, you simply started working as a child and never stopped. That's why most never learned to read or write. That's why the message of Krystina had to be spread by the disciples, who traveled to all the continents and _spoke_ the Gospyll to large crowds.

So ha! Grades can't get worse if you don't get graded at all!

In the years that followed, I didn't dare paint more eggs. Half the town believed my story and stopped painting too. The other half said it was nonsense and that I had caused the explosion some other way. I decided to stay _very far away_ from them during their painting sessions. You don't want to die a silly death because someone painted a green-red triangle on the bottom of an egg and caused your roof to collapse. Or something like that.

Once I was old enough, I left my town to spread the Gospyll and search for Astra Buni. The day I left turned into a day of celebration for my town. I don't know how I feel about that.

Goodbye. This was my Gospyll. I spell better now. Hope you liked story. I don't know whose turn it is next to write Gospyll. Why are we even taking turns?

Hmm. Starting a new Gospyll. That calls for a celebration!
