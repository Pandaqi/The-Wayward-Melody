---
title: "Something Has To Change"
---

I started this year (2025) with an ambitious plan and a lot of energy behind it. Or, at least, I tried to.

I knew I had to start making money from my creative work, instead of giving everything away for free or making things that aren't commercially viable. I knew I had to take some next steps. I knew something had to change.

And so I pulled the trigger on creating an online store. After years of making websites, selling things online (via other means, such as self-publishing books on Amazon), and improving my skills, I could craft quite a compelling plan.

The plan could be summarized as follows. Obviously, it's far more detailed and involved, but this is the gist of it.

1. A few weeks of _getting experience_ running an online store by launching one (using free, existing systems) on my old blog domain that I still had anyway.
2. A few weeks of coding to _build_ a custom website for the web shop + integrations with my existing websites.
3. A few weeks of _moving_ existing things I made to that shop.
4. A few months of _creating_ new products for the shop. These products would be puzzles, quizzes, escape rooms, etcetera.

Step 1 wasn't without its difficulties, but I made it through. That old domain has a functioning, validated webshop with ~80 diverse merchandise products.

Step 2 happened quite easily too, thanks to the lessons learned in Step 1. It showed me what I actually needed (both to comply with laws/policies, and to improve user experience), and I simply built those systems myself.

Step 3 felt a bit pointless as I was doing it, but I finished it in only a single week. I have a habit of nicely "archiving" all my old projects/source files anyway, so most of them could be copied to the store and that was that. Only the oldest projects needed some massive clean-up (renaming things, removing private files that snuck in there, compressing more, etcetera).

And then step 4 ...

By now, it's end of January. With my ironclad discipline and good habits, I started the year 2025 as I set out to do, and struggled through the steps as I planned it. Test store done. Custom store programmed and ready to be filled and launched.

My plan for the contents also became much stronger (... and bigger) in that time. I realized how to structure things, by returning to the thing that has always been my area of interest: _education_. Instead of blindly creating an escape room here, a puzzle there, and so forth, I wanted to actually create a _curriculum_. And for every _topic_, I'd create those game-like experiences to _show_ how much more fun and effective they are at teaching it.

And so a list of miscellaneous ideas became folders and files that roughly follow what's taught in primary school. Except, of course, I leave out the things that science has proven to be useless, and move to things that work better. Sometimes, our current curriculae are doing quite well, and I didn't see much to _change_. In those cases, I'd only _add_: explain alphabet through a puzzle, or a quiz, or magical fairy tales, instead of merely worksheets or "educational activities".

But this process has been _so tiring_. I've never had much motivation for anything to begin with, but now it's dropped below zero. I can't remember really liking anything or looking forward to working at any moment the past ten years, but at least I was still able to shrug that off and _do stuff_. 

At the end of 2024, I had so much _energy_ behind my ideas for escape rooms and puzzles, behind my idea of finally creating that store. But somehow ... somehow, when actual money gets involved, my motivation is just gone. When I actually create things with the intent of selling them---things that I know have a good chance of actually selling well---I just _can't_. I'm so incredibly _not interested_ in money, or success, or anything related to it, that it's actively damaging for me to work that way.

And so, literally on the first day of February, I get sick. Of course, it's the flu going around, it's cold (in a home that hasn't had heating for two years now ... or windows ...), it's not strange to get sick. But I stay sick. I keep being too tired to work. And when I do regain some energy, I'm so overwhelmed by the hatred of making things to sell in a store, that I can't get myself to do anything.

February flies by, a month lost. March flies by, a month lost again. 

I take tiny steps left and right, but it's a negligible pace. I finish some programming systems, clean up my computer, write some articles and accompanying texts for the online store, anything to _not_ have to do the meat of the work.

Okay, I think. Something has to change. Resting, waiting, letting my body "recharge", it's not doing anything.

And so, after all those years, saving money and checking out the best deals, I finally buy a new computer. Finally I can do more than wait 15 minutes for my laptop to boot, then write and hope text editor doesn't crash.

The computer arrives. It is, indeed, much much faster and allows me to finally draw stuff myself, record music again, etcetera. It's also faulty---just my luck!---but I send myself all the positive thoughts in the world and use it for a few more days to at least get stuff done.

{{% remark %}}
What was wrong with the PC? The Wi-Fi randomly cut out completely. One of the USB-ports was ... inconsistent in speed/quality/sustaining connection. But most of all, it made that typical coil whine noise. Which is just a soft beep sometimes, but quickly turns into a really annoying, distracting, high-pitched screech once you work with such a computer for an entire day. Having quite some knowledge about computers, I tried everything I could think of to debug the issues, but couldn't achieve much.
{{% /remark %}}

I _do_ get stuff done, with the new pc. _One_ set of products, around one topic, for the online store, is done in a few days. And quite good, if I say so myself.

And then I even receive good news: I participated in a writing competition and the publisher is interested enough in my work to send me a contract! That's the dream of every author, right? Your work is recognized, first and foremost. And then you actually get traditionally published (as in, well, anything that's not self-publishing). And what do I feel? _Nothing_. I just don't care for any of it, but I _know_---logically---that I need money to survive and some successes to advance my career. 

The moment this "great event" happens, my motivation only evaporates _even more_. The momentum I had from creating that one bundle of educational materials completely disappears, because ... I am successful? Because I have a good computer and nothing is really standing in my way?

After all those years, this is really the only conclusion I dare draw. Something in me is almost disgusted by money, and career, and "the grind", and being successful. As I grow in skill, as I have more success left and right, it has only made me hate my work and lose that final ounce of motivation. I am actually selling copies of my books now---and I have written less the past 6 months than I have done ever before. I am actually getting serious visitors on Pandaqi now---and I have made fewer new (board) games for it in the past 6 months than I have done ever before.

And so, I conclude, continuing in the same way isn't a great plan. 

From an outside perspective, my life the past ~10 years would _mostly_ look quite healthy and well-organized. I've always maintained great habits around physical and mental health. I've done some outside work and hit those deadlines, while having room for personal stuff. I mean, writing 10 books a year when you don't even have motivation to get out of bed is surely an achievement, right? For all I've accomplished, for how fit I am, my current habits and lifestyle are surely a good thing, right?

But it's a superficial level. It's measuring someone's condition based on those same factors I despise, such as "success" and "productivity". Because, if we're honest, I've really just been standing in a small dark room, alone, toiling away at projects I don't even care about myself. I can't remember working and not being so cold it distracts me endlessly. I can't remember working in a space that actually has proper (natural) light and doesn't feel like a prison cell. 

I can't remember working and actually liking it.

I've talked about this several times before. The way I was treated when I was young, the way I was forced through the schooling system I hate so much, it has basically made me lose all interest in life. For the past ten years, I have never really liked something, or wanted something, or felt motivated to do something, or felt ... happy. And I know that. I sought professional help, I sought ways to change it, but nothing worked. I was already doing the right things; I always had. The therapist couldn't really offer me anything more, or think of other suggestions.

So what do you do? What do you do when nothing actually feels rewarding? When you never _want_ to do anything? And when you are "successful", you push through and manage to do good work anyway, you aren't _satisfied_ with that at all?

The best way to understand it, really, is just to imagine that the ability to "feel good" is just missing for me. Like, every time you think "I want X" or "I like Y", just replace those thoughts/feelings with _nothing_. Not even negative or self-deprecating thoughts---my mind-set is too disciplined for that!---just a big _"no comment"_ from my brain on everything. I don't even think "my books are shit", I don't think about them _at all_, because I can't find the energy or motivation to care.

So, returning to my "work routine", I tried a few more things. Pretty sensible things, I think. But deep in my heart I already knew they were insufficient bandaids at best.

* Buying that new computer, as stated. For which I also bought a small drawing tablet and some other minor accessoires I needed for my work (and have needed for a looong time). But no, that typical "honeymoon phase" you sometimes have when buying a new shiny product did not happen. Of course, the fact it was slightly faulty didn't help, but still.
* Instead of creating my "products" from start to finish (first create all material for grade 1, then grade 2, and so forth), just pick and choose what I feel like doing in the moment. Unfortunately, I have too much experience with long-term projects to even _attempt_ this anymore. Before you know it, 6 months have gone by, and you have made 5% of everything, and nothing is finished, and now it will never be finished and you quit :p Doing things in a sensible order, and finishing A before starting with B, is _absolutely_ the way to go.
* Slow down on the physical exercise. Yes, I have a chronic physical illness and need discipline to keep it at bay. But do I _really_ need to run 5 miles every morning? Switching to once every 2 or 3 days is also fine.
* Create a "TO-DO LIST OF NON-WORK". This was actually my best idea on the list. Instead of merely planning my work to do every day, I would also plan my non-work. In other words, if I force myself to write for 1 hour, shouldn't I also force myself to sit in a comfortable chair and watch a nice TV show for 1 hour in the evening? I created a list of series I wanted to watch, a list of games I wanted to play, and forced myself to relax a bit every day.

These things might have worked long ago. But now I just found myself playing games or fiddling with an expensive new computer, not even enjoying _that_, and then realizing I'd achieved nothing again this week. I was falling behind any sensible deadline. And I could only launch the store once I had enough to sell, because the high costs of running it would have to be recouped.

So, again, I ask myself: what to do?

I need a much bigger change. And I need to go back to basics, to the few things that actually matter to every human, if we're honest.

I returned the computer, first of all. (It was well within that 30 day return period. Otherwise I'd have claimed warranty because it was faulty anyway.) I waited a bit, to make sure I received the money, then bought a new one. This one worked. (@TODO!)

I decided to change the scenery. There are some libraries and other public spaces around town where I can sit and work (for free). I threw my old laptop in my backpack and went to random such places without a plan. I was always hesitant to do this because I knew it would take extra effort and distract me endlessly. I _knew_ I'd get much less work done when in a foreign location with stuff happening around me. 

But, well, I now realized that "50% of usual productivity" is still better than "0%", which had now been the case for two months already. And cycling/running to those locations brought some minor exercise as well, so that's an extra benefit.

And I asked myself the question: _why am I in such a hurry?_ It always seemed like a paradox to me. At the same time, I both have absolutely no motivation to do anything, as well as feeling like I need to get mountains of work done _before yesterday_. At once, I feel like anything I do moves too fast _and_ too slow. It's both too exhausting _and_ keeps me restless and impatient.

I think I know the answer now. I have seen and felt, since I was ~11 years old, my body deteriorating. I have felt, since I was ~7 years old, that all I really care about is finding a lovely girl and leading a simple family life. Nothing else ever made me feel anything.

My friends/family laugh at me when I say I'm getting old. When I say I'm getting to an age where I should really find someone and start a family, otherwise it's just too late. They frown because I have just turned "only" 28. And because of a lifetime of good physical habits, or just lucky genes in that regard, strangers regularly approach me believing I'm a boy still in high school.

But when you combine that fact with my chronic illness, the way my body and brain are absolutely not getting better, you hopefully see why it's a ticking time bomb. Frankly, I am not certain if I'll be able to live independently anymore in ~5 years time. I am not certain I'll be able to use my brain in any meaningful way anymore in ~5 years time. I have always known this, with the proof right in front of me. No matter how hard I work, my body can destroy itself even faster.

I have realized, I guess, that I can't outwork this. I will never be able to get all my ideas made. I will never be able to work so hard that my chronic illness is fixed forever, or that I can impress the ladies with sheer skill and might (if that ever mattered at all ...), or that I will achieve riches or whatever. 

And so I just had to stop being in a hurry. "Losing" an hour or two every day to travel to a random library is fine. Only getting 30 minutes of meaningful work done in that place is fine. Delaying the launch of that online store by a year is fine, if that's what it comes to. I don't _have_ to release 10 books, 25 board games, and more in a year.

All that matters is getting new experiences every day. Getting away from the single place and routine in which I made stuff all those years. Maybe meeting someone new. Maybe realizing you can still write books when you're fifty, but starting a family is a bit harder then. Maybe my unlucky body and brain just weren't made for consistent work output, or for working behind a computer screen at all. And maybe being disgusted by getting paid for something, or reaching even an inkling of "success", is something I just have to unlearn as it brings me nothing now.

No, this is not a story that ends with "and everyone lived happily ever after, being more productive than ever before". Because that just isn't the goal anymore.

Before these changes, I achieved practically nothing over the course of 60 days of _trying really hard and feeling really bad about doing nothing_. After these changes, I got _some stuff done_, and I visited some new places and discovered some new things.

That's all.
