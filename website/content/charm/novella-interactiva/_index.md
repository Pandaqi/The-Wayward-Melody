{{% double-view-item %}}

A Novella Interactive is an **interactive story**. It means the story is _not_ a single text meant to be read from start to finish. 

Maybe the story is read _to_ children and regularly asks you to **pause and ask a question**. Or maybe it's a **"choice story"**, where you must skip to a different page number depending on the choices you make while reading. Or maybe it's something even more, turning a book into half a game.

{{% /double-view-item %}}

{{% double-view-item %}}

{{% section-foldable header="Why would I be interested?" %}}

Below are some of the biggest advantages to interactive stories.

* This kind of product **bridges the gap between pre-readers and readers**. It's a way to cause and keep excitement for stories. It's not _just_ a story ... there are choices, and interactivity, and other elements to make it a little bit more. Even if a child struggles with reading the story, there are those _other elements_ to help out and keep them going.
* Similarly, it bridges the gap between **non-readers and readers**. There are many people who convinced themselves they hate reading (or are "not smart enough" for it). I know several of them---and I also know they suddenly loved reading once they discovered interactive novels. They were a stepping stone. They were "their kind" of novel.
* It requires stories to be **short and streamlined**. You just don't have the space to make the story complicated or too long. An interactive story needs to be streamlined and tight, with no wasted word or plot.
* It allows narratives, stories and emotional events that **linear/regular stories can't match**.

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="Why would I not be interested?" %}}

Below are some of the biggest disadvantages of interactive stories.

* The interactive element can sometimes be perceived as **annoying or useless**. If someone is really into the story, stopping the story for some choice or question feels like breaking their flow and immersion.
* It **requires a bit more from the reader**. Be it the parent reading _to_ their children, or the child reading the book themselves.
* The plot is usually not as engaging and well-woven as in a linear novel. It **has to be chopped up and simplified** more to _allow_ you to read the book in different ways or make choices along the way (without the whole thing collapsing).

{{% /section-foldable %}}

{{% section-foldable header="How is the product delivered?" %}}

You always get at least two things:

* The story as a **printable PDF**. (The pages are the usual format for books. Your computer should support printing "2 pages per paper" too, creating a booklet if you want.)
* The story as an **ebook**. (We keep the ebook lean and simple on purpose, to make sure it _should_ load on any ereader you might ever find.)

We try to include the "interactive elements" within the book itself. It's nice to have it all in one place at the start. To really make it one _book_, not a collection of PDF files. But ... this isn't always possible. And in such cases there will be a separate PDF with "explanation and material".

On top of that, many of the educational stories include **bingo cards** or **quizzes** too. To keep kids engaged, to make sure they understand.
