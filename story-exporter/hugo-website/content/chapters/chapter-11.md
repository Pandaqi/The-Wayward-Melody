# Wrong Side of the Rainbow

Commander Leyla was the youngest commander of Rainbow Soldiers ever. So you might not believe me when I tell you there was a time she was even younger. A girl maybe 3 or 4 years old. Her family was poor. Everyone was poor, really, living in the Kingdom of Ice Dragons. 

Except the dragons; they had all the money, and food, and ice. They'd stay in their caves all year, doing nothing but demanding everyone bring them tasty food and gold coins. And once in a while, people would be sick of it. They would refuse. They would tell the dragon to do it themselves.

Every time that happened, the dragon would come out of their cave. And he'd show them who was the boss. A single dragon teeth is larger than a human. And when your home is made of ice, a fire-breathing dragon is the last visitor it wants. 

Needless to say, those who refused didn't refuse for very long.

Leyla had waited all day for father to return from "visiting the Ice Dragon". He'd taken what little food they had. So Leyla's stomach growled. She licked the ice to soothe it. Well, nothing to do then. She had to save energy. She'd go to bed---even though she was too hungry to sleep.

Until finally, her father's beard prickled her awake. He hung over her with a wide grin.

"Eat, dear. Eat!" he said.

Leyla looked to the side. Piles and piles of food were scattered around the kitchen. They had no refrigerator, of course, because their home _was_ the refrigerator.

"How ... how?"

"I pretended to give the dragon food," he whispered. "But instead I stole what the others had already given! The dragon never noticed! He was too busy---"

"Stealing is bad," said Layla.

"Eat first. Then make up your opinion."

"My opinion doesn't matter. The rules say stealing is bad. You stole."

"But ... dear?" Father helped Leyla to the food, confused. "Are you not happy?" 

Leyla refused to touch any of it. No matter how hard her stomach growled. No matter how her fingers itched and her tongue twisted.

"I'm telling the dragon. Crimes must be punished."

"_What?_"

"That's what you always said, papa," said Leyla coldly. As if she was listing the ingredients in a recipe. Solving a number formula. "Actions have consequences. Rules are there to be followed."

"No, no, that was different," said father. "I just wanted to make sure you behaved. Ate your vegetables. Stopped waking me when I was sleeping."

"Rules are rules," said Leyla. Father couldn't respond to that, because it was _also_ something he had told her many times.

Leyla walked out of the home. As it turns out, she didn't need to walk very far.

The Ice Dragon had come out of his cave. His every footstep shook the earth. His every wingbeat made hurricanes. And his eyes glowed in the dark as they spotted Leyla.

A thunderous voice made several igloos crumble. "Who _DARES_ steal my _FOOD_?"

"My father," said Leyla simply. As if telling a good friend about the members of her family. "That's our home over there."

The Ice Dragon was somewhat surprised by this. "Is this a _TRAP_?"

Leyla found it hard to understand him. He pronounced some words really angrily for some reason, putting emphasis on the wrong syllable. "No. It's not a ... tarp?"

"Then _PREPARE_ to _BURN_!"

"Me or my father?"

"Not you. You helped me. Your _FATHER_!"

The Ice Dragon flew low to the ground. Leyla's home was obviously too small for the beast. But with one eye, he could look through the front door and see the piles of food inside.

Father had already been packing his bags. When the dragon appears, he bolted out the back door.

The Ice Dragon blew a fiery breath. Leyla's home was destroyed, melted to puddles and bones, before she could blink.

She wasn't sure if father got away. Neither was the Ice Dragon. He seemed to think destroying the house was enough punishment.

"Who _ARE_ you, _GIRL_?" he asked.

"I'm Leyla. You just destroyed my home. Can I sleep at your place?"

The Ice Dragon chuckled. A low, slow sound as if stone giants were having a battle overhead. 

"Of _COURSE_," said the dragon. "I _HAVE_ great _PLANS_ for you!"

Leyla quickly became the Ice Dragon's secret protector. She would count all the food and make sure nothing was missing. She would tell the dragon news of what happened in the kingdom. People couldn't even _think_ about fighting the dragon anymore. Leyla would hear about it, and the people were punished before they had done anything.

The other Ice Dragons heard about it too. They tried to find their own protector, but nobody was like Leyla. No human would ever help a dragon. Those who did would often make mistakes. Or they'll ignore the rules when they didn't like them. Not like Leyla. She knew wrong from right. Stealing was wrong. Attacking others, human or dragon, was wrong. 

"But, oh Great Dragonax, you attack people all the time," said Leyla one day.

The dragon chuckled again. He was used to Leyla challenging him. He had learned from her, too. "Only if they attack first. It's called _self-defense_, human girl. If you don't harm a dragon first, they won't harm you."

"But my father didn't harm you. He just stole food."

"Without food, I die. Your father might as well have killed me!"

Leyla nodded. Sounded reasonable enough. Attacking someone in self-defense was _right_. Attacking someone for any other reason was _wrong_. Great. That was settled. 

To Leyla's eyes, the world was clear as ice. Her kingdom looked cyan---light blue---wherever you looked, reflecting the light blue sky. And your face. And all she saw was how _right_ she was. She had never made a mistake. She caught all the thieves, but left people alone who broke no rules.

Until one day another Ice Dragon, Fierce Flyerax, showed up at Great Dragonax' cave. 

"I am here to _TAKE_ your _PROTECTOR_!" he yelled. Leyla went purple as she realized all Ice Dragons spoke in the same way. 

Fierce Flyerax entered the cave without permission. "She _IS_ too _VALUABLE_! Leyla, come work for _ME_! I have _SO_ much more _GOLD_!"

Great Dragonax stayed calm. He looked at Leyla over his shoulder. "That's the problem, Flyerax. She's a good protector. Loyal. She won't leave me."

"Then _YOU_ chose _DEATH_!" yelled Flyerax. He breathed a massive fireball and dove into the cave.

Leyla wouldn't be able to tell you how the fight went. When dragons fight, the world holds its breath. When dragons fight, there are no winners. Most importantly, the very first attack send her flying backwards into the cave, hitting her head.

At some point, Leyla found herself in another part of the ice cave. Apparently there was an attic to the thing. She could see the dragons fight _below her_. Which gave her an idea. She hammered at the ice around her. Long, thin, sharp knives of ice dripping from the ceiling.

Until one final blow sent the whole thing crashing down. A massive rain of ice daggers came down on the fighting dragons. Leyla held onto some rocks to prevent falling with them.

She had killed an Ice Dragon.

"It was self-defense!" she yelled.

"It was _NOT_!" yelled the surviving dragon. "You were _NOT_ part of the _FIGHT_."

Leyla's voice wavered as a dark blue, the color of fear, painted the ice attic. "But my dragon _was_ part of the fight!" 

"You will _DIE_ for this, human _GIRL_!"

___

Leyla stumbled forward, slipping on the ice. Just a few more steps. She'd cross the border and enter another kingdom. One without ice. One that didn't look cyan _everywhere_. One with a rule that said Ice Dragons were not allowed.

A dragon's roar sounded behind her. Close. They were getting close.

They wouldn't dare cross the border, would they? The rules were clear. Ice Dragons were not allowed to leave their kingdom.

Leyla looked back. That was a mistake. The dragon was almost close enough to burn her.

"Hey! You!" yelled several voices. "Go back!"

_Me?_ Leyla looked up. No, not her.

Rainbow Soldiers patrolled the border. They pointed at the Ice Dragon and told the beast to stay away.

For just a moment, the blink of an eye, Leyla felt how silly this was. A massive fire-breathing dragon. And a few humans thought it would listen? That it would stop flying because they said "get back"?

Leyla made a final dash. A blind run towards the border. 

"Come here! Grab my hand!" they yelled towards her. Leyla's hands were frozen. The ice below her reflected the dragon _above her_, a hundred times larger than her tiny body.

Rainbow Soldiers grabbed her arms. Leyla was hoisted on top of a wall, carried through a tower, and then dropped on the other side.

"Thank you, thank you, thank you," she kept whispering.

"Don't thank us yet," said the soldier. He was a young boy, almost a man. His entire uniform was painted black in fear. 

The dragon wasn't stopping.

The dragon crossed the border.

"You _HUMANS_ always _BREAK_ the rules!" it roared. Just the loudness of that voice was enough to make the towers crumble. "We come _TO_ get _REVENGE_!"

"Escape! Flee! Run! Seek cover!" 

Everyone yelled different commands, but they all meant the same thing. They knew they couldn't defeat a dragon. Most of these soldiers were young boys. Most of them had been asleep, or eating, and didn't even have a sword ready.

The dragon beat its wings once, and all the buildings fell apart. It dove towards the nearest city, preparing to breathe fire.

"It's so big ... so big ... so big ..." mumbled a boy, huddled in the corner.

When the dragon set an entire forest ablaze, everyone had already given up. Leyla was lost in a crowd of panicked people. Some ran back home, others ran into Icestripe. The only other option was the Endless Blackness. It was nearby too, as it had swallowed all of Snowstripe, their neighbor kingdom.

The Rainbow Commander arrived. A man with a gray beard and sleepy eyes. He shot a few colors at the dragon, painting it like a clown. But it didn't hurt the beast at all. So the commander told everyone to "keep fighting", then ran away himself.

Leyla stood still like a statue. Rainbow Soldiers bumped into her, but she didn't move. She watched it unfold, painting the floor yellow-green out of guilt. No, it wasn't her fault. The dragons attacked; the humans here had done nothing wrong! It was against the rules. She would _not_ accept it!

The dragon had turned around. It was coming back to the border. She could already see a fireball growing in its chest.

Leyla ran away from everyone else. She made herself a target, standing alone amidst the rubble and burning homes. She stood out like a sore thumb.

The dragon saw her. He chuckled and came straight at her.

A young Rainbow Soldier ran towards her. "Go away! What are you doing? Don't you see the dragon coming for you!?"

She pretended not to hear.

Once the soldier reached her, she grabbed his hand. As loud as she could, she yelled, "Save yourself! Go into the Blackness over there! The dragon will never find us there!"

"B-But ... I was told ..."

"Yes! It's a big secret! The Blackness is a safe hiding place. Please believe me!"

"Well---" The soldier looked up. The dragon was about to set both of them on fire. He gulped, then nodded and ran for the Endless Blackness.

The dragon slowed down, waiting. He watched the brave soldier go. He watched him step into the Blackness. And not come back.

Leyla's plan had worked. She was a genius! All those fools running around her ... they clearly only had half a brain. If everyone would just follow her rules, nothing would ever go wrong again.

The dragon roared, then grinned, showing all his teeth. "Next _TIME_, human girl, don't give _AWAY_ your _SECRETS_!"

"Oh no!" said Leyla even louder. "You heard me! Oh no!"

The Ice Dragon dove down. But not towards Leyla. 

He flew straight at the Endless Blackness.

You've been listening to these stories for a while now, my child. You know what happens next. The Endless Blackness eats everything and takes away its color. Takes away what makes it unique, what made this dragon ... a dragon. At some point, you're spat out again. But who knows what you'll look like? Maybe the dragon was spat out as a black barren tree, or just a gray stone. Nobody will ever know. A dragon went in, and didn't come out. Just like the boy Leyla sent in, lying to him with her frozen heart.

All they know is that Leyla defeated another dragon and saved the kingdom.

The Rainbow Soldiers lifted her on their soldiers. They called her a hero, a savior, a true leader. Their commander was soon forgotten, and they made Leyla their new commander. The story reached all the kingdoms. Schools taught about hero Leyla. They told kids that, if they were brave and worked hard, they could become just like her one day.

Or as Leyla told everyone: "You see? The Rainbow Soldiers are protectors. We fight to save the world. We fight to make sure everyone, including dragons, follows the rules! Whatever my Rainbow Soldiers do, they do to protect you."

___

Another day, another loss. Leyla was tired. The Endless Blackness only grew every day. It was in almost _all_ the kingdoms now, slowly eating everything that lived.

She hadn't seen sunlight in months. She was also close to the Blackness, which cast a huge shadow over everything. If you got too close, you'd usually be caught in a thunderstorm that never ended.

Someone brought a carriage. Not for her, of course. Leyla refused any help and walked everywhere herself. The carriage was filled with many different people. Its windows had been replaced by metal bars, making it a moving prison. A moving prison that was painted a mix of bright yellow and dark blue.

"What did they do?" asked Leyla in a tired voice.

"They were surprised. Or happy. Either way, they painted the whole area yellow, and that is---"

"Forbidden. I know. I made up the law myself."

"And see that red bit on the wheels? Well, some were very angry about being locked up too. Which is also---"

"Forbidden, yes, I know."

"Do we really need to lock them up?" asked the carriage driver. His voice sounded uncertain. He fidgeted with his hat. "Can't we just say they learned their lesson---"

"No. The law is the law." Leyla's bright blue eyes flashed. Her lips trembled. "We can't let people be _surprised_. Happy people are people who are not paying attention. Lock them all up. I don't want to see their rule-breaking faces again."

"We need all the soldiers we can get, Leyla," said the driver.

"Yes!" said a woman inside the carriage. "Let us go! Please let us go back to our families. My two children."

"Please!" pleaded a man next to her. "We promise to stop feeling wrong emotions! We'll never be surprised again!"

"Rules are rules," said Leyla. She turned away again. She still had to draw up a plan for how they were going to attack the Endless Blackness tomorrow.

"But rules can change," said the driver. "_You_ keep changing them. What emotion will be forbidden tomorrow? Nobody knows!"

Leyla frowned as she turned back around. Who was this driver? Why did he think he could speak to her like that? There must be a rule against that. Tomorrow, she'd invent a rule that said nobody was allowed to ask Leyla too many questions. Would save her a lot of time, too. Great idea.

"Go home, driver," said Leyla. "I'll bring the carriage to prison myself."

"I'm the driver. Go home yourself."

How _dare_ he? Talk like that? Be mean to her? Who was this ... boy?

Then she noticed the colors around him. The driver painted half the carriage cyan. A blue that seemed extra bright compared to the dark blue of sadness. He had painted the entire unicorn he sat on too.

Leyla had always wondered what the color meant. It appeared around her _a lot_. She assumed it was because she was born in Icestripe. That she had cold hands, or something. But this was the first time she saw someone else paint with the color too.

What feeling created it? 

Driving a carriage? No, that wasn't a feeling. Wearing a hat? Also not a feeling, and something Leyla rarely did anyway. Being annoying? No, obviously not. Or Leyla would have to admit she was annoying all the time too.

"Do I need to lock you up too?" asked Leyla.

"You have no right. I broke no rules. Like these people, I did nothing wrong."

"Who---have we met before?" asked Leyla, trying to come closer.

Ugh. This driver was _so_ stubborn. Why didn't he just listen? Why did he keep going on about rulebreakers not being rulebreakes? Leyla was too tired for this, and---

"No. But you might have met one of my friends."

Leyla's heart raced. She turned around. She was alone, in the dark, and this driver was _not_ friendly.

"SOLDIERS! HELP!" cried Leyla. Footsteps sounded in the distance. Rainbow Soldiers were coming to save her any second.

"Good. Good, good, good," said the driver. 

He stepped down from his unicorn and lifted his hat. He looked quite friendly and extended his hand.

"My name is Molmo," he said in a posh voice. "I'm the prince of Sunstripe. Glad to make your acquintance."

"I'm ... glad to meet you too?" Leyla was more confused than ever. Was he on her side or not?

By now, many Rainbow Soldiers surrounded them. They looked confused too. Leyla had just called for help with ... shaking the hand of the richest prince in the world.

"Pretend nothing's wrong," whispered Molmo. Only Leyla could hear, not the Rainbow Soldiers. "In three seconds, that carriage lock is going to explode. Everyone is going to escape. You're going to look silly."

Leyla's eyes flashed ice-cold cyan again. Her handshake became so strong that she almost broke Molmo's hand. Her arms painted themselves red in anger.

"You---"

"No, don't try to prevent it," whispered Molmo. "I have powerful friends, including someone who can paint Piqarine. We've surrounded you're surrounding."

"I---"

"Well," said Molmo with a bright smile. "I look forward to working together with you---"

Leyla moved back her hand and _smacked_ Molmo in the face. Molmo fell down. He cried and touched his jaw to see if any of it survived.

A bright red circle erupted around Leyla, painting her anger on nearby soldiers.

The carriage suddenly fell apart. Not an explosion---that was a lie---but Tomsy silently opened the door from behind. Everyone inside the carriage left it, in absolute silence. One by one, tiptoeing on the grass.

The Rainbow Soldiers didn't even notice. 

They were busy arresting Leyla for attacking Molmo---which was against the law---and being angry---which was against the law.

"Get off of me!" yelled Leyla. "That was self-defense!"

"We all saw it," said an older man. "You hit a prince who was being friendly."

"He was not! He was about to attack me!"

"Sure."

The soldiers put colorful handcuffs around Leyla's wrists. They dragged her away, while she kept screaming they should let her go. 

"Rules are rules," they said.

"Well ... well ... the rules don't apply to _me_! I'm too important!" yelled Leyla. 

As soon as she said it, she knew how bad it sounded. Silly. Arrogant. A young girl throwing a tantrum. 

For the first time in her life, she wanted the rules to _not_ be the rules. 

Unfortunately, it was too late now.

With their commander gone, the Rainbow Soldiers had nobody to wake them up the next day. No plan. No resistance against the Endless Blackness.

And it was as if the monster sensed it. Because the next morning, it moved faster than ever before. It wanted to end the world _today_.
