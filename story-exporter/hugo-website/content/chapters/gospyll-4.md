# The Gospyll According To Astra

To understand the unfair trial of Krystina, we need to understand that crowds are eggstremely silly. A single being, yes, they can be smart. They often are. Especially if they're a bunny with an unusually large appetite for eggs. But put them in groups, and make several groups hate each other, and you have a recipe for disaster. The exact kind of disaster that Krystina wanted to solve.

Previous Gospylls have explained that the Amor Empire attacked and conquered Jerrysalum. This special city belonged to the Jurads before that happened. As such, the Jurads hate Amor and love anyone who fights them. 

Amor, on the other hand, was surprisingly smart. Perhaps that's why they became so big, defeated the godchildren, and lasted for so long. They knew that you couldn't run a kingdom when half your animals were plotting to kill you. They wanted the Jurads to _like_ them.

And that's why Amor followed a Jurad ritual. Each year, during Passover, they had a custom to release _one_ criminal. To "forgive them" for their sins, to give them a second chance. A new beginning, which was the exact thing Passover celebrated.

Instead of bringing Krystina to a secret trial somewhere, they brought her to the public square. The Amor soldiers shoved the platypus around and led her to a low, beige balcony. On the other side, soldiers brought a different prisoner to a similar balcony. The other prisoner was a massive sheep, covered in so many scars that it created magical patterns in their wool. They wore two eye patches as if sunglasses had already been invented. 

A crowd formed, cheering and clapping. The Amor governor, of this province, arrived on a middle balcony between the two.

If Astra hadn't known any better, she'd think a live performance was about to start.

But she _did_ know better, and so did the animals around her.

"I recognize her!" yelled another bunny. "She was with Krystina. She is one of _them_!"

Astra nervously hopped around. Angry Amor soldiers glared at her, and the accusation was drawing attention. She had a mind to flee, but she also wanted to stay at Krystina's trial. Maybe she could still save her best friend. 

Paws, teeth, and tails kept pointing at Astra. They were certain now that she was one of the disciples.

Astra blurted it out: "No! You are wrong! I have never seen this woman before in my life, I swear!"

Despite standing far away, Krystina seemed to hear her. The platypus looked up and shared a sad glance with Astra. She cast her eyes down again, inspecting the brown dirt and orange stones of the public square. 

Astra had to _do something_. She could save Krystina ... if she could only figure out her own magic. She had discovered long ago that she was a demigod too, but of what? So far, she felt most like the Demigod of Nervousness and the Demigod of Gnashing.

Astra fled from the crowds and tried to enter the balconies herself.

Governor Pillar clapped to get attention. Like all Amor elite, he was a wolf. But he was one of the smaller ones, lacking the sharp teeth and weapons others liked to carry around.

"As a gesture of good faith," he said, "we will recognize the Jurad custom to release one criminal during Passover. And we will give a voice to the people, and let _you_ decide."

Cheers. Yelling. Foot stomping. Astra heard it all vaguely as she tried to break into the building from the back.

"To my right is Bar-Bar," continued Pillar. "A freedom fighter, or so you say. He has fought against Amor occupation for years. Killing many of our soldiers, shedding blood unnecessarily. We captured him at last, and only one punishment fits such treason against Amor: crucifixion."

The mention of this punishment sent shock waves through the crowd. Several animals left, shielding their eyes. Others fell to the floor and prayed already. Crucifixion was not just a death sentence, it was an incredibly painful and harsh way to die. 

Astra struggled with the word too. She had to shield her long ears to keep hopping up the stairs to the balcony.

"To my left is Krystina," said Pillar. "She calls herself Queen of the Jurads. She claims she is the daughter of God, the messenger for whom you waited four thousand years. I do not need to remind you, of course, that these are lies. _I_ am king of Jerrysalum and its province, and the gods are dead. But our Amor laws, just as they are, do not punish you for telling lies."

Pillar didn't even dare approach Bar-Bar. But he felt comfortable placing a paw on Krystina's shoulder, and staying with her as he explained, at length, that Krystina had broken no laws.

"I will give you time to decide," said Pillar, and he briefly retreated. 

At that exact point, Astra hopped into the room as well. She had to hop backwards and bent her ears down to hide behind a lime green houseplant.

Pillar talked to some of his soldiers and advisors, including his wife. He was putting on his official clothes for the final verdict: a very impractical long white robe, and a golden sword so heavy he often lost his balance.

"Do you think they'll make the right choice?" he asked.

"They would be insane," responded an advisor, "to crucify someone who broke no laws."

"But Bar-Bar is beloved," said his wife. "They might just set him free instead."

"Then we shan't give them too much time to think about it!" said Pillar. "I don't want to go down in history as the person who crucified the savior. I want to be written into the Gospylls as the one who saved Krystina. Even though I worry about her challenge to my authority."

"_Does_ she challenge it, though?" said his wife. "Krystina has done nothing but _speak_, and tell people to stop fighting us. Bar-Bar is a rebel who actually took action and freed many provinces. I fear ..."

"The Jurads have crucified many _fake Messiahs_ before," said Pillar. "It is a criminal's death, lower than low. They even believe that, if crucified, you won't come back to life on the final Judgement Day. I told the actual _King of the Jurads_ to handle the case, but he didn't want anything to do with it. He didn't want to say it, but his eyes showed he felt the same way as I. This platypus ... she is ... _different_."

The crowd outside had grown alarmingly loud. Pillar hurried up and stepped outside again.

Astra fought with ... herself. She also had to fight back a sneeze and her desire to nervously hop around, but that was the easy part. 

What could she do? Pillar seemed a wise leader, who actually wanted Krystina freed. There was nothing more she could say. Should she trust the animals to do the right thing? Could she trust a crowd to make the smart verdict?

She stayed hidden until everyone was back on the balcony, and didn't say a word.

___


If you have ever been part of a team or a group project, you know how chaotic it can be. When ten people try to talk at once, you can't hear any of them. When ten people have different views, it can feel like speaking in circles, until whoever screams loudest wins.

Now imagine that, but with a crowd of thousands, and a moral dilemma as hard as Bar-Bar versus Krystina.

"She wanted us to pay our taxes to evil Amor!" yelled an unknown voice in the center.

"She is innocent!" responded another.

"She told us to be peaceful and accept Amor brutality!" yelled another voice to the left. "While Bar-Bar fought for us and our freedom!"

"And what has all this fighting brought you, huh?" responded another voice all the way on the right.

"She told us her favorite color was purple---and I _hate_ purple!"

"Please, please, one at a time," tried Pillar. Unsuccessfully. 

"She is a heretic, violating the Jurad commandments!" yelled the first voice again. 

From one of the side balconies, Astra had a clear view of the crowd. She had finally located who had spoken: the High Priest, wearing normal clothing and shielding his face with cloth.

Any time someone yelled something, it would flow through the crowd like a wave. The new information would change a few minds. Some would start to believe it and copy it. And when twenty people around you are convinced of something, it is really hard, even for the most stubborn of animals, to _not_ start believing the same thing.

But then this wave would hit an animal who truly believed something else. And they'd yell a counter argument and bounce the wave back the other way.

"She can perform miracles!" yelled one.

"Well then, let's see her miraculously save herself from crucifixion!" yelled another.

"She is a young platypus who did no wrong!"

"She has terrible taste in clothing!"

"Bar-Bar looks too awesome to die!"

"I like sheep!"

"She has a stupid name!"

"As if Bar-Bar is a normal name!"

"She is a danger to our people and our faith!" yelled the High Priest again, immediately supported by other Jurads strategically scattered in the crowd. Their opinions made the most waves and threatened to swallow the entire crowd.

Astra fidgeted and gnashed. She couldn't hold back anymore, not after hearing all those _lies_.

"She is the kindest animal there ever was and has done no wrong!" she yelled from her hidden balcony.

The crowd fell silent. Pillar turned around. "Did ... did that voice come from inside the building?"

It didn't take long for them to spot Astra. She was a small bunny, yes, but she hadn't yet learned that she could change the color of her fur to blend in.

Astra's words seemed to persuade a few animals in the crowd. They started voting in favor of freeing Krystina again, until the High Priest spoke up.

"It's her again! One of the disciples! If Krystina is to be punished, then that bunny too!"

Astra's breath caught. She could scarcely flee now, in a balcony high off the ground. Amor soldiers were all around her, and came for her now.

"Nonsense! Madness!" pleaded Astra. "I have never heard of Krystina before in my life!"

"Then how do you know she is the _kindest animal that ever was_?"

"I have reliable sources?"

"How did you even get on that balcony?"

"I, erm, live here?"

"Enough!" yelled governor Pillar. He took his sword grip in his mouth---the common way for wolves to use the weapon---and pointed at Bar-Bar. "All in favor of freeing Bar-Bar, let yourselves be heard."

Cheers erupted. Clapping, wing-beating, hoof-stamping. The more civilized animals raised wooden signs that said "FREE BAR-BAR", "JUSTICE FOR BAR-BAR", and "BETTER A REBEL THAN A RABBI".

It was a hurricane of noise, but hardly a great way to count votes. Astra, and Pillar, held out hope that even more noise would be made next.

Pillar pointed his sword the other way, at Krystina. "All in favor of freeing Krystina, let yourselves be heard."

The number of cheers could be counted on two paws. The previous hoof-stamping felt like a powerful earthquake; now it just looked silly. Several bunnies held up signs saying "NOT MY MESSIAH", "BREAK AMOR, NOT BREAD", and "IS THERE A THIRD OPTION?"

The message was clear.

All the good faith and arguments in the world could not battle the stupidity of a crowd, and a few animals who manipulated it.

Pillar sighed. With a dejected look, he declared the result.

"Then it is decided. Bar-Bar will be freed, forgiven for all sins."

He could not even look Krystina in the eye. He asked for a water basin and washed his paws, over and over. As if that would absolve him of guilt. Muttering how the _others_ had decided, not him. How he _had_ to follow the will of the people.

"Krystina ... is sentenced to death by crucifixion."

Krystina sunk to the floor, eyes closed. Bar-Bar, surprisingly, wasn't happy to hear it either, and some say he even tried to get them to change their mind.

Astra couldn't bear to hear it and fled the scene.

___


First of all, let me say that I am very fond of the Bearchitect that wrote the previous Gospyll. He is an eggcelent bear, and he helped me escape Jerrysalum. 

But his Gospyll wasn't entirely correct. Judash did not maniacally cackle and leave Jerrysalum with his payment for betrayal. I know that, because I ran into him.

The black sheep walked in circles, in the living room of his own home. He muttered to himself, kicked the table, and bumped his head against the walls on purpose. Astra had never seen sheep do that before, and it felt as if Judash were trying to punish himself.

"It wasn't supposed to go like this ... it wasn't supposed to go like this ..."

"Judash?" Astra stood next to him, and still he didn't see her.

"Just a warning ... just a warning ..."

"_Judash?_"

The black sheep looked up. But he didn't really see Astra, his eyes foggy and tearful.

"I need to set this right!" he exclaimed, and he ran from the building.

Astra had no trouble hopping after him. Until she noticed where he was going: straight at the most important district in Jerrysalum. The headquarters of the leaders, you might call it today. It contained the public square, it still contained quite a crowd, and Astra had just told herself she never wanted to see it again.

Why was he going there?

"I can set this right!" he said again, pushing aside anyone in his path.

"Set what right?" asked Astra. "This was the will of the crowd, the decision of the law! It's not your fault---"

Astra was young and naïve. A bit restless. A bit magical, too, though she still didn't know what or how. 

Judash showed her the money he received for betraying Krystina. Astra still didn't understand.

He showed her the arrest warrant for Krystina, and his contract with the leaders. Astra still didn't understand.

He walked into the building and demanded an audience with the High Priest. Astra thought she understood---was Judash trying to become a High Priest himself?---then realized she was wrong again.

They refused him. Some foxes tried to push him out the building.

But Judash pushed on, interrupted a meeting, then forcefully _threw_ the bag of silver coins at the High Priest.

"I don't want it anymore!" he said. "Turn it back. Let Krystina go. Take your money and undo everything."

The High Priest carefully collected the money, making sure not to waste a single coin on the floor. Only then they spoke: "It is done. No turning back now."

"You can always get a second chance! You can always be forgiven! Did we not see that this morning? My cousin Bar-Bar is free at last."

"Not for you, Judash," they said, and turned their back. "We thank you for your service. If you want the money back, here it is, but---"

"I don't want your filthy money! I want peace on Somnia! I want my savior back!"

"Then you were foolish to ever speak to me."

"Don't ... don't ..." Judash staggered and searched for support. Astra could barely support him and keep him on his hoofs. "Don't let me be damned for all time."

The foxes pushed them out of the building, successfully this time. Astra and Judash were immediately swallowed by a procession. Countless animals surrounded a wooden cross that struggled to stay upright. Krystina could not be seen, small as she was. Someone else must have been carrying the cross for her.

This is another inaccuracy in the Gospyll of the Bearchitect. The trees missing from Gethsemane weren't all used for the long wooden table. Some of them were used to build the wooden cross in advance. It would normally take days for such a punishment to be executed. But Krystina's cross was ready minutes after the verdict.

Everything happened so much faster than Judash, or Astra, could ever have feared.

"Stop it!" yelled Judash.

"Take that cross away!" yelled Astra, as they fought their way through the crowd.

"There they are again! Two of them now!" yelled a female ape, pointing an accusatory finger. Or, well, dear reader, the body part that would evolve into fingers in a few years time. "They are _still_ trying to prevent justice and save their beloved Krystina. Grab them!"

"Lies!" cried Astra immediately, hopping around to prevent capture. "Krystina is nobody to me, _nobody_! Just a platypus I have never seen before!"

Judash fell silent. He merely shared a final glance with Krystina. Then he used his wool to bounce away any attackers and clear a path for the disciples.

The rooster crowed. 

Many hours too late, I might add, because they were asleep and alarm clocks hadn't been invented yet. It's a tough job being the only rooster in a large city like Jerrysalum. Severely underpaid. 

But Astra suddenly realized that Krystina had been right. She had denied knowing her, and believing in her, three times.

Tears fell down Astra's cheek. 

This meant ... this meant there would _never_ be a place for her in the Heavenly Palace. Never! Like Judash, she felt damned for all time.

Astra fled Jerrysalum. In her sadness, she accidentally changed the color of her fur for the first time. It made her unrecognizable, preventing more questions about whether she was one of the disciples. Whenever she heard animals talk about the events, she would still claim she had never known Krystina, while also saying it was a disgrace.

And they talked about the events, oh that they did. Before they had even found a cross, the other provinces were talking about it. Those outside of the Amor Empire saw it as further proof that Amor was evil and had to be fought. Those inside the Empire, such as Jerrysalum, said it was a victory and the Amor listened to the will of its people.

Astra couldn't bear to hear it, again, and fled from town to town. Until she was so far removed from events that her Gospyll has to end here.

Previous Gospyll writers all claimed they were telling the truth, one hundred percent. They were all lying. But I have told you the _truth_, yes. Not a single lie can be found in my Gospyll, because that would be an injustice to Krystina. I only described what I personally saw or heard. And if anyone wants further proof, they can find me on Astra Island, where I hid away from the world. And design lovely stone sculptures of large heads.

This was the Gospyll according to Astra Buni.
