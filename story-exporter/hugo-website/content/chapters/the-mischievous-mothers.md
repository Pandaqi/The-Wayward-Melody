# The Mischievous Mothers

Once upon a time, a boy came home and had double vision. 

To his left was his mother, standing in the living room. But to his right, in the kitchen, stood his mother too!

"Take off your shoes, Tommy," said Mother One. Tommy started to obey.

"No, keep your shoes on love," said Mother Two. Now Tommy, uncertain, was frozen halfway.

What should he do? A good boy listens to his parents, true. But when he left home earlier today, he had only _one_ mother. It must be a _fake_, yes, he thought of the other.

"Which one of you is my mom?" asked Tommy left and right.

"I am."

"I am."

Both mothers responded to his plight.

Even though both mothers looked the same, Tommy immediately _felt_ which one was real. But he didn't _know_ why, and hugging the wrong mother made him squeal.

"Well don't just stand there," said Mother One, "do your school exercises, dear."

"No, stay there love," said Mother Two, "or you will slip on the wet floor, I fear."

He couldn't do both at once: stay _and_ don't stay. But which one was his real mother? How do you recognize a face?

Well, Tommy thought, his real mother was of small size. Her hair was red and long, and she had large brown eyes.

But then he looked left and right, and saw this didn't help. There were _so many people_ with red hair and brown eyes, he could even include himself!

"What's wrong with you, dear?" asked Mother One. "Come help me wash the dishes."

"You look great, love," said Mother Two. "Come to the kitchen and dine on baked fishes."

Another impossible command, so Tommy did nothing. This made both mothers angry, but where could he begin?

Well, he thought, his real mother often wore pink clothes. She had glasses and a pointy nose.

But then he looked left and right, and almost cried. There were _so many people_ with glasses and pointy noses, this could not make him decide!

"If you don't come _here_ now," said Mother One, "I won't read a bedtime story, boy!"

"If you don't come _here_ now," said Mother Two, "I'm taking away your favorite toy!"

Tommy had to close his eyes; seeing two identical people made his sight blurry. Tommy had to cover his ears; hearing two opposite commands made him worry.

What made him recognize his mother? It wasn't superficial things, like hair, clothes or eyes.

It was the lines around her mouth, the unique way she would smile.

It was the voice like a singer, audible within a mile.

It was the odd way she held her hands, or made perfectly square sandwiches with jam.

All these tiny details, combined, said: this is who I am.

He would recognize his mother anywhere, and now he knew why. He knew which voice to follow, and which one was a lie.

Tommy went into the kitchen and hugged Mother Two. He enjoyed the food, didn't slip, but asked: "What did you do?"

The other mother took off her wig and the fake glasses over her eyes. It was his neighbor's mother! She was simply in disguise!

"We thought it would be a funny joke," she said with a grin. 

"I'm just glad you still recognize me," said Tommy's mother, enjoying her win.

Ever since that day, Tommy never forgot the lesson. He'd think about the tiny things that made him able to recognize a person. 

Until one day, to his delight, he could return the joke. He arrived home with a friend of his, who was dressed in Tommy's cloak. He had his mother worried slightly, but she wasn't fooled, oh no. She simply asked a question only Tommy would know!

And they lived happily ever after, solving every mischievous case. Until mother told him the story of the _Watchers of Face_ ...
