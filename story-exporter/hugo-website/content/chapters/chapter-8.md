# Get Over It

_Round and round the rovers go. Their ship was special, you must know. It had no steering wheel, it was not a submarine. Yet now it was sinking, and underwater it was last seen._

Rovy and Pyra were on a ship. And it was sinking quickly. They had just found a new magical stone, but it did not save them. Down they went, until Rovy had to take a very deep breath ... and go underwater. Pyra held her breath too, and she held Rovy's hand, and together they held onto the ship.

But you probably know, dear listener, that humans can't breathe underwater. They can only hold their breath for so long. And the ship was not changing its mind and going back up.

They were stuck at the end of Cacketoo Cove. There was a wall in front of them; and a large traffic jam behind them. And their breath was running out. Rovy twisted around in a panic. Pyra reluctantly let go of the ship and tried to swim to the surface.

But then the strangest thing happened.

Rovy accidentally opened his mouth underwater. And he could ... breathe?

"Pyra! Try it!"

Pyra was scared to try. But if Rovy could _talk_ underwater, then it was probably safe, right?

She opened her mouth too. And yes, she could breathe. The water stayed away. What kind of magic was this?

The ship landed at the bottom of the ocean. The two children landed on its deck again.

And they saw what kept them safe: a large white bubble around the ship. Like someone blowing gum. If they could blow a bubble that was big enough to contain a _ship_. Inside the bubble, everything was safe. There was air to breathe, and the water was to scared to get close. Some very confused fish tried to get into the bubble too, but bounced away.

Pyra was still holding their new stone. The magical stone they found on a sign in Cacketoo Cove.

She could guess what it did now.

"It makes us go _down_!" she yelled. "The new stone. It _makes_ us go underwater, and it keeps us safe there."

She was about to let go, but Rovy stopped her.

"That's the solution!" he said. "We're still in the cove now. But look: there's nothing in front of us. If we move forwards a bit, and _then_ go up ..."

"We are out of the cove!" said Pyra happily. "We'll end up behind it!"

And so they did. First, they used the _Forwards Stone_ to move forward. There was nothing in their way, except some coral, fishes, and one small shipwreck.

Then, Pyra let go of the _Downwards Stone_. 

Immediately, the ship left the ocean floor. It floated _up_, back to the water surface. It passed some confused fish who would certainly tell their parents all about the strange things they'd seen today.

It broke the surface with a large splash. The bubble around the ship disappeared. Rovy and Pyra stood in the sun, breathing in fresh air. They smiled and hugged each other. For they had, indeed, escaped the cove. They were new behind it, in a part of the ocean they never saw before.

The sun was rising. They had spent an entire night in that traffic jam in the cove. But this was a good thing, because they had an entire day ahead of them to find the treasure. And, they could see much further than at night.

What they saw was a ring of islands. They stopped moving to make sure they didn't run aground. _Run aground_ is a phrase that means you sail a ship into land so quickly that it gets stuck, like a knife in butter. A very big knife in very big sandy butter.

Where could they go? Their ship didn't have wheels. It couldn't drive over land, like a bike or a car.

They tried going underwater again. By holding the _Downwards Stone_, they sank and confused even more fishes. This time, though, it wasn't the solution. 

Islands, dear listener, are just hills and mountains. Hills that happen to be higher than the water surface. It's not like there's empty space below islands for a ship to sail through. It's just more land, more dirt, more stone.

So, they let go of the _Downwards Stone_ again. They let their ship float back up. And then, as they stood in the morning sun, they saw a cart.

"Let's take that!" said Rovy immediately. "Let's plunder them! A cart will let us move over land."

"Aye! Arr! Aye!" yelled Pyra, swept up by Rovy's enthusiasm.

They moved their ship close to the island. Not so close, of course, that they ran aground. They were right next to the cart, when they _jumped_.

"Hands in the air!" yelled Rovy as he came down.

"Resistance is fuel!" yelled Pyra. 

"What?"

The woman pushing the cart looked up in surprise. "Do you mean _resistance is futile_?"

"Yes! That!"

"What does that mean?" asked Rovy. He fell on the cart and nearly broke the wheel.

"I don't know," said Pyra. She fell down on the woman, taking them both to the ground.

The kids scrambled to their feet and showed their swords.

"That cart is ours now!"

The woman brushed dirt from her dress and mumbled. "You could have just asked---"

"Run away! The pirates have won!" said Rovy.

The cart was filled with clean clothes, just washed. Now Pyra had made them dirty again. She had hoped for a soft landing in soft clothes. But her back hurt really bad, and she believed she must have landed on something sharp.

The woman complained about the youngsters these days. She looked as if she were about to cry.

She lowered her sword. 

"Are we ... are we the _bad guys_?" asked Pyra.

Rovy crossed his arms. "No, we can't be. Only one of us is a guy. _Common sense,_ as Brokebeard would say."

Pyra rolled her eyes. "You know what I mean. It's not nice to steal other people's vehicles. Isn't that why our family is poor? Father said someone did that to him."

"Then father should have been more like a pirate too!" Rovy said it with a loud voice and a fist raised. But he did not really believe it, dear listener. Because he liked his father very much. He thought it was the strongest and most fearsome man he'd ever seen. His father _would_ have been a great pirate, if he had not been so ... kind.

Rovy sighed. 

"Sorry. It was just an, erm, joke. You can have your cart back, ma'am."

Pyra rummaged through the clothes. She finally found why her back hurt when she landed in the cart.

Another magical stone. Hidden in the clothes. Another new symbol---a new direction for their ship to move in.

At this point, dear listener, Pyra got suspicious. How did they keep finding the exact stone they needed? Why was nobody else finding them? Whose ship was this? Why was it empty when they found it?

She apologized to the woman, then went back to the ship.

When her feet touched the deck, she got tingles in her stomach.

The ship went _upwards_. In other words, it started to _fly_.
