# "Planning & Routine"

**Description:**
As a child starts going to school, or reaches age three or four,
They must learn to consider how to structure their days more,
This resource contains calendars and planners to remember tasks or teach routine,
Teaching best practices without a word or good habit unseen

**Extra**: 
The material inside this resource can obviously be used by anyone to maintain good habits or day-to-day structure. It's mostly aimed, however, at young children experiencing this for the first time, such as when learning to brush their teeth or go to school.

An important part in convincing a child to keep up good habits, of course, is making them _understand_ why it's wise, and making it more _fun_ (with resources like these). The crucial oft-forgotten element, though, is that you need the right _mind-set_ to try this at all! That's why this resource---like most---also nudges children to have an active mind-set and be ready to put in effort or try new things.

It's a bit too early to explore the nitty-gritty of time, reading the clock, calendars, etcetera. This resource is a good first step towards that.

**Contents:**
* A simple (fairy tale) story about routine, planning and habits. Read it to your children to approach the general idea.
* A general weekplanner meant to write down school stuff and other hobbies. _Unique Twist: Explain to "change it up". The important thing is to have a varied/balanced planning._
* A general calendar where you can draw/write an activity to do, and the child/parent can cross it off every day/whenever they do it. _Unique Twist: If you do the thing every day/without fault, some image comes out of the end result?_
* A general "I tried something new today" or "I tried X, it failed" calendar? You're not rewarded, but it fosters this idea of _trying_ something and learning from it.
* Several _mazes_ that of progressing difficulty. (A maze is the simplest version of "try X, see if you get out, if not, try Y".) _Unique Twist: The correct directions through the maze MATCH an example planning/calendar given. To reinforce how those work._
  * => a very simple concept of "try direction A, doesn't work, now try direction B". And these can be explained by simply showing an image of a solved maze + "crossing walls" and sad faces.

**Advanced Spells:** Exhausted this resource? Then continue exploring this topic with:
* (Explorers) The Come-Back-Every-Day / The Rat Who Returned
  * One story is just about what planning/routine/habit _means_.
  * One story is about how something might not feel fun or useful at the moment, but doing it _again and again_ every day yields huge results.
* (Quizmasters) Quiz of When
  * "This is the fox' streak for brushing teeth. Did they succeed?" (you merely need to see if all squares are crossed off)
  * "This is how much time you have left today. Pick the option that fits in the schedule." (and then the multiple choice options are just different sets of hobbies/tasks/other things to do)
  * "This is what the fox did this week. Is X a habit/repeated?"
  * "What do you do when you get stuck? / The fox is stuck solving this maze. What to do?" (the answers are things like "getting angry" or "trying the same line again and again", with the correct answer showing they tried another line)
* (Puzzlepages) The Planning Puzzles
  * PUZZLE: The same thing, but now it shows icons on the side of how often you did something that day. And you have to fill up the planning/cross it off to fit the rules of the puzzle. (Example: on monday, it says "2xSchool, 2xExercise", and you must write this into blocks. BUT RULES: no two blocks of the same type may touch horizontally.)
* (Runaways) The Curious Calendar
  * Combine quiz/puzzle/exercises into a textless mini escape room.
  * This is really also about PATTERN recognition, no? Show someone's schedule for Mon-Thu, then add a big question mark on Friday and let the kid figure out that the pattern repeats.
  * (Linear path, every step color-coded, no language, mostly about reconstructing physical stuff (solve jigsaw/place X back where it belongs/simply combine two things and it reveals answer) => this means the parent will have to do a lot of setup work)
