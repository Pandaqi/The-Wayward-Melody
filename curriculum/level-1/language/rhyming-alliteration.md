**Contents**:

* Explanation
  * Vague: "Words rhyme if they sound the same at the end, but not before that"
  * Define (Onset-Rime): "Two parts of a word: onset is the initial sound; rime is the vowel and any consonant sounds that follow it." (IN A 1-SYLLABLE SOUND)
  * Define (Rhyming): "Two words rhyme if the last vowel and consonants after are the same, but everything before is different." (GENERALIZED)
* Simple stuff like asking if two words rhyme, going through something like "Listen to me and check if the middle+end sound the same."
* RHYMING QUIZ => Guardian says words, Apprentices need to pick from 4 options which one rhymes.
* RHYMING "GAME" => Spread tiles across an area. Guardian picks one and they all say it. Now they must pick another tile that rhymes; Guardian will sound out the words again if needed.
* ALLITERATION is a nice addition as it simply asks if that very first sound---the onset, already taught---is the same in two words.
  * @SOURCE: https://earlylearningideas.com/how-to-teach-rhyming-words/

**@NOTE:** Often, the different parts are represented by _different objects_ first. (Such as actual different tiles in size/color, one for ONSET and one for RIME.) You touch the object representing a "part" of a word and make the sound. Once clear, you push the objects together and say the whole word/blended sound.

* (Quizmasters) 
  * Quiz of Rhyme
    * Do these two things rhyme? ( = You hear some clip from a song, or two phrases after each other, and must merely say _whether_ it rymes)
    * Pick the answer that rhymes? ( = You hear one bit of audio/one sentence; the answers are audio too; listen and pick the one that rhymes) 
    * Complete this sound so that it rhymes? 
    * Come up with your own rhymes? ( = You get a word/sound; you must write down/yell/give something that rhymes on your own)
  * Quiz of Alliteration
    * Do these two things alliterate?
    * Pick the answer that alliterates.
    * Come up with your own alliteration.
