# Pencil Control

**Description:**
With the mind full of curiosity, a body growing and moving
A strange wobbly pencil in hand might make an apprentice disapproving
This resource teaches many ways to hold and use pen on paper
To give anyone the finest of motor skills to very much enjoy later

**Extra:**
When designing these teaching resources, we must admit we almost forgot this essential skill. To any teenager or older, being able to grab a pen and write, scribble, mark anything you want (quickly) is second nature. We forget how hard it is to learn. And how it's one of those invisible skills that we never notice, but we'd suddenly feel severely obstructed if we couldn't use it.

That's why we created this resource with a wide variety of challenges to test and practice pencil control. We obviously recommend working through it, as a large number of further challenges and games (especially puzzles) require being able to write letters and numbers on a paper :)

**Contents:**
* Images of different grips, asking you to try them all (and even invent your own), and see how easy it is to create circles/shapes that way!
  * Maybe relate this to ANIMALS. Like, "elephant grip" and "giraffe grip" and stuff.
* You can ask to _trace_ existing letters/alphabet/drawing.
* Literally ... just _coloring in_ existing drawings.
* Connecting Lines =>
  * There's a general path ( = wide train path or something) connecting two ends. Your task is to draw a line connecting the two ends, while _staying_ between those two extremes.
  * Connecting the dots/different elements.
  * **You can do a LOT by simply asking kids to "draw a line from start to finish, through X/avoiding Y/with pattern Z"!** (Like a Mario platformer on paper, for example.)
* Copying Lines =>
  * Some lines are given around the sides + a dotted line (orthogonal/inward).
  * Draw the exact same line, but now at the dotted line.
  * When done, all those lines combined will create a new image again.
* (Might go further with that and ask you to rotate/flip given shapes. Or draw it again, but twice as large. Maybe draw it X times, every time larger, so we can now USE that in Math sequencing things.)
