This resource teaches the most common **fine motor skills**. It mostly contains fun exercises and challenges to practice _cutting_ and _folding_, but also dives into many other useful skills for your hands. 

The resource briefly teaches _what_ a skill is---such as explaining how to hold scissors---and then makes it _fun_ to train your fingers stronger and improve your coordination. This will simply take time, and it will be annoying from time to time because young bodies simply _don't_ have that strength yet. But that's why we make the resources, and why we (hopefully) provide enough fun and variety to keep your Apprentice going :)

The crucial activity of [Pencil Control](/teachers/level-1/physical/pencil-control/), a precursor to handwriting and drawing lines, is so important that it received its own teaching resource. It's not really covered in this one. And if you have to choose, we recommend trying _this resource_ first, as the skills in here are more general and more directly exciting to kids.

As stated at [My First Gross Motor Skills](/teachers/level-1/physical/my-first-gross-motor-skills/), though, the best way to train these skills is usually _not_ through a PDF/paper/exercises like this. It will always be to just let your Apprentices play, and hold things, and touch things in the real world. Play sports, build stuff, be creative, use your hands all day, and the coordination and strength will follow too. This resource is mostly intended as a guide, nudging Apprentices to do things and create things with their hands.
