# Phantasy Phonics (Phonemic Awareness)

**Description**:
The first step to understanding a word
Is to recognize syllables and letter sound
With enough practice and effort
These resources build a strong language ground

**Extra**:

Identifying different sounds is the very first step to learning language. This resource contains several ways to start making sounds and matching those to letters (or words/images).

Research has also shown that it's helpful to involve sound _at every step of language learning_. We recommend spending quite some time on these phonics, but even when you progress, keep being vocal and keep sounding out letters/words. We evolved to _speak_ and to learn language through _sound_, and so this is still the most efficient way for our brains to learn it.

For that reason, we probably recommend spending most time with the Quizzes for this topic instead (see the list of links below). 

These are digital quizzes, which means they can play sounds and audio fragments. They also allow randomization and lots of variation in how Apprentices are "tested". This prevents "superficial" memorization. If you take the exact same test every day---e.g. showing an Apple and asking kids what's the first letter---you risk not teaching anything at all. They simply memorize that they need to say "A" when you show an Apple and that's that. 

This resource contains quiz-like experiences too, but they are physical only and it's up to the Guardian to actually say the words/make the sounds (correctly).

**Contents**:

* Explanation
  * Define: "Words are made out of small sound units. Any time you change the sound you make / change your mouth position, that's a new part."
* Starts with identifying FIRST, LAST, MIDDLE sounds (CVC words) => then moves to identifying anywhere/all 
  * Simply ask "what's the first/last/middle sound?"
  * Sort images/icons to be below the thing which has the same start/end/middle
  * In a group of things, encircle all that have the same start/end/middle sound
  * In a group of things, encircle THUMBS UP if all share a sound, THUMBS DOWN otherwise. (Bonus: Encircle the thing that is DIFFERENT.)
* Copy from _Quiz of Phonemes_ again.
* Elkonin Boxes (instruct how to use + that you can use it _further_ if/once it's more advanced) =>
  * Stage 1: You say a word (slowly, enunciating each part). Apprentices need to identify phonemes by stomping/repeating/placing a new disk on the paper.
  * Stage 2: Actually collect the proper letters/visualization of the phoneme. (CH I M P)
  * Stage 3: Actually collect the individual letters. (C H I M P)
  * Stage 4: Write the phonemes/individual letters yourself.
