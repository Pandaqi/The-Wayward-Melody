# The Gospyll According To A Bearchitect

I must admit my reluctancy to write this Gospyll. My only connection to Krystina, before the terrible events in this story, was our occupation. She was a woodworker; I am a woodworker. Some may call me the best builder there ever was, but I'd have to give that praise to my father. He built entire structures and cities, the first of their kind. Many of his creations still stand today, even after the violence of the First Conflict.

Whereas I, personally, experienced my peak when I built the wooden table for The Last Supper.

At the time, of course, none of the disciples called it that. It was just a supper. A very inconvient supper at that.

Their requirements for the table were strange, to say the least. It doesn't happen often that thirteen people---Krystina and her twelve apostles---share the same dinner table. It certainly doesn't happen often that they all want to sit _on the same side_. It was better for the painting, they claimed. 

To make matters even worse, all the apostles were different animal species. The table had to be slanted to accomodate for their different heights. Some parts had to receive extra holes or even a small water basin, for Krystina herself.

After several days of woodworking, I had built the longest table ever created. My strong bear hands never felt this tired---nor this fulfilled. With my nails, I scratched my name into one of the legs. In another leg, I scratched the words _To Krystina, Queen of the Jurads_.

I just placed the table in the room when Krystina arrived. She was alone for now.

"Does it please you, my queen?" I asked.

"Oh yes, yes it does. You've outdone yourself, Bungo Junior! A place in heaven will surely be reserved for you."

To me, building a long wooden table seemed a pretty low bar to cross. But it was nice to hear that this new God of ours had lowered his standards. The old family of gods, especially fearsome Ardex at its head, could be merciless and have awfully strict rules. They were all gone now.

So, I wondered, who was this "God" that they spoke of? Who could be left? If Krystina was the messenger, then for whom?

Before I could ask, however, a large dove flew through the windows---which were just holes in the wall.

Krystina lit some nice candles and placed some flowers on the table. Really tied the room together, I must say. If this savior thing didn't work out, that platypus could always become an interior designer. 

Then she asked the Bearchitect for the menu and when the food would be ready.

"I am an architect of objects, my queen, not of food. But you are in good hands. Or, should I say, _wings_."

Their cook flew into the room and brought the first loafs of bread. It was none other than Dilova, the first of doves, considered by some to be the first of all birds. She was ancient by now and had run a moving restaurant for much of that time. She was also good friends with the old gods, especially Cosmo, who was a bird himself. When they were defeated, she had no other choice than to flee Amor and start again in Jerrysalum. A place she hoped the Amor Empire would never reach, but she was very wrong.

In the presence of Dilova, the mysterious dove paid his respects, then dared speak freely. I, of course, sneakily hung around to eavesdrop, so I could write this Gospyll.

"Before the night is over," he told Krystina, "one of your disciples will betray you."

"Impossible!" she said.

"Have I ever told you something untrue?"

"I suppose you haven't, but---"

The dove hopped even closer. His wings drooped, and his beak seemed to do the same. His voice was low and gravely, serious and ice cold. 

"And before Passover is done, you will be dead."

Krystina shivered. She looked all around herself, as if assassins were waiting in every corner now, as if every shadow contained her biggest fear.

Was this God's plan? To end it all just as she became successful? The dove had often given her serious predictions like this, such as "you will sacrifice yourself", but she had ignored them. Easy to think nothing would touch her. Easy to think the prophecies were so vague they could mean _anything_. Easy to think that such a sacrifice could wait in line until she was a hundred years old.

"How do you know all this?" she asked. Her fins trembled and she splashed water from the basin in her face. "Where do you get your information?"

"I'm a spy, Krystina. And a good one."

"So you are spying on ... God? Who is it? And don't tell me you don't know his name, like that snake in the desert did!"

The dove grinned. "Well, that snake _did_ learn from the very best. Some gods are wise enough to stay hidden." 

He gestured to a wall painting depicting the old gods. Ardex, Bella, all of them looking majestic and powerful, as if nothing could touch them. A sad expression was all that was needed: throughout history, gods who intervened too much and showed themselves, were more likely to make themselves vulnerable.

Krystina understood now why she was a _messenger_, and God was never going to show his true face.

"Which of my disciples will betr---" she started asking, but the door flew open and the first of her friends already hopped inside.

___

The twelve disciples admired the table I built. It was a very good-looking table, I assure you.

One by one, the apostles entered. Krystina smiled at them all. She pretended nothing was wrong. But deep inside, she was looking for signs. Who was about to betray her? How? She could not imagine any of her friends bringing a weapon or attacking her.

Of course, dear reader, she took _betrayal_ far too literally. Her disciples loved her. None of them, not even Judash, would ever harm her directly. That didn't mean the spy dove was wrong.

As the disciples entered, Krystina used the water from the basin to wash their paws. Or feet, as Bartholo the Ape wished his hind paws to be called. It was a simple habit that Krystina had preserved from when she was young, performing simple duties such as washing clothes for money. This ritual cleansing also helped during her healings: it improved hygiene and prevented diseases from spreading.

Most of all, it reminded her disciples to stay humble, a servant of this mysterious God. This was where it went wrong with all previous messengers, Judash had told her yesterday. They started to believe _they_ were more important than the _message_.

Once a disciple had been cleaned, they took their place. Dilova flew overhead and brought food, but nobody touched it until everyone sat.

And then a discussion broke out.

"I am clearly the best apostle," said Astra the Bunny with a grin. "Do you know how many bunnies I turned into Krystina's followers?"

"But that's easy," responded Bartholo the Ape, laughing. "It seems as if every second three more bunnies are born! They're a plague!"

"No, no, no," said Wamez the Wolf more seriously. "If someone were to list the apostles, surely my name would be at the top. I have been Krystina's disciple the longest."

Judash grunted. "Only because she _forgot_ to make me and Astra one too. We have known her longer than you."

"Madness," said Bartholo, "this isn't even a discussion. When they write their Gospylls about us, they will mention me the most! Clearly!"

I can confidently say that Bartholo was wrong about this. And so was Wamez, and even Judash. I imagine it's Astra Buni we will write the most about, but that's a story for another time. 

Krystina slammed the table, sending bread rolling to the floor. "Stop this! Now!"

The gathering fell silent.

"The greatest among you must be the most humble; the best leader is the one who is like the servant. Let the history books write how we created world peace, rather than mention our names. I fear sometimes, disciples, that you are losing your way."

Often, Krystina didn't know where the words came from. But she found herself speaking wisdom she never knew she had. As if, just for that moment, this mysterious God _was_ her.

The disciples looked at each other and nodded. Astra mostly licked her long teeth at the sight of the bread.

"But how do we know where to go in the first place?" asked Astra. "How do we know the way?" 

To which Krystina said: "I _am_ the way. You can find heaven by believing in _me_."

If Krystina had been less busy spouting wisdom, she would have seen Judash cringe at the statement. Instead, her eyes glazed over, and it seemed she received another vision. As if the soul of God temporarily lived in her. 

"Before the night is over," said Krystina solemnly, watching everyone's face. "One of you will betray me."

"Never!" was the unanimous response.

"If so ..." started Judash carefully, "would it not be wise to flee?"

"I will not abandon my mission, or my friends."

"Might I suggest ... a nice garden to spend the night?" offered Judash. "I have a good feeling about you being there exactly at midnight."

Krystina would have frowned, if platypi were capable of that.

"Yes," continued Judash, "the fourth tree on the right seems like a great hiding place."

All disciples looked at him. He shrugged his black wool. 

"Just trying to save our savior," he muttered. "You know the tree, right? The one with that message carved into it?"

"Yes, I know, but doesn't saying the hiding place defeat the purpose of---"

Judash stroked his chin and pretended to ponder this. "And it's probably best if you go there _alone_."

Now Krystina was comletely lost. "Am I not safer with others around?"

"But you just said," whispered Judash, looking left and right, "one of _us_ will betray you."

"Which means eleven of you will not. Let that be your lesson: do not focus too much on the imperfections and the sin, but focus on the many things that go right in this world." 

Suddenly, Krystina's head jerked to the side. Her eyes seemed to glow as she stared down Astra Buni.

"But before dawn arrives and the rooster crows, you will deny me three times."

"Never!" yelled Astra again, but she was alone this time. She cast down her eyes, studying her plate that was still frustratingly empty. Her soft paw fell over Krystina's. "I would not abandon you, not for a hundred golden coins."

"Anybody who accepts me as the messiah will be accepted into the Heavenly Palace," said Krystina. "But anybody who would deny me, would be denied entry into the Heavenly Palace too."

It was at this point that everyone realized I, their friendly Bearchitect, was still in the room. And they didn't care. Maybe that's why they all wanted a long table and sit on one side---to leave space for any stranger who wanted to join on the other side. 

If you ask me, that's how Krystina should be remembered. As someone who, magical or not, cared for all and left room for all.

Finally, Krystina broke the bread and poured the wine.

"Take, eat. This bread is my body. I am the living bread come down from heavens, to give spiritual sustenance, and to be broken and consumed for your benefit."

This caused some actual frowns, mostly from Bartholo the Ape who had eyebrows. But they were used to vague lessons from their messiah, so they didn't think too much of it.

"Drink, enjoy. This wine is my blood."

This, too, was met with a shrug and a quick check if the wine was wine and not actual blood. 

They passed around the food. They complained about the downsides of one long table: it took ages for the food to reach you. They all yelled through each other's stories, asking for a loaf of bread from the other corner of the table, or wine that had to pass twelve different paws before you could drink it. Bartholo moved his seat seven times, only to find the food equally far away every time. Astra Buni complained that all the carrots could just as well have been on the other side of the world. She was one more miscommunication to the other side of the table away from just hopping across. Which, to be clear, was obviously not good dinner etiquette.

From my place in the corner, I could see all the disciples enjoy the food, laugh, and make jokes about the metaphors Krystina was using. 

All but one.

Judash' face was thunderous. Krystina had just told them to be humble and selfless. Not to attach their names to their work, or think themselves holy. Judash would completely _agree_. If he could, he'd erase his name from every Gospyll ever written!

And now Krystina called _herself_ a _gift from heaven_? She wanted credit for all the bread that would ever be eaten? Wanted to be remembered for every wine that would ever be drunk? What was next? They'd have to pray to _her_ before every meal they ate?

It confirmed Judash' worst fears. It removed the final sliver of doubt. That much was clear from his contorted face.

Now, dear reader, we can talk about Judash and whether he was a villain or not. I did not know him that well. But I can assure you that _his_ Gospyll is full of _lies_. A hundred gold coins? It's a well-known fact that Judash betrayed Krystina for far less than that: just thirty pieces of silver. Three visits by the High Priest to "persuade" him? Don't make me laugh. They wouldn't dress up and sneak into alleys to speak to him! If you ask me, Judash made the decision all on his own. He needed barely a push to betray his best friend and our promised savior.

As Judash left the room, he whispered in Krystina's ear.

"You want to meet this mysterious God? He's waiting by that tree I mentioned, in the gardens, at midnight."

A few hours later, their bellies were full and their stories told,. Krystina thanked them all for the warmth and friendship, then left too.

___

Krystina enjoyed a clear evening in the garden of _Gethsemane_. A name so difficult to pronounce for snakes that they stayed away and never even bothered. But it was a beautiful name for a beautiful garden. Krystina visited often. To study the stars and remember the old god's promise that, one day, the Jurad people would be as numerous as they. The night sky was so pure, all plants in the garden so well-made, Krystina could feel certain that this world was no accident. It was designed by that mysterious god.

Who, needless to say, did not show up. As the dove said: God's greatest chance at survival is to not actually appear. Judash' trick was a simple one, but it had worked wonders. Judash himself, sources told me, was in the garden too, cackling as he counted his money and fled to safer pastures.

By the time I entered the gardens myself, a platoon of Amor soldiers was already on my tail. Krystina had maybe fifteen minutes to flee. To leave Jerrysalum, abandon her mission, and go live a safe life somewhere else.

It is my biggest regret that I gave her a choice. I should have just picked her up and taken her away. Instead, when I caught up with her, I said,

"They're going to arrest you. They will not be merciful. Please, flee."

"I was planning on hiding for a bit behind that large oak tree," said Krystina. "But where is it?"

"I cut it down to build your table."

"Ah. Then the tree Judash mentioned will have to do."

I stomped the earth in frustration. "Don't you understand? Judash has lured you here! This will be your death! Why don't you leave?"

Krystina fell down to the ground. She looked awfully tired. Deflated and sad, leaky on all sides. The way she must have looked when she entered that desert, without food or drink, for the first time. Only worse now, because she understood perfectly well. For the first time in her thirty-three years on this planet.

"I see now, father," she mumbled at the stars. "They need a big story. Washing their feet isn't enough. Healing a few isn't enough. Multiplying bread is considered a party trick."

"What are you mumbling about?" I yelled. My bear ears, which are very good I assure you, picked up the clanging of swords in the distance. The shouting of commands. The platoon of Amor soldiers combed through the gardens, cutting away every tree and vine in their path. Like a catastrophe heading straight for one specific woman, small and tired. Worst of all, they destroyed my very favorite tree.

"Go! Leave!"

"Don't you see? Judash calls me _just a platypus_. He doesn't believe I am holy at all. Just an animal of flesh and blood. All my miracles, all my lessons, my forty days in the desert, none of it convinced him."

"You would die? Just to convince an evil black sheep?"

"Not just him. Everyone. They all believe the same lies. That animals are just made to be evil. That anyone will sin if given enough time. That the world is supposed to be unfair and nothing can change that. That ... that all the gods are dead and now there is nothing left to believe in."

"Go!"

"No, _you_ go. Before they arrest you too, just for being associated with me."

Krystina's beak traced the messages carved into the tree. _Queen of the Jurads, the sinless savior that was promised._ And  _I love you Eric, kisses and hugs_. And also: _Has anybody seen my pet bunny? She has long ears and likes painting eggs._, but that's less relevant to this Gospyll.

The Amor soldiers, all wolves, held special torches between their jaws. More and more, the calm and dark garden was lit up and disturbed.

I am sad to say that I obeyed Krystina's final command. I fled. If I'd stayed, I might have caught a glimpse of God, as they are said to have appeared to her. Just for a few brief seconds, they confirmed Krystina was doing the right thing, and told her what would happen next.

Krystina cried, her tears red from the torchlight and Amor uniforms. And she recited the start of the Gospyll of Lent: _People need a big story to replace a big lie._

She let herself be captured by the soldiers. The promised savior was in chains, carried on wolfback like a bag of garbage. Tomorrow they would already hold her trial, meant to prove that she was guilty and hand out a sentence, or set her free.
